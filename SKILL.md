# Advertising Science

The codex is the source of truth. On Lucky's machine it lives in the vault: `Obsidian God-level Marketing Vault/God-level Marketing/wiki/science/00-Codex.md` and its topic notes. In the team package (repo `EverythingAI-Pro/advertising-science`) the same codex ships as `references/codex/`, refreshed by the daily research run; if the vault path does not exist on this machine, read `references/codex/` instead. This skill is the protocol for using and growing it.

## Evidence tiers (the spine of everything)

- **T1**: platform docs or engineering publications. How the machine is built.
- **T2**: a real test with shown data. Ours beat everyone else's.
- **T3**: practitioner claim from real spend, no shown test. Strong hypothesis, never a law.
- **T4**: theory. An idea, nothing more.

Statuses: `active`, `contested` (disagreeing credible sources, both recorded), `superseded`, `refuted` (kept forever so we never relearn it).

## Using the science (every ad decision)

1. Name the decision (scale, cut, structure, bid, creative volume, diagnosis, forecast).
2. Read the relevant topic note(s) from the codex. Meta delivery, Auction, Learning & Signal, Creative, Scaling, Attribution, Google Auction, PMax, TikTok, Emerging Channels, Marketing Math.
3. Apply claims by tier: T1/T2 can decide. T3 informs but a T3-only decision must be named as a bet, not a law. T4 never decides.
4. If codex claims conflict with a client's own account data, the account wins and the conflict gets recorded in the codex as a contested claim.
5. If the decision would make a good test, say so: every T3 we test on our own accounts becomes a T2 with our numbers. That upgrade path is the whole point.
6. If the codex is silent on the question, say "the codex is silent here", decide on judgment, and log the gap in the Harvest Log so research targets it.

## Growing the science (claim merge protocol)

New information (transcript, doc, update) is decomposed into claims. For each claim:

- **Matches an existing claim**: add the source and date to it. Never create a duplicate.
- **Contradicts an existing claim**: set status `contested`, record both sides with sources. A T1/T2 contradiction of a T3 claim supersedes it instead.
- **New**: next free ID in its topic (`MD-`, `AU-`, `LS-`, `CR-`, `SC-`, `AT-`, `GA-`, `GP-`, `TT-`, `EC-`, `MM-`), tier it honestly, date it.
- **Fluff** (motivation, repetition of banked claims, pure promotion): discard silently. We bank claims, not videos.

A claim entry looks like:

```
### MD-001 · Creative is the primary targeting input post-Andromeda
Tier: T1 · Status: active
Meta's multi-stage retrieval system selects candidate ads by predicted
per-user relevance of the creative itself; audience settings mostly bound
the candidate pool.
Sources: Meta engineering blog (Andromeda post); BlueSense 2026-07-28; Charley T.
Last touched: 2026-08-18
```

## Current laws (hot layer)

Updated only when the codex changes at law level. Each law cites its claims; depth lives in the codex. Last rebuilt: 2026-08-19 (second pass) from **829 claims** (50 T1, 76 T2, 592 T3, 111 T4, 61 contested, 5 superseded) across 11 topic files.

**Read this before quoting any law below.** The 2026-08-19 second pass moved 14 of them, and the direction was almost entirely toward LESS certainty, not more. Four laws that read as settled are now contested, two turned out to be arguments about vocabulary rather than about the world, and one survivor clause we had leaned on broke outright. That is the engine working. A law that says "contested" is more useful than a law that says something false with confidence.

**Meta delivery and creative**

1. **Creative is the targeting.** Meta reads the creative content itself at the retrieval stage (T1 architecture, MD-022/MD-025); the avatar or scenario named in the ad decides who sees it (MD-001, T3). Verified case: niching one product's creative to one avatar halved cost per purchase, $73 to $35 at equal spend (CR-005, T2). Settings only set boundaries: location, minimum age, language, exclusions. Strongest support yet arrived 2026-08-19: an operator ran United States, 18 to 65, women, no detailed targeting and no retargeting audiences at all, changing only the avatar in the creative, and swaps the person's ethnicity in an otherwise identical ad to unlock a new group (MD-001).
**Tier guard, and it is load-bearing.** Matt Steiner, a named Meta executive over monetization infrastructure, ranking and foundational AI, describes retrieval as a per-user decision made on creative content attributes. That is **T3, not T1**, and must never be promoted. He quotes no document and shows no data in 20 minutes. A named platform employee asserting on a podcast is testimony, not documentation. The T1 half of this law rests on the engineering publications at MD-022/MD-025, nothing else.
2. **Near-duplicate collapse is now CONTESTED. Speak it as a bet, not a mechanism.** The position: near-duplicate ads collapse into one entity ID with zero unique reach, so diversity must live at the concept level (angle x offer x persona), and a format change (static to video) escapes the collapse (MD-003). Ben Heath arrives on the collapse side, saying Meta enters only one hook variant into the auction. Against it, Blue Sense in Jan 2026: "it might count the bodies as the same ad and group them together. Well, I can tell you it doesn't because this is still working incredibly well in our accounts," backed by a 27-April ad revived to the account's top two by 4 newly shot hooks. Assertion against assertion, **nobody has shown an entity ID or a unique-reach figure on either side** (MD-003, contested, both T3). Boundary worth keeping: the rebuttal is scoped to swaps that change the VISUAL, which is consistent with MD-043. The practical advice survives either way, because concept-level diversity is cheap insurance.
3. **Do not run the doom cycle. BOTH halves of this law are now contested, including the clause we thought was safe.** The original position: the top-spending "worst CPA" ad is usually the TOF engine, killing it can crater the ad set, and churn-killing ads into replacements is the most-cited reason accounts stop scaling (MD-010 T3, SC-012 T2).
**Top spenders.** Charley T says kill the budget hog so the others can spend. Against him: Faris across 100+ accounts leaves high spenders on in 99%+ of cases and reports roughly 30% more spend at the same target just from switching a previous buyer's paused high spenders back on; Blue Sense never lists killing the hog as an option at all; Theriot shows a CBO going $98 to $51 to about $35 cost per purchase with two concurrent top spenders and no kill anywhere (SC-099, T2); Piliero never kills a high spender and reads at ad-set level. Meta's own ranking lead adds that the system already reallocates off weak creative. **Score is 3 voices to 1 plus a platform statement, and still zero controlled data on either side** (SC-008, SC-085, both contested). Weight of opinion is not evidence.
**Low spenders, and this is the 2026-08-19 change.** We used to say killing LOW spenders is always safe. Blue Sense contradicts it head-on: "what a lot of people do, which is a massive mistake, is they just come in here and turn these ads off. Don't do that." Two mechanisms given: the starved ad plays an assist role in the conversion sequence, and it is the ad set's reserve when the current winner fatigues (SC-009, **flipped to contested 2026-08-19**). Likely reconciling boundary, untested: Theriot only kills once cumulative spend has accrued at zero purchases, so a genuinely zero-spend ad costs nothing to keep. **What still survives on every side: judge at ad-set level, never on the ad's own row.**
Settle it on our own accounts, the test is cheap and reversible: kill the top spender for 2 to 3 days and read ACCOUNT-level cost per result. Counterweight still on file: CTC's back-test found manual on/off decisions had no measurable impact at all (both T2).
4. **Relevance is a bid.** A more relevant ad wins auctions against higher bidders (AU-002, T1). Creative quality is the cheapest CPM lever we have.

4a. **The live-ad-count debate is an artefact of undefined units. Never quote a count without stating the unit.** Per ad set or per account, discrete ads or flex units, live concurrently or launched per month, and whose definition of "an ad" (CR-154, contested). Blue Sense counts only ads that differ enough to be a statistically relevant test, so colour, headline, primary-text and **landing-page changes are all the SAME ad**, and his only figure is a monthly LAUNCH rate from a forecast (11 more ads a month to move $130k to $160k net sales), never a live count. Charley T's unit slides between discrete ads and 322 flex units, roughly 8x. Heath's 20 is per ad set and stands **unrevised as of 2026-05-19**. Haynes's 75-90 is per ad set times 3. Theriot runs 10-20 live ad sets at 3 creatives each, roughly 30-60 live ads on one audience. Piliero went 165 live ads to 62 while ROAS moved 0.63 to 1.4 on $14k over 30 days (T2 counts, confounded with a simultaneous restructure). Meta's own position: reasonable diversity beats an extremely large number, no threshold given.
**Trap:** the "2 to 5" ads in Meta's creative testing tool (raised to 7 by August) is a UI cap on a split test. It is not Heath revising toward Charley T. Related and also contested: whether replicating proven winners is the top allocation bucket at 50-60% of output, or the most common cause of new ads getting zero delivery (CR-033).

4b. **"Hook swap" means two things and only one of them has any evidence.** A rewrite or re-cut of an EXISTING shoot is near-free, should be exhausted before retiring an asset, and **has never been shown to revive a fatigued winner**. The swap that does have a real case is a NEW SHOOT WITH A NEW PERSON: a 2-body 9-hook testimonial running about 5x the account's next best ad fatigued at 6-7 months and returned to the account's top two at the same daily spend throughput after **4 newly shot hooks of a different person** (CR-124, contested, T3, no CPA or spend shown). The operator's own ceiling: "often just a change in wording can get a lot of people in, but it's not going to get everyone in. We do need a change in visuals."
**Half of this disagreement is vocabulary.** Theriot lands opposite only because he DEFINES a hook swap as the 3 variants inside one ad idea, identical script, voiceover and text hook, varying 3 to 5 seconds of visual. Under that definition a hook swap can never be a new argument, because the argument is held constant by construction. His "new avatar plus new format plus new angle" and Blue Sense's "new hooks of a different person" are the same move under two names. Haynes filming 25-30 separate arguments is the same family again.
**The surviving empirical question is much narrower than the debate: does re-cutting the first 3 to 5 seconds of an existing shoot revive a fatigued winner? Nobody has run it.** Also note the 10-hooks-for-the-effort-of-one-body arithmetic counts EDIT effort only and does not cover the shoot day the case actually required. The 80% and 90%-never-past-the-hook figures are folklore, traceable to an Ogilvy print-headline readership observation restated decades later as a video retention statistic; not one operator quoting a percentage has a retention curve behind it.

4c. **Opportunity Score is a vendor recommendation engine, not a grade. Never report it to a client as one.** Meta's own description: a 0 to 100 real-time health check on the account and its ads that issues ranked optimisation recommendations, shown in one Meta post instructing an advertiser to move from online-only to omnichannel optimisation (MD-099, **T1 for existence and surface ONLY**, same guard as 19b). How it is computed, what it weights, whether it ranks by advertiser outcome or by Meta revenue, and whether following it leaves an account better off are all unknown, and both sources are Meta marketing posts whose only figures are Meta-chosen case studies. This matters because we have already quoted a score without defining it: ChiroWorks sits at 60 out of 100 in Lesson 002. Treat the panel as a list of things Meta wants changed, check each against the codex, and act only on the ones that survive.

**Learning and signal**

5. **Never significant-edit a working ad set.** Targeting, creative, optimization event, bid strategy, adding an ad, or a 7-day pause always reset learning (LS-002/LS-003, T1). An ad set needs ~50 optimization events in the week after its last significant edit to exit learning (LS-001, T1).
**Budget moves reset it only when large, and the threshold is size-dependent.** A 3% daily raise is unlikely to reset. A doubling-scale raise does, with a **1 to 2 day resettle** before results are readable. Meta's own illustration is a 10x, $100 to $1,000; Heath's 2x ($50 to $100/day) is T3 practitioner from a $300M book with no learning-status data shown. The 1-2 day resettle is the first resettle duration on file anywhere in the codex, so quote it as a practitioner figure.
6. **The results column IS the targeting.** Meta finds more people like whoever fired the optimization event, so feed it only qualified conversions (LS-008, T3, active).
**The contested claim is LS-009, volume-first against quality-first. Say which side you are betting on.** The quality side now has three voices to one: Charley T's full lead-gen chain (all lead events look identical to Meta, so the model learns to find form-fillers; pushing CPL down makes quality worse; **Andromeda executing the objective BETTER is precisely why this degraded rather than improved**; fix is a custom "engaged lead" pixel event on the email destination page, then send the qualified lead back as a purchase valued at average lead value), plus Piliero's ecommerce "purchase only, never add to cart, no exception." **Do not read that as settled.** Nobody on either side ran a controlled test, and the volume side holds the stronger mechanism, because the ~50-events-a-week threshold at LS-001 is T1 documentation and every qualification gate cuts volume toward that floor. Stacking assertions on one pan is consensus, not evidence.
**The reconciliation to actually operate on (LS-026): take the deepest event that still clears learning.** That makes the answer volume-dependent rather than doctrinal, so the volume side is right at low volume and the quality side at high.
**This is the most operationally relevant law in the codex for our own book,** because every client we run is lead gen: chiropractic clinics, commercial truck dealers, local service businesses. The failure mode Charley T describes is exactly the cheap-CPL campaign that books no appointments.

**Scaling**

7. **Budget step size is folklore territory, and the band is far wider than we thought.** Prescriptions now run **3% to 100% per day**, and the right question is not "what percentage" but **"what percentage, at what budget, held for how long"** (SC-001, T3, contested). Heath makes step size a decreasing function of budget: 100% at $50/day, 50% at $100/day, 20% at $800/day, each held 5 to 10 days, with a 3% daily default and a 5 to 7% hard stop (SC-100). His 3% sits below our entire previously banked range and his 100% sits above it, and both are internally consistent only because of the hold duration. Theriot confirms 20%/day gated on yesterday's cost per acquisition, doubles budget 2 to 3 times in a single day on Black Friday, and says timing within the day is irrelevant. **Three transcripts, no controlled step-size test anywhere.** A ROAS dip after a raise is upper-funnel time lag, wait before rebuilding (SC-002, T3).
8. **Minimum viable daily budget is 1x target cost per result** per day; three roster operators converged on this independently (MM-019, T3).

8a. **A cost cap buys the cheapest conversion available, and "cheapest" is greedy on TWO independent axes.**
**Catalogue axis.** A cap has no AOV term, so at a $50 cap a $50 AOV product returns 1.0 ROAS and a $100 AOV product returns 2.0 ($80 gives 1.6, $90 gives 1.8), and Meta drains the budget onto the cheapest SKU because a 1.0 ROAS conversion is the easiest to buy (AU-040, T3). Shown live: a sitewide markdown pulled capped spend onto a $46 AOV product and the fix was off-platform, raise the price and re-merchandise the collection page (AU-045, T2).
**Audience axis.** A cap can only spend when it hits the target, so it buys the people already nearest to purchase and quietly turns a prospecting campaign into de facto middle- and bottom-funnel retargeting. A second agency turned caps off in 2026 for exactly this and **never mentions AOV once** (AU-049; AU-026 reads the same mechanism temporally, harvest then crash, rather than as steady-state drift).
**Keep the two apart, because they break differently.** A shipped ROAS guardrail would fix the catalogue axis and do nothing to the audience axis. Splitting SKUs by price band, likewise.
**Two rules: never mix price bands inside one capped ad set, and never read a capped campaign's CPA as a cold-acquisition cost.** Secondary reason to avoid caps: attribution cannibalisation, "the cost cap steals conversions out of the maximize conversion value or at least it steals the last click attribution." Operating conditions on file: never mix a cap with max-conversions, run it in a separate ad account, and every named spend floor now sits above six figures a month ($100k / $250k / $300k, unreconciled).
**Caveat the whole law.** An operator on eight- and nine-figure accounts concedes there is "not really enough conclusive data to prove that this is really outperforming just optimizing for value optimizing for conversions" (AU-051), and nobody on the roster has shown capped against uncapped on the same account.
**Operating consequence for us: a client price change or promo calendar IS a delivery change. We have to see it before it ships.**

8b. **Accumulated learning lives in the ad account, not the pixel.** A fresh ad account starts from zero even on the same pixel (SC-094, T3). So client ad accounts get created under the CLIENT's Business Manager with partner access to us, never the reverse. Getting this backwards throws away the asset when the relationship changes.

**Attribution**

9. **Turn view-through attribution off and audit for it**; rebuild any ad set where view-through exceeds ~10% of conversions (AT-012, T2).
**The structural mechanism, new 2026-08-19 and it upgrades this law.** Meta cannot drop a browser cookie from the app, so the impression-to-conversion join only exists if a pixel event already fired. **Every 1-day-view conversion is therefore a prior site visitor by construction.** That moves the claim from "view-through skews warm" to "a cold person cannot produce one" (AT-011). Paired with a real account reading 75x ROAS on $2-3k/month Meta against $70k/month Google.
**The exception, and it is an AND condition:** Meta is the sole traffic driver AND the purchase cycle is long. That covers 5-10% of ecommerce or fewer, and the trade you are making is signal starvation, not accuracy (AT-076).
House rule stands: never call spend "wasted" from a last-click zero. Contested scale: one auditor finds 1-day view running 30-50% of reported ROAS as the norm, which would make the 10% threshold a healthy exception rather than a typical account (AT-037, T3). AT-037 also carries the delivery half: the 7-day-click plus 1-day-view setting biases DELIVERY toward view-through, not only the report, so roughly 90% of accounts overspend on existing customers and the fix is two-sided. The "underreports only 10-20%" figure inherits the sign problem in 9a below (AT-008, **downgraded T2 to T3 on 2026-08-19**: both sources are the same speaker citing Haus and CTC meta-analyses from memory, studies never shown).

9a. **The incrementality benchmarks expired, and the SIGN of the error is account-dependent. This is the 2026-08-19 correction.** The old rule fixed the direction: true incremental sits near 28-day click, roughly 7-day click times 110-115%, i.e. always correct UPWARD. **That is wrong as a per-account rule.** One shown account ran 4.77 on 7-day click against 3.25 incremental, which is 47% OVER-attribution, while the same operator argues a second account UNDER-attributes because its acquisition MER runs above the platform number (AT-074, T2). The sign is a property of the account, set by channel mix and AOV.
**110-115% is a population average across pooled tests. Never apply it to a single account without reconciling against aMER first.** The multiplier was also derived on the OLD wider 7-day-click column, so it does not survive the March 2026 outbound-click-only redefinition either (AT-008 cross-referenced to AT-036). Never quote it against post-March-2026 data as current.
10. **Meta redefined 7-day click around March-April 2026** to require an outbound click, moving likes, comments, shares and page clicks into a new 1-day engaged view bucket, so 7-day-click ROAS fell overnight with no change in real performance (AT-036, T3; delivery-side half at AU-017). **Reporting consequence, and it is a client-facing one:** a step-down in 7-day-click ROAS dated to that window is a definitional artefact and must never be written up as a performance decline.
11. **Never quote Meta's Incremental Attribution to a client as a measured lift. We no longer know what it actually is.** The client-facing rule is unchanged and holds. The MECHANISM behind it flipped to contested on 2026-08-19, because three incompatible explanations are now on file **from the same speaker**: a factor derived from conversion-lift experiments run by OTHER advertisers in your niche (2026), a permanent 10% holdout of your own target demographic (Jun 2025), and the arithmetic midpoint of 1-day and 7-day click (May 2025). **None is documented** (AT-035, contested; the same description also sits at MD-034).
They disagree on the only thing that matters operationally: whether the number is a lift measured on your own account. If the midpoint explanation is right it is a click-window proxy rather than a causal instrument, which would make a hand-rolled proxy just as defensible and weaken the case for switching ad sets onto it. The client-reporting rule survives under two of the three mechanisms, so keep it. It still beats standard attribution on the T2 evidence (AT-001 to AT-004).
11a. **Period-end reports read the most incomplete days as final.** Conversions keep landing after a window closes, so the newest days in any report are still filling in. Every week-over-week and month-over-month comparison therefore structurally flatters the EARLIER window. Cut the same tail off both windows or state the uncertainty (AT-068, T3). At low conversion counts a zero-conversion day is baseline variance, not a finding (AT-031). And never write "Meta broke" or "platform issue" without the spend-spike-at-strange-CPM signature; it is the least likely explanation and the only one with no plan attached (AT-066).

12. **Prune by MONEY LOST, never by low spend as such.** In a shown 89-ad campaign the winning ads and the losing ads consumed the same budget, $48,594 against $48,800, while the losers ran a $230 CPA and a negative $0.98 gross profit per transaction (MD-046, T2). Cutting that campaign from 89 ads to 4 and launching nothing new for six weeks 10x'd spend in 90 days, with weekly gross profit per transaction holding at 136/133/137/124 even as platform ROAS fell below 2 (SC-050, T2).
**Reconciling this with law 3's new low-spender contest, because they look opposed and are not.** SC-050's losers were FUNDED: $48,800 of real spend at negative profit per transaction, so cutting them removed a measured loss. Blue Sense is describing near-zero-spend ads carrying near-zero loss. So: an ad that has spent real money at negative profit per transaction goes, an ad that has spent nothing stays. **The boundary is untested, so state it as a hypothesis.** This still does not license killing top spenders, which law 3 forbids.

**Google**

13. **Quality Score is a diagnostic, not an auction input**; auction-time quality is computed fresh per auction (GA-010, T1).
14. **Under tCPA, bid adjustments move the CPA target itself**: +40% mobile on a $10 tCPA means a $14 mobile target; -100% excludes the device (GA-004, T1).
15. **Exact match beats PMax for a matching query; search themes only tie with phrase/broad** (GP-001, T1). PMax audience signals are suggestions, never constraints for the whole campaign life (GP-004, T1 core). **Hedge the 30-day half.** The claim that after ~30 days of conversion data Google bypasses signals entirely, making tweaks on a mature account a non-intervention, is a **single-source T3 assertion**. Four further Blue Sense Google episodes were read in full on 2026-08-19, two of them on PMax structure, and not one restates it. Signals are described only as directional lookalike seeds. Do not state the bypass as settled.
16. **PMax over-reports and standard shopping under-reports, and the over-report now has a size.** Apply a **30 to 40% discount to PMax reported ROAS even with brand exclusions already ON**, so a reported 5.0 reads as roughly 3.8. In the majority of audits, **70 to 80%** of conversions in campaigns the client believes are cold are warm or existing customers, which is higher than our previously banked 50%+ (GP-005/GP-006, T3). A PMax ramp can therefore hold reported ROAS flat while adding no backend revenue, and the standard shopping campaign doing the cold acquisition loses the credit.
**Run brand exclusions, and ship the remedy in the same change window.** Hard exclusions have a real cost we had not recorded: they vacate the branded Shopping slot and let competitors place above your organic branded listing (GP-034). So pair every brand exclusion with a dedicated branded Shopping campaign. Counterweight worth knowing before you spend on brand defence at all: audited nine-figure brands had nobody bidding on their brand term while spending $30-50k/month defending it, and smart bidding will bid a branded search up to $2-4 a click that a dedicated brand campaign buys near 2 cents (GA-053, GA-054).
The reading signal: a WIDENING gap between Google-reported and backend revenue is the ceiling indicator and triggers a structural audit, not a bidding tweak (AT-062). **Still contested where the ceiling sits:** GP-006 near $15-20k/month, GP-028 anywhere from $3k to $100k/month, neither with data, and no third figure arrived on 2026-08-19. Do not average them. Stop planning against $15-20k as a fixed number.

16a. **A share of Google spend is now unmeasurable.** As of Dec 2025, broad match, shopping feeds, PMax and AI Max can already serve inside AI Overviews and AI Mode with no off switch and no reporting breakout (GA-043, T3). Every "where the Google budget went" statement we make to a client inherits that unknown, so say so rather than implying full visibility.
17. **Raising target ROAS is an audience-temperature dial, not an efficiency dial.** Each increase shrinks the bid pool and cuts cold audiences first, so reported efficiency rises while new-customer acquisition falls (GA-019, T3, now three sources). Missing target then starts a self-reinforcing spiral that requires an aggressive cut to escape, not an incremental one (GA-020, T3). The dial runs both ways: setting tROAS as low as 50% is used deliberately to force top-of-funnel.

17a. **The first measured Google result in the codex, and it says Demand Gen harvested the warm pool.** A 21-day geo holdout on an Australian account, Victoria plus Queensland as the spending geo against the rest of Australia, states chosen by correlating monthly order movement and isolating p-values, 12-month baseline, 90% confidence. **New customers: no statistical relevance.** 2,857 actual against 2,771 expected, a lift of 85 where 166 was needed. New-customer orders actually FELL in both arms, down 4.5% in the control and 1.41% in the test states, so the entire apparent gain is the test states falling less far. **Returning customers: a real 6.8% lift**, 1,521 against 1,211 expected, at $39 per returning-customer order. Implied new-customer iCAC was $140 and unprofitable, and since the result is null that $140 is an arithmetic artefact, never quote it as a measured iCAC (GA-055 / GP-040 / AT-072, T2, **one experiment recorded three times, never weigh it three times**).
**The transferable rule, and it generalises far past Demand Gen: never read a blended lift number.** A blended read on this test would have shown a clean positive and been reported as acquisition when the lift was retargeting. Split new-customer from returning-customer outcomes before the test starts. The right response to a null acquisition result is also on file: the agency kept the campaign at reduced budget and reclassified it as retargeting rather than switching it off or defending it.
**Scope is tight: one advertiser, one market, one campaign type, 21 days.** The source's own baseline figures are internally inconsistent (2,500 and 254 early against 2,771 and 166 later, probably a 95% design scenario against a 90% result read, never stated). The headline survives either way, because a lift of 85 misses both thresholds. It transfers as a method and a prior, not as a verdict on PMax.

17b. **Meta's own geo lift product is gated near $100k/month spend** (AT-070). For almost every account we run, the quasi geo test is the only incrementality instrument actually available, so learn to size it backwards from the lift you would need to detect (AT-071, T2). A location doing five orders a week has no volume floor for any geo test at all.

**TikTok**

18. **3-5 ad groups per account, 3-5 genuinely different creatives per ad group; learning settles after ~25 results or 7 days; refresh into the EXISTING ad group** (TT-002/TT-003/TT-004, T1).
19. **TikTok incrementality splits, and the DECIDER is contested. Do not predict, test.** The split itself is well attested: 4 of 5 fashion lift tests strongly positive against 4 of 4 inverse holdouts outside fashion showing none, versus repeated null state-level holdouts on US accounts at $20-40k/month with one clean third-party US geo-lift.
