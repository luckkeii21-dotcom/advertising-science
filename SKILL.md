# Advertising Science

The codex is the source of truth. On Lucky's machine it lives in the vault: `Obsidian God-level Marketing Vault/God-level Marketing/wiki/science/00-Codex.md` and its topic notes. In the team package (repo `EverythingAI-Pro/advertising-science`) the same codex ships as `references/codex/`, refreshed by the daily research run; if the vault path does not exist on this machine, read `references/codex/` instead. This skill is the protocol for using and growing it.

## Evidence tiers (the spine of everything)

- **T1**: platform docs or engineering publications. How the machine is built.
- **T2**: a real test with shown data. Ours beat everyone else's.
- **T3**: practitioner claim from real spend, no shown test. Strong hypothesis, never a law.
- **T4**: theory. An idea, nothing more.

Statuses: `active`, `contested` (disagreeing credible sources, both recorded), `superseded`, `refuted` (kept forever so we never relearn it).

## Using the science (every ad decision)

1. Name the decision (scale, cut, structure, bid, creative volume, diagnosis, forecast).
2. Read the relevant topic note(s) from the codex. Meta delivery, Auction, Learning & Signal, Creative, Scaling, Attribution, Google Auction, PMax, TikTok, Emerging Channels, Marketing Math.
3. Apply claims by tier: T1/T2 can decide. T3 informs but a T3-only decision must be named as a bet, not a law. T4 never decides.
4. If codex claims conflict with a client's own account data, the account wins and the conflict gets recorded in the codex as a contested claim.
5. If the decision would make a good test, say so: every T3 we test on our own accounts becomes a T2 with our numbers. That upgrade path is the whole point.
6. If the codex is silent on the question, say "the codex is silent here", decide on judgment, and log the gap in the Harvest Log so research targets it.

## Growing the science (claim merge protocol)

New information (transcript, doc, update) is decomposed into claims. For each claim:

- **Matches an existing claim**: add the source and date to it. Never create a duplicate.
- **Contradicts an existing claim**: set status `contested`, record both sides with sources. A T1/T2 contradiction of a T3 claim supersedes it instead.
- **New**: next free ID in its topic (`MD-`, `AU-`, `LS-`, `CR-`, `SC-`, `AT-`, `GA-`, `GP-`, `TT-`, `EC-`, `MM-`), tier it honestly, date it.
- **Fluff** (motivation, repetition of banked claims, pure promotion): discard silently. We bank claims, not videos.

A claim entry looks like:

```
### MD-001 · Creative is the primary targeting input post-Andromeda
Tier: T1 · Status: active
Meta's multi-stage retrieval system selects candidate ads by predicted
per-user relevance of the creative itself; audience settings mostly bound
the candidate pool.
Sources: Meta engineering blog (Andromeda post); BlueSense 2026-07-28; Charley T.
Last touched: 2026-08-18
```

**Meta delivery and creative**

1. **Creative is the targeting.** Meta reads the creative content itself at the retrieval stage (T1 architecture, MD-022/MD-025); the avatar or scenario named in the ad decides who sees it (MD-001, T3). Verified case: niching one product's creative to one avatar halved cost per purchase, $73 to $35 at equal spend (CR-005, T2). Settings only set boundaries: location, minimum age, language, exclusions. Strongest support yet arrived 2026-08-19: an operator ran United States, 18 to 65, women, no detailed targeting and no retargeting audiences at all, changing only the avatar in the creative, and swaps the person's ethnicity in an otherwise identical ad to unlock a new group (MD-001).
**Tier guard, and it is load-bearing.** Matt Steiner, a named Meta executive over monetization infrastructure, ranking and foundational AI, describes retrieval as a per-user decision made on creative content attributes. That is **T3, not T1**, and must never be promoted. He quotes no document and shows no data in 20 minutes. A named platform employee asserting on a podcast is testimony, not documentation. The T1 half of this law rests on the engineering publications at MD-022/MD-025, nothing else.
**2026-08-24 gave the T1 half its first new documentary support in twenty months, and it is a single sentence worth memorising (MD-120).** Meta's own engineering post on GEM, the foundation model behind ads recommendations across Instagram and Facebook, lists its inputs: "two categories of features: sequence features (e.g., user activity history) and non-sequence features (e.g., **user location, ad creative representation**)." So the creative enters the central model as a first-class named feature, sitting alongside location, and GEM "is trained on ad content and user engagement data". **Be precise about what that buys.** It documents that the creative is an input. It does not say the creative decides who sees the ad, it does not rank the features, and it says nothing about avatars. The avatar claim stays T3.
**A second post supplies the mechanism underneath (MD-119).** Ranking runs in two stages: an OFFLINE user model that turns long histories into cached embeddings, and an ONLINE model that scores those against the live ad. The offline stage "strictly separates user features from ad and context features to ensure user embeddings remain independent of any particular ad candidate". So who you are is precomputed and ad-blind; the ad-specific match happens later, per ad, through target-aware attention. That is the architecture our "creative is the targeting" shorthand has been gesturing at.

1a. **The ad set has a CONTROLS section and a SUGGESTIONS section, and only one of them binds BY DEFAULT. The "by default" was added 2026-08-21 and it is a correction, because we had been stating this as an absolute.** Location, minimum age, language and exclusions are hard boundaries Meta will not cross. Interests, lookalikes and custom audiences sit in the suggestion section and Meta routinely goes beyond them. Selecting a 180-day website-visitor custom audience does **not** restrict delivery to that audience (MD-001 family, T3). Heath stopped testing interest versus lookalike versus open targeting in separate ad sets entirely for this reason, and now splits by LOCATION only. Operating consequence for our local clients: geo is the one targeting lever that still does what it says.
**The section labels are now read off Meta's own screen, which is the strongest support this law has ever had.** An operator walks the panel and names the split out loud: "In the top here, there is a control section, and then underneath is a suggested audience section. In the controls, these are hard exclusions." He demonstrates audience size collapsing when a 10-mile radius is entered, and states the suggestion behaviour just as plainly: Meta shows the lookalike people the ads, "But then once it kind of gets that, it's going to go beyond it."
**THE EXCEPTION, and anywhere the codex says these audiences bind nothing, this sentence has to travel with it.** A documented toggle makes them bind. Ticking "further limit the reach of your ads" and switching off "use as suggestion" converts the suggested audience into hard targeting, and **three independent operators have now demonstrated it in the UI**, with audience size visibly dropping and reverting on screen (MD-013). So the accurate statement is that interests, lookalikes and custom audiences are suggestions **by default and binding on request**. The operator who demonstrates it most clearly recommends against using it outside retargeting, and nobody has shown performance data on either setting. Practical consequence: an account whose targeting appears not to bind should be checked for this toggle before the behaviour is attributed to Andromeda.

**The reconciliation is testable and it is better than either side alone: exclusion binding is a MATCH-QUALITY function, not a platform constant.** An exclusion binds against the people Meta can match to your list and leaks against everyone it cannot. Andrew Faris defines the excluded population three ways at once (pixel purchase audience, full Klaviyo purchaser list, Shopify Existing Customers Plus) and reports "really tight exclusions" as a result, which is the same variable as Charley T's own 8.5 match-score floor. **So keep operating on exclusions over inclusions, and stop calling them absolute.** Settle the size of the leak on our own book by opening the audience-segment breakdown on any account with a purchaser exclusion; it has still never been opened on any of our five.
**Geo is the one input in this law with no leak reported by anybody, and 2026-08-25 sharpened what it buys (MD-131).** Location binds on WHERE and buys nothing on HOW MUCH per place: inside a multi-country ad set Meta concentrates on the cheapest countries. **Scaling**

7. **Budget step size is folklore territory, and the band is far wider than we thought.** Prescriptions now run **3% to 100% per day**, and the right question is not "what percentage" but **"what percentage, at what budget, held for how long"** (SC-001, T3, contested). Heath makes step size a decreasing function of budget: 100% at $50/day, 50% at $100/day, 20% at $800/day, each held 5 to 10 days, with a 3% daily default and a 5 to 7% hard stop (SC-100). His 3% sits below our entire previously banked range and his 100% sits above it, and both are internally consistent only because of the hold duration. Theriot confirms 20%/day gated on yesterday's cost per acquisition, doubles budget 2 to 3 times in a single day on Black Friday, and says timing within the day is irrelevant. **Three transcripts, no controlled step-size test anywhere.** A ROAS dip after a raise is upper-funnel time lag, wait before rebuilding (SC-002, T3).
**Two more operators landed on 2026-08-21 and the picture sharpened into something more useful than a range.** Piliero scales 20 to 30% every 3 to 5 days on a 7 to 14 day lookback. Shiver scales 20 to 30% every 2 to 4 days and concedes the choice is a feel, "I like to stick on 20%, I feel it feels better to me." **So the PERCENTAGE is converging across operators at roughly 20 to 30%. The HOLD DURATION is not, running 2 to 4, 3 to 5 and 5 to 10 days. The BUDGET LEVEL is stated by nobody except Heath.** Read that way, the 3% floor and the 100% ceiling stop being rival answers to one question and become cadence artefacts of different hold lengths. Piliero also adds a third independent variable nobody else addresses, headroom against target: at 4x on a 2x goal he jumps 100% or more rather than stepping. Three operators, three different governing variables, none of them answering the others, and still zero controlled tests.
**⚠ 2026-08-25 identified a THIRD quantity that gets mistaken for a step size, and it is also stated as "20%" (SC-139).** A cap on what SHARE of the daily budget may sit in test at any moment: "you never want more than 20% of your total budget being tested." The instrument is an ad-set spending MINIMUM equal to 1x target CPA, held exactly 7 days and then removed, not a budget increase at all. **So this codex now holds three different objects all quoted as 20% by the same operator community: a step ceiling per qualifying day (SC-134), a share of budget held in test (SC-139), and a throttle on a dominant ad set (SC-140). Establish which one before comparing any two.**
The same operator explicitly rejects small increments elsewhere, "just don't fall victim to only being able to scale 10 or 15%", with incremental attribution as the gate. **That is consistent with the top rung of the headroom ladder at SC-135 rather than a fourth position, so it corroborates the resolution instead of reopening it.**
**Watch the source quality on any step size quoted at us.** Piliero states his own band twice more in the same video as "10% to 30%", so one of the two figures in this law comes from a transcript that contradicts itself.
**⚠ 2026-08-23 found the reason this range has never converged, and it is a units error, not a disagreement about the world (SC-134). A step size is a CEILING PER QUALIFYING DAY. It is not a growth rate.** Theriot states "we can just keep bumping up that budget by 20% a day" on a screen-shared account, and in the same breath gives its history: $100/day to $5,000/day over "almost a year". Compounding 20% daily would cover that in about 22 days. It took roughly 365. **So the realised average step is about 1.08% per day, roughly eighteen times smaller than the ceiling, and the 20% is the most he is permitted to move on a day the CPA gate clears.** Read as steps instead of as a rate, that account had about 22 qualifying days in a year, one green light every 17 days. (This sentence read "a small fraction of one percent per day" until the 2026-08-23 teacher pass recomputed it. 1.08% is a small fraction of the 20% ceiling, not of one percent. Corrected here and at SC-134.) Anyone reading a percentage as a plan will model 50x in three weeks and be wrong by a factor of sixteen. That single distinction plausibly explains the whole 3%-to-100% spread, because operators quoting a ceiling and operators quoting a realised cadence are answering different questions. Same failure mode as live-versus-launched ad counts at law 4a.
**Two further corrections from the same pass.** Charley T publishes THREE named methods, not one number, and they sit at the bottom of the band: linear ($10 to $50/day flat), fractional (**2% daily**), and marginal (raise by the margin between actual and target CPA, 5% on a $50 target running at $45, 7-day lookback). He is also the only operator on file with a scale-down protocol: **half the speed you scaled up, then do not touch anything for 48 to 72 hours.** And **Piliero now has a THIRD incompatible figure**: "doubling spend is common practice. We don't worry about increasing by 10 or 12 or 15%", which explicitly rejects the band he is elsewhere recorded inside. ~~**His step-size testimony is no longer usable as a point estimate.**~~
**✅ RESOLVED 2026-08-24, and the resolution generalises to the whole law (SC-135). His three "incompatible" figures are one conditional ladder keyed to HEADROOM ABOVE TARGET, and each video quotes a different rung.** Stated in full on 3 August 2026: "If you are just above your target, move sizes are usually in the 5 to 10% range... you're at a 2.5. That's a big chunk above. I'm more comfortable there with a target move size of 20 to 30%. And then, if you're over-exceeding your goal... you're hitting a three or even four, then you can move your spend by massive chunks. We're talking 50-plus percent." The 3-to-5-day cadence and 7-to-14-day lookback attach to the middle rung. He adds a fourth variable nobody else in this law states: **at large absolute spend the percentage compresses because the dollar move is the real quantity**, "you might want to move it up 500, 1,000, 2,000 dollars, which might only be 5 to 10 to 15%." **His testimony is usable again, as a ladder rather than a point estimate.**
**So the 3%-to-100% spread now has two documented causes, neither of which is disagreement about the world:** the units error at SC-134 (a ceiling per qualifying day read as a growth rate) and this one (operators quoting whichever rung they most often stand on). **Before comparing any two step sizes, ask what the account's headroom against target was.** One cleanup owed: if any 10-to-30% figure in this codex traces to his 17 August 2026 video, it is wrong, because that line is about gross MARGIN and not budget. Note also that Theriot holds 20% percentage-based all the way to $5k/day, which directly opposes Heath's decreasing-function ladder at SC-100. Two operators, opposed on whether step size shrinks as budget grows, neither with a test.
8. **Minimum viable daily budget is 1x target cost per result** per day; three roster operators converged on this independently (MM-019, T3).

8a. **A cost cap buys the cheapest conversion available, and "cheapest" is greedy on TWO independent axes.**
**Catalogue axis.** A cap has no AOV term, so at a $50 cap a $50 AOV product returns 1.0 ROAS and a $100 AOV product returns 2.0 ($80 gives 1.6, $90 gives 1.8), and Meta drains the budget onto the cheapest SKU because a 1.0 ROAS conversion is the easiest to buy (AU-040, T3). Shown live: a sitewide markdown pulled capped spend onto a $46 AOV product and the fix was off-platform, raise the price and re-merchandise the collection page (AU-045, T2).
**Audience axis.** A cap can only spend when it hits the target, so it buys the people already nearest to purchase and quietly turns a prospecting campaign into de facto middle- and bottom-funnel retargeting. A second agency turned caps off in 2026 for exactly this and **never mentions AOV once** (AU-049; AU-026 reads the same mechanism temporally, harvest then crash, rather than as steady-state drift).
**Keep the two apart, because they break differently.** A shipped ROAS guardrail would fix the catalogue axis and do nothing to the audience axis. Splitting SKUs by price band, likewise.
**Two rules: never mix price bands inside one capped ad set, and never read a capped campaign's CPA as a cold-acquisition cost.** Secondary reason to avoid caps: attribution cannibalisation, "the cost cap steals conversions out of the maximize conversion value or at least it steals the last click attribution." Operating conditions on file: never mix a cap with max-conversions, run it in a separate ad account, and every named spend floor now sits above six figures a month ($100k / $250k / $300k, unreconciled).
**Caveat the whole law.** An operator on eight- and nine-figure accounts concedes there is "not really enough conclusive data to prove that this is really outperforming just optimizing for value optimizing for conversions" (AU-051), and nobody on the roster has shown capped against uncapped on the same account.
**Operating consequence for us: a client price change or promo calendar IS a delivery change. We have to see it before it ships.**

8b. **Accumulated learning lives in the ad account, not the pixel.** A fresh ad account starts from zero even on the same pixel (SC-094, T3). So client ad accounts get created under the CLIENT's Business Manager with partner access to us, never the reverse. Getting this backwards throws away the asset when the relationship changes.
**2026-08-25 adds two independent sources and, better, a hard mechanic instead of an abstraction (SC-141).** Jon Loomer: "You will very likely have a low daily spending limit to start. The only way you can get that limit increased is by maxing it out and establishing a strong history." That is an account-level trust artefact with a real cost, and it does not travel with the pixel. Ben Heath locates the loss in the account in the one case where the pixel plainly survives, a disable-and-replace: "you do miss out on some learning... which means your results out of the new ad account might be slower, might be worse for a while."
**Honest scoping, because this law has been quoted more confidently than its evidence supports. Neither cites documentation, and Loomer never says whether pixel data survives an account change.** Three T3 sources now, still undocumented. Loomer also supplies the counterweight: "A new business is absolutely at a disadvantage. But that doesn't mean that you can't get results... Meta's systems are smart enough to learn quickly."

8c. **Do NOT warm up a new ad account with traffic or engagement objectives. Launch straight onto the terminal event (LS-068, contested).** Jon Loomer: "There is no evidence that starting with top-of-the-funnel actions like traffic or engagement will be beneficial. And truly, this is likely to be counterproductive." The mechanism is law 6 applied to account setup: cheap TOF optimization finds the people most likely to click and least likely to buy, and "then your history and built-in remarketing audiences will be composed of low-quality engagement."
**Contested by an operator who does the opposite deliberately.** Charley T runs a few-dollars-a-day engagement campaign on every organic Instagram post to pre-train delivery, claiming CPMs stabilise and AOV rises. **Neither shows data, both T3.** The scope difference that may dissolve it: Loomer is describing the optimization event of the MONEY campaign, Charley T a separate cheap campaign alongside it. Relevant because every new client starts on a fresh ad account and the ritual costs real weeks if it is wrong.

**Attribution**

9. **Turn view-through attribution off and audit for it**; rebuild any ad set where view-through exceeds ~10% of conversions (AT-012, T2).
**The structural mechanism, new 2026-08-19 and it upgrades this law.** Meta cannot drop a browser cookie from the app, so the impression-to-conversion join only exists if a pixel event already fired. **Every 1-day-view conversion is therefore a prior site visitor by construction.** That moves the claim from "view-through skews warm" to "a cold person cannot produce one" (AT-011). Paired with a real account reading 75x ROAS on $2-3k/month Meta against $70k/month Google.
**The exception, and it is an AND condition:** Meta is the sole traffic driver AND the purchase cycle is long. That covers 5-10% of ecommerce or fewer, and the trade you are making is signal starvation, not accuracy (AT-076).
House rule stands: never call spend "wasted" from a last-click zero. Contested scale: one auditor finds 1-day view running 30-50% of reported ROAS as the norm, which would make the 10% threshold a healthy exception rather than a typical account (AT-037, T3). AT-037 also carries the delivery half: the 7-day-click plus 1-day-view setting biases DELIVERY toward view-through, not only the report, so roughly 90% of accounts overspend on existing customers and the fix is two-sided. The "underreports only 10-20%" figure inherits the sign problem in 9a below (AT-008, **downgraded T2 to T3 on 2026-08-19**: both sources are the same speaker citing Haus and CTC meta-analyses from memory, studies never shown).

9a. **The incrementality benchmarks expired, and the SIGN of the error is account-dependent. This is the 2026-08-19 correction.** The old rule fixed the direction: true incremental sits near 28-day click, roughly 7-day click times 110-115%, i.e. always correct UPWARD. **That is wrong as a per-account rule.** One shown account ran 4.77 on 7-day click against 3.25 incremental, which is 47% OVER-attribution, while the same operator argues a second account UNDER-attributes because its acquisition MER runs above the platform number (AT-074, T2). The sign is a property of the account, set by channel mix and AOV.
**110-115% is a population average across pooled tests. Never apply it to a single account without reconciling against aMER first.** The multiplier was also derived on the OLD wider 7-day-click column, so it does not survive the March 2026 outbound-click-only redefinition either (AT-008 cross-referenced to AT-036). Never quote it against post-March-2026 data as current.
10. **Meta redefined the attribution windows in March 2026, and this is now T1, confirmed off Meta's own screen** (AT-036, **upgraded T3 to T1 on 2026-08-20**; delivery-side half at AU-017). Old option set: click-through, engaged view (video only), view-through. New: the "video only" qualifier is gone, and engaged view now counts **an ad click including a like, share or save, followed by a conversion within 1 day**. He dates it explicitly: "this changed in March." A second operator independently places it around March 2026. So 7-day-click ROAS fell overnight with no change in real performance.
**Tier discipline: the surface and Meta's on-screen definitions are T1. Any operator's interpretation of what to do about it stays T3.** ~~One detail is banked but NOT settled: a 5-second-watched or 97%-of-length threshold was read off the screen, but which window it governs was rendered ambiguously and is marked unverified.~~

Everything else, the like, the comment, the share, the "see more" expansion, the click-to-expand video, moved to engage-through. The panel now exposes three independent windows at ad set level behind "show more options": click-through 7 or 1 day, engage-through 1 day or none, view-through 1 day or none, with Meta actively discouraging setting engage-through to none.
**⚠ And the artefact has a SIZE now, which is client-facing (AT-097). Video-led campaigns absorb the biggest drop, statics the smallest. Anyone comparing creative formats across the March 2026 boundary will conclude video got worse. It did not.** The 97% clause is still odd as written in both transcripts and has not been checked against Meta's documentation.
**Reporting consequence, and it is a client-facing one:** a step-down in 7-day-click ROAS dated to that window is a definitional artefact and must never be written up as a performance decline.
**The instrument to settle our own view-through share is live and we have never opened it (AT-082).** Columns, then Compare attribution settings, splits one conversion total four ways: 1-day click, 7-day click, 1-day view, 1-day engaged view. The MECHANISM behind it flipped to contested on 2026-08-19, because three incompatible explanations are now on file **from the same speaker**: a factor derived from conversion-lift experiments run by OTHER advertisers in your niche (2026), a permanent 10% holdout of your own target demographic (Jun 2025), and the arithmetic midpoint of 1-day and 7-day click (May 2025). **None is documented** (AT-035, contested; the same description also sits at MD-034).

11d. **Warm-audience leakage now has three shown accounts and a frequency read nobody had published (MD-133, T2).** Sam Piliero, breakdown open: "Our engaged audience has spent $161,000 out of the $317,000 in ad spend. 11c. **New-customer spend is NOT a proxy for incremental spend (AT-099, T3).** Andrew Faris names it as his central argument: "Some spend going towards new customers is actually not incremental, and plenty of spend targeted at existing customers is incremental." His structural argument for the second half is the strong part: email and SMS cannot reach the whole past-customer base, open rates "top out at like 50%", so paid retention reaches people who would otherwise not have heard.

11a. **Period-end reports read the most incomplete days as final.** Conversions keep landing after a window closes, so the newest days in any report are still filling in. Every week-over-week and month-over-month comparison therefore structurally flatters the EARLIER window. Cut the same tail off both windows or state the uncertainty (AT-068, T3). At low conversion counts a zero-conversion day is baseline variance, not a finding (AT-031). And never write "Meta broke" or "platform issue" without the spend-spike-at-strange-CPM signature; it is the least likely explanation and the only one with no plan attached (AT-066).

12. **Prune by MONEY LOST, never by low spend as such.** In a shown 89-ad campaign the winning ads and the losing ads consumed the same budget, $48,594 against $48,800, while the losers ran a $230 CPA and a negative $0.98 gross profit per transaction (MD-046, T2). **Reconciling this with law 3's new low-spender contest, because they look opposed and are not.** SC-050's losers were FUNDED: $48,800 of real spend at negative profit per transaction, so cutting them removed a measured loss. Blue Sense is describing near-zero-spend ads carrying near-zero loss. So: an ad that has spent real money at negative profit per transaction goes, an ad that has spent nothing stays. **The boundary is untested, so state it as a hypothesis.** This still does not license killing top spenders, which law 3 forbids.

Every "where the Google budget went" statement we make to a client inherits that unknown, so say so rather than implying full visibility.
17. **Raising target ROAS is an audience-temperature dial, not an efficiency dial.** Each increase shrinks the bid pool and cuts cold audiences first, so reported efficiency rises while new-customer acquisition falls (GA-019, T3, now three sources). Missing target then starts a self-reinforcing spiral that requires an aggressive cut to escape, not an incremental one (GA-020, T3). The dial runs both ways: setting tROAS as low as 50% is used deliberately to force top-of-funnel.

17a. **The first measured Google result in the codex, and it says Demand Gen harvested the warm pool.** A 21-day geo holdout on an Australian account, Victoria plus Queensland as the spending geo against the rest of Australia, states chosen by correlating monthly order movement and isolating p-values, 12-month baseline, 90% confidence. **New customers: no statistical relevance.** 2,857 actual against 2,771 expected, a lift of 85 where 166 was needed. New-customer orders actually FELL in both arms, down 4.5% in the control and 1.41% in the test states, so the entire apparent gain is the test states falling less far. **Returning customers: a real 6.8% lift**, 1,521 against 1,211 expected, at $39 per returning-customer order. Implied new-customer iCAC was $140 and unprofitable, and since the result is null that $140 is an arithmetic artefact, never quote it as a measured iCAC (GA-055 / GP-040 / AT-072, T2, **one experiment recorded three times, never weigh it three times**).
**The transferable rule, and it generalises far past Demand Gen: never read a blended lift number.** A blended read on this test would have shown a clean positive and been reported as acquisition when the lift was retargeting. Split new-customer from returning-customer outcomes before the test starts. The right response to a null acquisition result is also on file: the agency kept the campaign at reduced budget and reclassified it as retargeting rather than switching it off or defending it.
**Scope is tight: one advertiser, one market, one campaign type, 21 days.** The source's own baseline figures are internally inconsistent (2,500 and 254 early against 2,771 and 166 later, probably a 95% design scenario against a 90% result read, never stated). The headline survives either way, because a lift of 85 misses both thresholds. It transfers as a method and a prior, not as a verdict on PMax.

17b. **Meta's own geo lift product is gated near $100k/month spend** (AT-070). For almost every account we run, the quasi geo test is the only incrementality instrument actually available, so learn to size it backwards from the lift you would need to detect (AT-071, T2). **Economics**

20. **Gross margin sets the ROAS breakeven** (87 points of margin is profitable at 1.3x; 50-60 points needs ~2.0x); fix margin before blaming ads (MM-010, T3). A live account now sits behind it: 56.77 points of gross margin at $58.50 blended CAC leaves 12.61 points of contribution, and contribution margin percent is the ceiling on net profit when returning customers are under 15% of revenue, so 12.61% minus 10% opex leaves 2% maximum net.
**LTGP:CAC of 2-3 is the scale zone, and on 2026-08-20 the contested numerator got an answer better than a winner: operators genuinely differ, so the ratio is not portable** (MM-002). Three of them state their numerator out loud. **Theriot uses REVENUE, pre-COGS, twice, on screen**, so anyone quoting his LTV:CAC figures as gross-profit-based is misreading him. **Faris uses GROSS PROFIT**, applying 60 points of margin first and keeping raw-revenue LTV as a separate column. **Blue Sense uses GROSS PROFIT at cart level.**
**The finding IS the operating rule: state which numerator a ratio uses before acting on it or comparing it to anyone else's.** A 2.5 on revenue and a 2.5 on gross profit are different businesses, and at 50 points of margin they are a factor of two apart. Do not average them and do not assume.
