# Advertising Science

The codex is the source of truth. On Lucky's machine it lives in the vault: `Obsidian God-level Marketing Vault/God-level Marketing/wiki/science/00-Codex.md` and its topic notes. In the team package (repo `EverythingAI-Pro/advertising-science`) the same codex ships as `references/codex/`, refreshed by the daily research run; if the vault path does not exist on this machine, read `references/codex/` instead. This skill is the protocol for using and growing it.

## Evidence tiers (the spine of everything)

- **T1**: platform docs or engineering publications. How the machine is built.
- **T2**: a real test with shown data. Ours beat everyone else's.
- **T3**: practitioner claim from real spend, no shown test. Strong hypothesis, never a law.
- **T4**: theory. An idea, nothing more.

Statuses: `active`, `contested` (disagreeing credible sources, both recorded), `superseded`, `refuted` (kept forever so we never relearn it).

## Using the science (every ad decision)

1. Name the decision (scale, cut, structure, bid, creative volume, diagnosis, forecast).
2. Read the relevant topic note(s) from the codex. Meta delivery, Auction, Learning & Signal, Creative, Scaling, Attribution, Google Auction, PMax, TikTok, Emerging Channels, Marketing Math.
3. Apply claims by tier: T1/T2 can decide. T3 informs but a T3-only decision must be named as a bet, not a law. T4 never decides.
4. If codex claims conflict with a client's own account data, the account wins and the conflict gets recorded in the codex as a contested claim.
5. If the decision would make a good test, say so: every T3 we test on our own accounts becomes a T2 with our numbers. That upgrade path is the whole point.
6. If the codex is silent on the question, say "the codex is silent here", decide on judgment, and log the gap in the Harvest Log so research targets it.

## Growing the science (claim merge protocol)

New information (transcript, doc, update) is decomposed into claims. For each claim:

- **Matches an existing claim**: add the source and date to it. Never create a duplicate.
- **Contradicts an existing claim**: set status `contested`, record both sides with sources. A T1/T2 contradiction of a T3 claim supersedes it instead.
- **New**: next free ID in its topic (`MD-`, `AU-`, `LS-`, `CR-`, `SC-`, `AT-`, `GA-`, `GP-`, `TT-`, `EC-`, `MM-`), tier it honestly, date it.
- **Fluff** (motivation, repetition of banked claims, pure promotion): discard silently. We bank claims, not videos.

A claim entry looks like:

```
### MD-001 · Creative is the primary targeting input post-Andromeda
Tier: T1 · Status: active
Meta's multi-stage retrieval system selects candidate ads by predicted
per-user relevance of the creative itself; audience settings mostly bound
the candidate pool.
Sources: Meta engineering blog (Andromeda post); BlueSense 2026-07-28; Charley T.
Last touched: 2026-08-18
```

**Meta delivery and creative**

1. **Creative is the targeting.** Meta reads the creative content itself at the retrieval stage (T1 architecture, MD-022/MD-025); the avatar or scenario named in the ad decides who sees it (MD-001, T3). Verified case: niching one product's creative to one avatar halved cost per purchase, $73 to $35 at equal spend (CR-005, T2). Settings only set boundaries: location, minimum age, language, exclusions. Strongest support yet arrived 2026-08-19: an operator ran United States, 18 to 65, women, no detailed targeting and no retargeting audiences at all, changing only the avatar in the creative, and swaps the person's ethnicity in an otherwise identical ad to unlock a new group (MD-001).
**Tier guard, and it is load-bearing.** Matt Steiner, a named Meta executive over monetization infrastructure, ranking and foundational AI, describes retrieval as a per-user decision made on creative content attributes. That is **T3, not T1**, and must never be promoted. He quotes no document and shows no data in 20 minutes. A named platform employee asserting on a podcast is testimony, not documentation. The T1 half of this law rests on the engineering publications at MD-022/MD-025, nothing else.

1a. **The ad set has a CONTROLS section and a SUGGESTIONS section, and only one of them binds BY DEFAULT. The "by default" was added 2026-08-21 and it is a correction, because we had been stating this as an absolute.** Location, minimum age, language and exclusions are hard boundaries Meta will not cross. Interests, lookalikes and custom audiences sit in the suggestion section and Meta routinely goes beyond them. Selecting a 180-day website-visitor custom audience does **not** restrict delivery to that audience (MD-001 family, T3). Heath stopped testing interest versus lookalike versus open targeting in separate ad sets entirely for this reason, and now splits by LOCATION only. Operating consequence for our local clients: geo is the one targeting lever that still does what it says.
**The section labels are now read off Meta's own screen, which is the strongest support this law has ever had.** An operator walks the panel and names the split out loud: "In the top here, there is a control section, and then underneath is a suggested audience section. In the controls, these are hard exclusions." He demonstrates audience size collapsing when a 10-mile radius is entered, and states the suggestion behaviour just as plainly: Meta shows the lookalike people the ads, "But then once it kind of gets that, it's going to go beyond it."
**THE EXCEPTION, and anywhere the codex says these audiences bind nothing, this sentence has to travel with it.** A documented toggle makes them bind. Ticking "further limit the reach of your ads" and switching off "use as suggestion" converts the suggested audience into hard targeting, and **three independent operators have now demonstrated it in the UI**, with audience size visibly dropping and reverting on screen (MD-013). So the accurate statement is that interests, lookalikes and custom audiences are suggestions **by default and binding on request**. The operator who demonstrates it most clearly recommends against using it outside retargeting, and nobody has shown performance data on either setting. Practical consequence: an account whose targeting appears not to bind should be checked for this toggle before the behaviour is attributed to Andromeda.
**2026-08-23 added the strongest UI reading yet AND caught the operator contradicting himself ten days later.** Ben Heath names Meta's two sections off the screen: "we've got controls, so those are hard boundaries, hard constraints, Meta won't go outside that targeting. It's basically just location and a few other things, but then we've got within the suggestion audience... include these custom audiences." He then demonstrates the hardening path naming BOTH switches, "further limit the reach of your ads" plus unticking "use as suggestion", after which "Meta is going to put ads in front of these people and these people only." **Then on 2026-03-27, ten days later, he says detailed targeting is "always going to be added in as suggestions."** The word "always" is wrong as stated, and the two reconcile only through the partial rollout he mentions himself ("Not everyone has this"). **Never accept a blanket "always a suggestion" claim without asking which account and which date.** The measured consequence of getting this wrong is now on file at MD-116.

1b. **Audience segments (new / engaged / existing) are a REPORTING breakdown, not a delivery control. This is resolved on Meta's own documentation, 2026-08-20, and it had been contested for three runs** (MD-019, **T1, contested flag removed**). Meta's help article is titled "Create audience segments in Meta Ads Manager" and opens "You can use audience segments to enable breakdown reporting." You read the split under Breakdown, By demographics, next to age, gender and region. In the Marketing API it is `user_segment_key`, a **breakdowns parameter on the insights endpoint**, with no matching field on the campaign object.
**There WAS a real delivery control and Meta killed it** (MD-019b, T1). `existing_customer_budget_percentage` was an ad set parameter sitting among the bidding fields, capping "the maximum percentage of the budget that can be spent on the existing customers." Meta's help centre now reads "is no longer available," and the API docs say campaigns using it "will be paused" at **Marketing API v26.0, which shipped 29 July 2026**. That is why no practitioner has caught up: the removal is weeks old.
**So Ben Heath was describing a real product that Meta removed, and Charley T is right about the product as it stands.** The only segment-level budget control left is structural: two ad sets, custom audience included in one and excluded in the other, per-ad-set spend caps, and audience relaxation forced off. **Any playbook that says "define your segments and Meta splits the budget for you" is now wrong.** Setting the definitions is still free and still buys a clean spend split by segment, so keep doing it, for reporting.
**One loose end, deliberately not folded in.** Meta's **value rules** now expose an "audiences" criterion that applies a **bid multiplier** by audience label, with "new audiences" selectable, dated to roughly the week of 2026-06-23 (MD-100, **T1 for surface existence only**). A bid multiplier is delivery. It is a different, richer taxonomy than the three segments above, it has not been checked against Meta's documentation, and nobody on the roster has run it. Check the docs before treating it as a working segment-level delivery control.
2. **Near-duplicate collapse is now CONTESTED. Speak it as a bet, not a mechanism.** The position: near-duplicate ads collapse into one entity ID with zero unique reach, so diversity must live at the concept level (angle x offer x persona), and a format change (static to video) escapes the collapse (MD-003). Ben Heath arrives on the collapse side, saying Meta enters only one hook variant into the auction. Against it, Blue Sense in Jan 2026: "it might count the bodies as the same ad and group them together. Well, I can tell you it doesn't because this is still working incredibly well in our accounts," backed by a 27-April ad revived to the account's top two by 4 newly shot hooks. Assertion against assertion, **nobody has shown an entity ID or a unique-reach figure on either side** (MD-003, contested, both T3). Boundary worth keeping: the rebuttal is scoped to swaps that change the VISUAL, which is consistent with MD-043. The practical advice survives either way, because concept-level diversity is cheap insurance.
**2026-08-23: the collapse side got its most confident statement yet and still shows nothing.** Fraser Cottrell: "Every single ad in your ad account is labeled with a similarity score. This is behind the scenes in Meta, but essentially it looks at all your ads and goes, 'That one's very similar to that one.' and then treats them as the same asset." He cites no source and is not a Meta employee, so the mechanism is T4. **His entire evidence is eyeballing two ad-account thumbnail grids, and he concedes on camera that the account he calls collapsed is currently working: "They might be seeing results from this, but it's only a matter of time until they don't anymore."** That is the unfalsifiable form of the claim. **After four operators and eight months, still nobody on either side has shown an entity ID or a unique-reach figure.** One real difference from how we state this law: his diversity unit is FORMAT plus audience segment (UGC, podcast, founder-led), not angle x offer x persona, which is a weaker standard, and he never addresses whether a format change escapes the collapse.
3. **Do not run the doom cycle. BOTH halves of this law are now contested, including the clause we thought was safe.** The original position: the top-spending "worst CPA" ad is usually the TOF engine, killing it can crater the ad set, and churn-killing ads into replacements is the most-cited reason accounts stop scaling (MD-010 T3, SC-012 T2).
**Top spenders.** Charley T says kill the budget hog so the others can spend. Against him: Faris across 100+ accounts leaves high spenders on in 99%+ of cases and reports roughly 30% more spend at the same target just from switching a previous buyer's paused high spenders back on; Blue Sense never lists killing the hog as an option at all; Theriot shows a CBO going $98 to $51 to about $35 cost per purchase with two concurrent top spenders and no kill anywhere (SC-099, T2); Piliero never kills a high spender and reads at ad-set level. Meta's own ranking lead adds that the system already reallocates off weak creative. **Score is 3 voices to 1 plus a platform statement, and still zero controlled data on either side** (SC-008, SC-085, both contested). Weight of opinion is not evidence.
**Low spenders, flipped to contested 2026-08-19.** We used to say killing LOW spenders is always safe. Blue Sense contradicts it head-on: "what a lot of people do, which is a massive mistake, is they just come in here and turn these ads off. Don't do that." Two mechanisms given: the starved ad plays an assist role in the conversion sequence, and it is the ad set's reserve when the current winner fatigues (SC-009). Heath and Theriot both supply the same assist mechanism independently, and Heath gives the diagnostic: an ad taking spend with weak conversions sitting next to a cheaper ad Meta is under-funding.
**The first shown data arrived 2026-08-20, and with it a principle that only half works.** A real account at $350,000 spend, 2,800 purchases, $172 AOV, $125 blended CPA and **198 live ads** had **95 ads turned off at once**, and those 95 jointly held **10.89% of total account spend** (T2, figures on screen). The stated principle: **kill risk scales with the SPEND SHARE removed, not the ad count.** "Turning off 95 ads is the same thing as turning off one ad that spent 11 out of $100." Priced that way, the fight between "never kill low spenders" and the 89-ads-to-4 prune stops being a contradiction, because Blue Sense is defending near-zero-spend ads where removal is a near-zero move.
**Do not promote this to law, and here is why.** The prune at law 12 removed roughly HALF its campaign's spend, $48,800 against the winners' $48,594, which the spend-share principle would score as the maximum-risk move available. It 10x'd spend anyway. So spend share explains why culling a starved tail is cheap and **fails to predict the one case we have where a big cull worked**. It is T4, stated not measured, and untested (SC-107). Nobody has shown data on whether killing low spenders helps or hurts.
**⚠ 2026-08-23: the one voice on the kill side is not saying what we recorded. Correct this before repeating the 3-to-1 score.** Read in full, Charley T never says kill the budget hog. He says kill the worst-PROFIT ad among the top spenders: "Look at the ads spending the most money, and identify the ones making you the least profit per sale." And he caps it, which nobody else in this contest does: "Turning off ads is the same thing as changing the budget. So, never turn off more than 20% of the spend on any day" (SC-132). **Nobody in this contest defends killing a PROFITABLE high spender.** The four leave-on voices are defending profitable top spenders and he is cutting unprofitable ones, so most of the apparent split was a disagreement about which ad was being described. The surviving question is narrow and still open: whether an unprofitable-LOOKING top spender is actually unprofitable, or is a top-of-funnel engine whose value lands on other ads. Still zero controlled data on either side. It also reconciles with law 12, where the ads cut were funded AND at negative profit per transaction.
**What still survives on every side: judge at ad-set level, never on the ad's own row.**
Settle it on our own accounts, the test is cheap and reversible: kill the top spender for 2 to 3 days and read ACCOUNT-level cost per result. Counterweight still on file: CTC's back-test found manual on/off decisions had no measurable impact at all (both T2).
4. **Relevance is a bid.** A more relevant ad wins auctions against higher bidders (AU-002, T1). Creative quality is the cheapest CPM lever we have.

Theriot and Blue Sense count LAUNCHED ads per week or month.** Those are different quantities, which is why the numbers never reconciled (CR-154).
**What each operator actually means, now on the record.** Heath: 20-plus, explicitly **per ad set, live concurrently**, against an old Meta recommendation of 5 or 6, with no upper limit he manages to because the binding constraint is creative production capacity. Theriot: across five transcripts read in full, **not one number is a concurrency count**. Blue Sense: four distinct units appear and none is a live account ad count. Shiver: 5 ads in one ad set, one offer each, around $200/day.
**The vocabulary half is solved. State the unit before quoting any number, and never set a launch rate against a concurrency count.** The genuine disagreement that survives is narrow and it is only this: how many ads should be LIVE AND ELIGIBLE at once.
**On 2026-08-21 that surviving question got two more answers and they do not agree either.** Live concurrently in one ad set: Piliero says 4 to 6, "that seems to be the sweet spot these days". Shiver launches 5 to 10 and names the anti-pattern, "Do not make 10 different ad sets with one ad inside and have $5 each." Heath says 20-plus with no upper limit. **Three operators with real books, a 10x spread, zero comparative tests between them.**

So copy tests never consume ad slots. That confirms Blue Sense's long-standing position that colour, headline and primary-text changes are the SAME ad, and it means anyone counting headline variants as separate ads is inflating their own number.

The swap that does have a real case is a NEW SHOOT WITH A NEW PERSON: a 2-body 9-hook testimonial running about 5x the account's next best ad fatigued at 6-7 months and returned to the account's top two at the same daily spend throughput after **4 newly shot hooks of a different person** (CR-124, contested, T3, no CPA or spend shown). The operator's own ceiling: "often just a change in wording can get a lot of people in, but it's not going to get everyone in. We do need a change in visuals."
**Half of this disagreement is vocabulary.** Theriot lands opposite only because he DEFINES a hook swap as the 3 variants inside one ad idea, identical script, voiceover and text hook, varying 3 to 5 seconds of visual. Under that definition a hook swap can never be a new argument, because the argument is held constant by construction. His "new avatar plus new format plus new angle" and Blue Sense's "new hooks of a different person" are the same move under two names. Haynes filming 25-30 separate arguments is the same family again.
**The surviving empirical question is much narrower than the debate: does re-cutting the first 3 to 5 seconds of an existing shoot revive a fatigued winner?**
**2026-08-23: somebody now CLAIMS it, with no data, and the definition half is settled on screen.** Fraser Cottrell, on a hook spliced onto an existing body, says it "put this ad back on the map when it had fatigued." That is the first direct claim of a re-cut revival in the codex, and it carries no before, no after, no curve, and no spend. He attributes the result to a guess ("That was probably the main reason why it performed"), and in the same transcript a different hook swap on the same body failed outright. **So the answer moves from "nobody has run it" to "one operator asserts it and shows nothing." Do not treat that as evidence.** Separately, Theriot's definition is now confirmed in the UI, verbatim: "All three videos are exactly the same uh just a slightly different hook. Identical videos varying only the VISUAL hook, which means a hook swap under his definition cannot carry a new argument by construction. **The vocabulary half of this law is closed. Anyone claiming a hook swap should carry a new argument is describing a different object.** Also note the 10-hooks-for-the-effort-of-one-body arithmetic counts EDIT effort only and does not cover the shoot day the case actually required. 4c. **Opportunity Score is a vendor recommendation engine, not a grade. Never report it to a client as one.** Meta's own description: a 0 to 100 real-time health check on the account and its ads that issues ranked optimisation recommendations, shown in one Meta post instructing an advertiser to move from online-only to omnichannel optimisation (MD-099, **T1 for existence and surface ONLY**, same guard as 19b). **Learning and signal**

5. **Never significant-edit a working ad set.** Targeting, creative, optimization event, bid strategy, adding an ad, or a 7-day pause always reset learning (LS-002/LS-003, T1). An ad set needs ~50 optimization events in the week after its last significant edit to exit learning (LS-001, T1).
**Budget moves reset it only when large, and the threshold is size-dependent.** A 3% daily raise is unlikely to reset. A doubling-scale raise does, with a **1 to 2 day resettle** before results are readable. Meta's own illustration is a 10x, $100 to $1,000; Heath's 2x ($50 to $100/day) is T3 practitioner from a $300M book with no learning-status data shown. The 1-2 day resettle is the first resettle duration on file anywhere in the codex, so quote it as a practitioner figure.
**2026-08-23: the 50 is soft in BOTH directions, and most accounts never clear it at all.** Heath relays Meta's number and then contradicts it from his own book: ad sets exit at 20 or 40 a week, "There doesn't seem to be an absolute number", and "I don't think I've ever seen an example where a campaign or an ad set has been running more than 50 results per week and has ended up in learning limited." He gives the only learning-phase DURATION on file, 24 to 48 hours, and reports ad sets flipping to active with no rise in weekly conversions, crediting stability rather than volume. Blue Sense goes further: **"for 90% of ad accounts, what do you do to be able to exit the learning phase and get at least 50 purchase events a week? The answer is you don't."** His mechanism for what happens instead is banked at LS-063 and is **T3 assertion, not documentation**: Meta cascades UPSTREAM to the nearest event with enough volume, purchases then clicks then impressions, at which point ad-level CPM becomes the allocation signal.
**The operating consequence of that cascade is the most actionable thing in this pass and it applies to every account we run.** Video CPMs sit systematically above image CPMs, so in the sub-threshold state a mixed-format ad set pools its spend into the statics and **the video never gets read at all**. His rule: structure mediums into separate ad sets so the cheap format cannot starve the expensive one. Every client we run is permanently below the threshold and we routinely mix statics and video in one ad set.
6. **The results column IS the targeting.** Meta finds more people like whoever fired the optimization event, so feed it only qualified conversions (LS-008, T3, active).
**The contested claim is LS-009, volume-first against quality-first. Say which side you are betting on.** The quality side now has three voices to one: Charley T's full lead-gen chain (all lead events look identical to Meta, so the model learns to find form-fillers; pushing CPL down makes quality worse; **Andromeda executing the objective BETTER is precisely why this degraded rather than improved**; fix is a custom "engaged lead" pixel event on the email destination page, then send the qualified lead back as a purchase valued at average lead value), plus Piliero's ecommerce "purchase only, never add to cart, no exception." **Do not read that as settled.** Nobody on either side ran a controlled test, and the volume side holds the stronger mechanism, because the ~50-events-a-week threshold at LS-001 is T1 documentation and every qualification gate cuts volume toward that floor. Stacking assertions on one pan is consensus, not evidence.
**The reconciliation to actually operate on (LS-026): take the deepest event that still clears learning.** That makes the answer volume-dependent rather than doctrinal, so the volume side is right at low volume and the quality side at high.
**The volume pole got weaker on 2026-08-21, and the reason is arithmetic rather than argument (LS-060).** On a high-ticket lead-gen account the learning floor is not reachable at any sane budget, so "protect event volume" is defending something you never had. Worked on camera at a $500 cost per booked call: 50 conversions in 7 days is $25,000 a week. **Scaling**

7. **Budget step size is folklore territory, and the band is far wider than we thought.** Prescriptions now run **3% to 100% per day**, and the right question is not "what percentage" but **"what percentage, at what budget, held for how long"** (SC-001, T3, contested). Heath makes step size a decreasing function of budget: 100% at $50/day, 50% at $100/day, 20% at $800/day, each held 5 to 10 days, with a 3% daily default and a 5 to 7% hard stop (SC-100). His 3% sits below our entire previously banked range and his 100% sits above it, and both are internally consistent only because of the hold duration. Theriot confirms 20%/day gated on yesterday's cost per acquisition, doubles budget 2 to 3 times in a single day on Black Friday, and says timing within the day is irrelevant. **Three transcripts, no controlled step-size test anywhere.** A ROAS dip after a raise is upper-funnel time lag, wait before rebuilding (SC-002, T3).
**Two more operators landed on 2026-08-21 and the picture sharpened into something more useful than a range.** Piliero scales 20 to 30% every 3 to 5 days on a 7 to 14 day lookback. Shiver scales 20 to 30% every 2 to 4 days and concedes the choice is a feel, "I like to stick on 20%, I feel it feels better to me." **So the PERCENTAGE is converging across operators at roughly 20 to 30%. The HOLD DURATION is not, running 2 to 4, 3 to 5 and 5 to 10 days. The BUDGET LEVEL is stated by nobody except Heath.** Read that way, the 3% floor and the 100% ceiling stop being rival answers to one question and become cadence artefacts of different hold lengths. Piliero also adds a third independent variable nobody else addresses, headroom against target: at 4x on a 2x goal he jumps 100% or more rather than stepping. Three operators, three different governing variables, none of them answering the others, and still zero controlled tests.
**Watch the source quality on any step size quoted at us.** Piliero states his own band twice more in the same video as "10% to 30%", so one of the two figures in this law comes from a transcript that contradicts itself.
**⚠ 2026-08-23 found the reason this range has never converged, and it is a units error, not a disagreement about the world (SC-134). A step size is a CEILING PER QUALIFYING DAY. It is not a growth rate.** Theriot states "we can just keep bumping up that budget by 20% a day" on a screen-shared account, and in the same breath gives its history: $100/day to $5,000/day over "almost a year". Compounding 20% daily would cover that in about 22 days. It took roughly 365. **So the realised average step is a small fraction of one percent per day and the 20% is the most he is permitted to move on a day the CPA gate clears.** Anyone reading a percentage as a plan will model 50x in three weeks and be wrong by a factor of sixteen. That single distinction plausibly explains the whole 3%-to-100% spread, because operators quoting a ceiling and operators quoting a realised cadence are answering different questions. Same failure mode as live-versus-launched ad counts at law 4a.
**Two further corrections from the same pass.** Charley T publishes THREE named methods, not one number, and they sit at the bottom of the band: linear ($10 to $50/day flat), fractional (**2% daily**), and marginal (raise by the margin between actual and target CPA, 5% on a $50 target running at $45, 7-day lookback). He is also the only operator on file with a scale-down protocol: **half the speed you scaled up, then do not touch anything for 48 to 72 hours.** And **Piliero now has a THIRD incompatible figure**: "doubling spend is common practice. We don't worry about increasing by 10 or 12 or 15%", which explicitly rejects the band he is elsewhere recorded inside. **His step-size testimony is no longer usable as a point estimate. Quote him only as a range, with the date attached.** Note also that Theriot holds 20% percentage-based all the way to $5k/day, which directly opposes Heath's decreasing-function ladder at SC-100. Two operators, opposed on whether step size shrinks as budget grows, neither with a test.
8. **Minimum viable daily budget is 1x target cost per result** per day; three roster operators converged on this independently (MM-019, T3).

8a. **A cost cap buys the cheapest conversion available, and "cheapest" is greedy on TWO independent axes.**
**Catalogue axis.** A cap has no AOV term, so at a $50 cap a $50 AOV product returns 1.0 ROAS and a $100 AOV product returns 2.0 ($80 gives 1.6, $90 gives 1.8), and Meta drains the budget onto the cheapest SKU because a 1.0 ROAS conversion is the easiest to buy (AU-040, T3). Shown live: a sitewide markdown pulled capped spend onto a $46 AOV product and the fix was off-platform, raise the price and re-merchandise the collection page (AU-045, T2).
**Audience axis.** A cap can only spend when it hits the target, so it buys the people already nearest to purchase and quietly turns a prospecting campaign into de facto middle- and bottom-funnel retargeting. A second agency turned caps off in 2026 for exactly this and **never mentions AOV once** (AU-049; AU-026 reads the same mechanism temporally, harvest then crash, rather than as steady-state drift).
**Keep the two apart, because they break differently.** A shipped ROAS guardrail would fix the catalogue axis and do nothing to the audience axis. Splitting SKUs by price band, likewise.
**Two rules: never mix price bands inside one capped ad set, and never read a capped campaign's CPA as a cold-acquisition cost.** Secondary reason to avoid caps: attribution cannibalisation, "the cost cap steals conversions out of the maximize conversion value or at least it steals the last click attribution." Operating conditions on file: never mix a cap with max-conversions, run it in a separate ad account, and every named spend floor now sits above six figures a month ($100k / $250k / $300k, unreconciled).
**Caveat the whole law.** An operator on eight- and nine-figure accounts concedes there is "not really enough conclusive data to prove that this is really outperforming just optimizing for value optimizing for conversions" (AU-051), and nobody on the roster has shown capped against uncapped on the same account.
**Operating consequence for us: a client price change or promo calendar IS a delivery change. We have to see it before it ships.**

8b. **Accumulated learning lives in the ad account, not the pixel.** A fresh ad account starts from zero even on the same pixel (SC-094, T3). So client ad accounts get created under the CLIENT's Business Manager with partner access to us, never the reverse. Getting this backwards throws away the asset when the relationship changes.

**Attribution**

9. **Turn view-through attribution off and audit for it**; rebuild any ad set where view-through exceeds ~10% of conversions (AT-012, T2).
**The structural mechanism, new 2026-08-19 and it upgrades this law.** Meta cannot drop a browser cookie from the app, so the impression-to-conversion join only exists if a pixel event already fired. **Every 1-day-view conversion is therefore a prior site visitor by construction.** That moves the claim from "view-through skews warm" to "a cold person cannot produce one" (AT-011). Paired with a real account reading 75x ROAS on $2-3k/month Meta against $70k/month Google.
**The exception, and it is an AND condition:** Meta is the sole traffic driver AND the purchase cycle is long. That covers 5-10% of ecommerce or fewer, and the trade you are making is signal starvation, not accuracy (AT-076).
House rule stands: never call spend "wasted" from a last-click zero. Contested scale: one auditor finds 1-day view running 30-50% of reported ROAS as the norm, which would make the 10% threshold a healthy exception rather than a typical account (AT-037, T3). AT-037 also carries the delivery half: the 7-day-click plus 1-day-view setting biases DELIVERY toward view-through, not only the report, so roughly 90% of accounts overspend on existing customers and the fix is two-sided. The "underreports only 10-20%" figure inherits the sign problem in 9a below (AT-008, **downgraded T2 to T3 on 2026-08-19**: both sources are the same speaker citing Haus and CTC meta-analyses from memory, studies never shown).

9a. **The incrementality benchmarks expired, and the SIGN of the error is account-dependent. This is the 2026-08-19 correction.** The old rule fixed the direction: true incremental sits near 28-day click, roughly 7-day click times 110-115%, i.e. always correct UPWARD. **That is wrong as a per-account rule.** One shown account ran 4.77 on 7-day click against 3.25 incremental, which is 47% OVER-attribution, while the same operator argues a second account UNDER-attributes because its acquisition MER runs above the platform number (AT-074, T2). The sign is a property of the account, set by channel mix and AOV.
**110-115% is a population average across pooled tests. Never apply it to a single account without reconciling against aMER first.** The multiplier was also derived on the OLD wider 7-day-click column, so it does not survive the March 2026 outbound-click-only redefinition either (AT-008 cross-referenced to AT-036). Never quote it against post-March-2026 data as current.
10. **Meta redefined the attribution windows in March 2026, and this is now T1, confirmed off Meta's own screen** (AT-036, **upgraded T3 to T1 on 2026-08-20**; delivery-side half at AU-017). An operator walks the ad-set attribution setting with the old and new versions side by side and reads Meta's product copy aloud. Old option set: click-through, engaged view (video only), view-through. New: the "video only" qualifier is gone, and engaged view now counts **an ad click including a like, share or save, followed by a conversion within 1 day**. He dates it explicitly: "this changed in March." A second operator independently places it around March 2026. So 7-day-click ROAS fell overnight with no change in real performance.
**Tier discipline: the surface and Meta's on-screen definitions are T1. Any operator's interpretation of what to do about it stays T3.** One detail is banked but NOT settled: a 5-second-watched or 97%-of-length threshold was read off the screen, but which window it governs was rendered ambiguously and is marked unverified. Do not repeat the mapping until Meta's documentation is checked.
**Reporting consequence, and it is a client-facing one:** a step-down in 7-day-click ROAS dated to that window is a definitional artefact and must never be written up as a performance decline.
**The instrument to settle our own view-through share is live and we have never opened it (AT-082).** Columns, then Compare attribution settings, splits one conversion total four ways: 1-day click, 7-day click, 1-day view, 1-day engaged view. It has never been opened on ChiroWorks, Chiropraise, Phoenix Truxx, SJR Commercial or Mattia. Opening it is the cheapest way to turn the contested view-through share at AT-037 into our own T2. It settles click versus view, not incrementality.
11. **Never quote Meta's Incremental Attribution to a client as a measured lift. We no longer know what it actually is.** The client-facing rule is unchanged and holds. The MECHANISM behind it flipped to contested on 2026-08-19, because three incompatible explanations are now on file **from the same speaker**: a factor derived from conversion-lift experiments run by OTHER advertisers in your niche (2026), a permanent 10% holdout of your own target demographic (Jun 2025), and the arithmetic midpoint of 1-day and 7-day click (May 2025). **None is documented** (AT-035, contested; the same description also sits at MD-034).
They disagree on the only thing that matters operationally: whether the number is a lift measured on your own account. If the midpoint explanation is right it is a click-window proxy rather than a causal instrument, which would make a hand-rolled proxy just as defensible and weaken the case for switching ad sets onto it. The client-reporting rule survives under two of the three mechanisms, so keep it. It still beats standard attribution on the T2 evidence (AT-001 to AT-004).
11a. **Period-end reports read the most incomplete days as final.** Conversions keep landing after a window closes, so the newest days in any report are still filling in. Every week-over-week and month-over-month comparison therefore structurally flatters the EARLIER window. Cut the same tail off both windows or state the uncertainty (AT-068, T3). At low conversion counts a zero-conversion day is baseline variance, not a finding (AT-031). And never write "Meta broke" or "platform issue" without the spend-spike-at-strange-CPM signature; it is the least likely explanation and the only one with no plan attached (AT-066).

12. **Prune by MONEY LOST, never by low spend as such.** In a shown 89-ad campaign the winning ads and the losing ads consumed the same budget, $48,594 against $48,800, while the losers ran a $230 CPA and a negative $0.98 gross profit per transaction (MD-046, T2). Cutting that campaign from 89 ads to 4 and launching nothing new for six weeks 10x'd spend in 90 days, with weekly gross profit per transaction holding at 136/133/137/124 even as platform ROAS fell below 2 (SC-050, T2).
**Reconciling this with law 3's new low-spender contest, because they look opposed and are not.** SC-050's losers were FUNDED: $48,800 of real spend at negative profit per transaction, so cutting them removed a measured loss. Blue Sense is describing near-zero-spend ads carrying near-zero loss. So: an ad that has spent real money at negative profit per transaction goes, an ad that has spent nothing stays. **The boundary is untested, so state it as a hypothesis.** This still does not license killing top spenders, which law 3 forbids.

Four further Blue Sense Google episodes were read in full on 2026-08-19, two of them on PMax structure, and not one restates it. Signals are described only as directional lookalike seeds. 16a. **A share of Google spend is now unmeasurable.** As of Dec 2025, broad match, shopping feeds, PMax and AI Max can already serve inside AI Overviews and AI Mode with no off switch and no reporting breakout (GA-043, T3). 17. **Raising target ROAS is an audience-temperature dial, not an efficiency dial.** Each increase shrinks the bid pool and cuts cold audiences first, so reported efficiency rises while new-customer acquisition falls (GA-019, T3, now three sources). Missing target then starts a self-reinforcing spiral that requires an aggressive cut to escape, not an incremental one (GA-020, T3). The dial runs both ways: setting tROAS as low as 50% is used deliberately to force top-of-funnel.

17a. **The first measured Google result in the codex, and it says Demand Gen harvested the warm pool.** A 21-day geo holdout on an Australian account, Victoria plus Queensland as the spending geo against the rest of Australia, states chosen by correlating monthly order movement and isolating p-values, 12-month baseline, 90% confidence. **New customers: no statistical relevance.** 2,857 actual against 2,771 expected, a lift of 85 where 166 was needed. New-customer orders actually FELL in both arms, down 4.5% in the control and 1.41% in the test states, so the entire apparent gain is the test states falling less far. **Returning customers: a real 6.8% lift**, 1,521 against 1,211 expected, at $39 per returning-customer order. Implied new-customer iCAC was $140 and unprofitable, and since the result is null that $140 is an arithmetic artefact, never quote it as a measured iCAC (GA-055 / GP-040 / AT-072, T2, **one experiment recorded three times, never weigh it three times**).
**The transferable rule, and it generalises far past Demand Gen: never read a blended lift number.** A blended read on this test would have shown a clean positive and been reported as acquisition when the lift was retargeting. Split new-customer from returning-customer outcomes before the test starts. The right response to a null acquisition result is also on file: the agency kept the campaign at reduced budget and reclassified it as retargeting rather than switching it off or defending it.
**Scope is tight: one advertiser, one market, one campaign type, 21 days.** The source's own baseline figures are internally inconsistent (2,500 and 254 early against 2,771 and 166 later, probably a 95% design scenario against a 90% result read, never stated). The headline survives either way, because a lift of 85 misses both thresholds. It transfers as a method and a prior, not as a verdict on PMax.

17b. **Meta's own geo lift product is gated near $100k/month spend** (AT-070). For almost every account we run, the quasi geo test is the only incrementality instrument actually available, so learn to size it backwards from the lift you would need to detect (AT-071, T2). A location doing five orders a week has no volume floor for any geo test at all.

**Economics**

20. **Gross margin sets the ROAS breakeven** (87 points of margin is profitable at 1.3x; 50-60 points needs ~2.0x); fix margin before blaming ads (MM-010, T3). A live account now sits behind it: 56.77 points of gross margin at $58.50 blended CAC leaves 12.61 points of contribution, and contribution margin percent is the ceiling on net profit when returning customers are under 15% of revenue, so 12.61% minus 10% opex leaves 2% maximum net.
**LTGP:CAC of 2-3 is the scale zone, and on 2026-08-20 the contested numerator got an answer better than a winner: operators genuinely differ, so the ratio is not portable** (MM-002). Three of them state their numerator out loud. **Theriot uses REVENUE, pre-COGS, twice, on screen**, so anyone quoting his LTV:CAC figures as gross-profit-based is misreading him. **Faris uses GROSS PROFIT**, applying 60 points of margin first and keeping raw-revenue LTV as a separate column. **Blue Sense uses GROSS PROFIT at cart level.**
**The finding IS the operating rule: state which numerator a ratio uses before acting on it or comparing it to anyone else's.** A 2.5 on revenue and a 2.5 on gross profit are different businesses, and at 50 points of margin they are a factor of two apart. Do not average them and do not assume.
21. **Discounting raises required ROAS exponentially while breakeven rises only linearly**, so the gap between a 15% and a 25% discount can be the gap between an achievable target and an impossible one; model it before publishing the offer (MM-085 to MM-092, T4 worked models).
