# Attribution & Incrementality

What platform-reported numbers mean, where they lie, holdouts and geo tests, MER, blended measurement.

Part of the [[00-Codex|Advertising Science Codex]]. Claims follow the tier system (T1 docs, T2 shown test, T3 practitioner, T4 theory).

## Claims

## Incremental Attribution: The Switch Decision

### AT-001 · Meta incremental attribution flipped from losing to standard attribution (0.8x, 2024-Jul 2025) to beating it (1.26x, Jul 2025-Jul 2026) in Haus geo-holdout tests
Tier: T2 · Status: active
Haus measured IA-vs-standard head-to-head cells with geo-holdout studies, ignoring in-platform metrics entirely. The geometric-mean ratio of incremental ROAS (IA cell over standard cell) was 0.8x in the 2024-July 2025 sample and 1.26x in the July 2025-July 2026 sample. The 2025 sample was about a dozen tests and Haus caveated "not there yet"; the 2026 refresh reversed the verdict. Test mix skewed US: 90% US, 7% UK, 3% Canada.
Sources: Andrew Faris, DTC Brands See 38% Better Meta Ads Performance When They Switch To Incremental Attribution - Olivia, 2026-08-10
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-002 · DTC-only brands see up to 38% better incremental performance from switching to incremental attribution; omnichannel brands see much smaller gains
Tier: T2 · Status: active
In the Haus data, DTC-only brands (no Amazon or retail KPI) showed the biggest IA-over-standard gains, up to 38%, while brands with an omnichannel secondary KPI gained far less. Proposed mechanism is signal completeness: when Meta sees every purchase (all on the dotcom), the lift-trained model optimizes better than when it is blind to Amazon/retail conversions. Faris notes the mechanism is not fully settled since incomplete signal also afflicts standard attribution.
Sources: Andrew Faris, DTC Brands See 38% Better Meta Ads Performance When They Switch To Incremental Attribution - Olivia, 2026-08-10
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-003 · For DTC-only brands the downside of switching to incremental attribution is near zero: worst IA test results were roughly at par with standard
Tier: T2 · Status: active
In the Haus test distribution, the bottom of the IA results for DTC-only brands was basically even with standard attribution while the top end ran roughly 38-40% better, so risk is asymmetric in IA's favor. Faris's rollout rule: under roughly $5k/day spend, just switch (no 50-50 test, you can always switch back); under roughly $10k/day, move your highest-volume-optimized spend straight over to IA and watch. Olivia's counterpoint: the sample is still small, so a larger account should test rather than blanket-switch.
Sources: Andrew Faris, DTC Brands See 38% Better Meta Ads Performance When They Switch To Incremental Attribution - Olivia, 2026-08-10
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-004 · Faris moved 100% of clients to manual bids with incremental attribution and zero accounts got worse
Tier: T3 · Status: active
After retesting IA in mid-2026, AJF Growth runs all clients on manual bids with incremental attribution; the bulk of spend is cost cap plus IA. Some clients saw no big change but none regressed. The standout effect was total spend volume increasing at the same targets (more ads getting spent) rather than dramatically different ad selection. He has not observed the "good at first, decays over time" pattern some report on X, and speculates that pattern is exposed creative-volume weakness or misread seasonality.
Sources: Andrew Faris, DTC Brands See 38% Better Meta Ads Performance When They Switch To Incremental Attribution - Olivia, 2026-08-10
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-005 · Incremental attribution works worse for value optimization (tROAS) than for volume optimization, likely because far less value-optimized conversion-lift data exists to train on
Tier: T3 · Status: active
When AJF moved highest-value (tROAS) spend to IA the impact was much smaller and sometimes slightly negative, versus clear gains on volume-optimized spend. Mechanism: historically much less total value-optimized spend and fewer value-optimized lift studies feed the IA model, so it is slower to get good there. Same logic predicts weaker international results; the Haus dataset was 90% US so international IA is essentially untested.
Sources: Andrew Faris, DTC Brands See 38% Better Meta Ads Performance When They Switch To Incremental Attribution - Olivia, 2026-08-10
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-006 · Incremental attribution (announced Aug 2024) uses models to predict whether the ad caused the conversion, and now works for both reporting and delivery optimization
Tier: T3 · Status: active
Unlike standard attribution, incremental attribution does not rely on click/view/engage windows; it models causation. Over roughly the year before mid-2026 it evolved from reporting-only into an optimization option, so it can change which conversions delivery optimizes toward. When optimizing under standard attribution Meta can load up on view-through conversions if that raises reported results; incremental optimization removes that incentive.
Sources: Jon Loomer, Most Advertisers Should Only Use Incremental Attribution, 2026-07-29
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-007 · Incremental attribution results converge on click-based attribution: they roughly match the click-through portion of standard results, and decomposed IA sums to about 7-day click plus 1-day engaged view
Tier: T3 · Status: active
Two independent sources land on the same convergence. Loomer finds incremental-attributed conversion counts line up more or less with click-through conversions under standard attribution; accounts propped up by view/engage-through see a large surface drop that is reporting, not performance, and he believes most advertisers who abandoned IA did so because of that perceived drop. Taylor Holiday, from CTC's in-flight CLS studies, observes that decomposing IA's credited conversions and summing 7-day click, 1-day engaged view, and 1-day view converges on roughly what 7-day click plus 1-day engaged view reported, effectively the old 7-day click standard, though the comparison is inexact because Meta added a 5-second no-click engaged-view definition. Implication: IA may be re-deriving the attribution the industry pressed Meta into, now grounded in lift data.
Sources: Jon Loomer, Most Advertisers Should Only Use Incremental Attribution, 2026-07-29; Andrew Faris, Do Cost Caps Work? Taylor Holiday Brings $200M Of Data, 2026-08-06
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-035 · Meta's incremental attribution applies a factor derived from conversion-lift experiments run by other advertisers in your niche, so it is directional evidence about your industry rather than a measured lift for your account
Tier: T3 · Status: active
Advertisers assume the setting holds out 10% of their own users in real time and reads the difference. It does not. Meta takes conversion-lift experiments run by competitors in the niche, reads how incremental those campaign types were, and applies a factor into your account. [[Meta Delivery & Andromeda#MD-034|MD-034]] banks the model-trained-on-lift-studies description; the consequence that matters when reading the number is this: an incremental attribution figure is evidence about your industry, and it is not a measured lift for your account. Never quote it to a client as a verified lift figure, and never present it as the output of a holdout you ran. The underlying arithmetic he gives for what the factor represents: a 2% purchase rate in the exposed cell against 1% in the holdout means a reported 5 ROAS is really 2.5. Read it via Columns, Compare attribution setting, Incremental attribution. Caveat on the source: the same speaker in another video describes incremental attribution as a live 10% user holdout with a difference-in-differences calculation, which his own quote here contradicts. Take the competitor-lift-factor version as the considered one and treat the holdout description as loose shorthand. Asserted from audit practice, no Meta documentation cited and no test shown.
Sources: Blue Sense Digital, How to Scale an eCommerce Brand Profitably in 2026 The Full System, 2026-06-15; Blue Sense Digital, Meta Ads Creative Strategy in 2026 The Full System, 2026-05-11
Last touched: 2026-08-18

## How Much Platform-Reported Numbers Lie

### AT-008 · Meta 7-day-click optimized campaigns underreport true contribution by 10-20%; 28-day click attribution is approximately correct
Tier: T2 · Status: active
Meta-analyses of Meta lift tests compiled by Haus and CTC show 7-day-click campaigns underreport their true incremental contribution by roughly 10-20%, which means 28-day click in-platform reporting lands about right. This is Faris's core reason for never using Northbeam/Triple Whale for any client: Meta's own platform reporting tied to click (and now IA) is reliable enough, and that conclusion is itself a regression across all published tests rather than one account's result.
Sources: Andrew Faris, DTC Brands See 38% Better Meta Ads Performance When They Switch To Incremental Attribution - Olivia, 2026-08-10
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-009 · Platform ROAS can sit flat at 20 while the business declines: stripping view-through dropped one large retailer from 20 to 7, and incremental attribution took it to ~1
Tier: T3 · Status: active
Default platform attribution over-credits so heavily that the reported ROAS number stays constant while true acquisition efficiency collapses, hiding a CAC blow-up for months. Example given for a well-known large Australian retailer: reported 20x to 7x without view-through conversions, then roughly 1x on incremental attribution. Rule: a 20 ROAS in Facebook "is not real". Professor Charley T's practitioner-side version of the same point: ROAS is trivially gameable to 100x by restricting delivery to email openers, which will not scale and will not add revenue, so a high ROAS is largely a measure of audience warmth. Actionable form: when an account reports an unusually high ROAS, check what share of delivery went to warm and owned audiences before treating it as a win. [[Attribution & Incrementality#AT-038|AT-038]] turns the same audit ladder into a positive test by reconciling each attribution setting against acquisition MER.
Sources: Blue Sense Digital, The Death Spiral That Kills Most eCommerce Brands, 2026-07-22; Professor Charley T, The BEST AD ON META after Andromeda, 2026-01-10
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-010 · Meta's default attribution credits any same-day ad interaction followed by a conversion, so platform-reported results overstate causal impact
Tier: T3 · Status: active
Meta takes credit if a user interacted with an ad and later converted, even when an email or word-of-mouth actually drove the sale, as long as the ad was served that same day. The right question is incremental: what results did you get that you would not have gotten without the ads. See AT-021 for when incrementality measurement is worth buying.
Sources: Ben Heath, $100 vs $100,000 Facebook Ads Strategy, 2026-08-12
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-011 · View-through attribution over-credits ads that were merely scrolled past, misroutes budget, and its conversions are largely non-incremental; turn it off in nearly all campaigns
Tier: T3 · Status: active
Multiple independent sources converge on this. Shiver's mechanism: a prospect already in your DMs scrolls past your static ad for a second, books via the DM link the same day, and the ad takes credit; because the ad with the most attributed conversions gets more budget, view-through inflation misroutes spend to ads that did not cause conversions. His setting: keep 7-day click as primary, 1-day engaged-view is acceptable, disable 1-day view in roughly 99% of campaigns once you get 1-3+ conversions/day, unless pixel fires are so scarce nothing hits the results column. Loomer's independent incrementality argument: an impression served within 1 day of a conversion earns credit even if the person never consciously saw the ad, isolated remarketing audiences make the inflation worse, and a high view/engage-through share is a strong argument the ad did not cause those conversions. Blue Sense Digital adds the specific channel of distortion and a weaker fallback position: view-through over-attributes on existing-customer revenue, so ad sets and creatives that happen to touch the existing base read artificially well and pull budget toward themselves, and if you will not remove 1-day view from the campaign setting, at minimum exclude it from ad-set budget decisions. He runs it as one data-integrity package with 7-day-click reporting at every spend tier and existing customers excluded from cold campaigns. Magnitude of the inflation in [[Attribution & Incrementality#AT-037|AT-037]].
Sources: Dr. Matt Shiver, How to Run Facebook Ads for Coaches & Agency Owners (FREE COURSE), 2026-08-04; Dr. Matt Shiver, How the Facebook Ads Algorithm Actually Works (And How to Beat It), 2026-07-21; Jon Loomer, Most Advertisers Should Only Use Incremental Attribution, 2026-07-29; Blue Sense Digital, How To Structure A Meta Ads Account At Every Spend Tier In 2026, 2026-06-01
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-012 · Audit attribution mix with the "compare attribution settings" columns: view-through at ~10% or less of conversions is tolerable; if it dominates, duplicate the ad set without view attribution
Tier: T2 · Status: active
Shown client example: 427 message conversions, 390 from 7-day click, 37 (~10%) from view-through, which he leaves running. If instead a large share (his hypothetical: 302 of 427) were view-through, the ads are taking credit for conversions that were not theirs and the ad set should be rebuilt with view-through off. Second shown case, from Blue Sense Digital's Ads Manager screenshots on an audited ecommerce account: switching the reporting column to 7-day click alone dropped ROAS from 2.14 to 1.68 (a 21% cut) and attributed revenue from about $79k to about $62k in one month, roughly $17k of over-attribution, converging toward the ROI the P&L showed. He states roughly 95% of audited accounts never touch the column. His mechanism for why view-through concentrates on returning customers: a view-through can only fire for someone who already hit the site and triggered the pixel, so those conversions cluster on people already deep in the purchase journey. Tension to hold rather than resolve: [[Attribution & Incrementality#AT-037|AT-037]] puts the typical view-through share at 30-50% of reported ROAS across audits, so the ~10% tolerance here describes a healthy exception rather than the norm. Treat 10% as the level you want to find, not the level you should expect.
Sources: Dr. Matt Shiver, How the Facebook Ads Algorithm Actually Works (And How to Beat It), 2026-07-21; Blue Sense Digital, How To Structure Your Meta Ads for Profit Free Live Webinar, 2025-02-19
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-013 · Meta does not invent conversions; over-reporting (including outright double-counting) comes from advertiser-side event setup, so verify platform counts against backend actuals
Tier: T2 · Status: active
Two independent sources. Loomer's mechanism: the results column counts attributed event fires, so when reported conversions exceed reality the causes are advertiser-side, most commonly failed deduplication across multiple event sources, or events firing prematurely at the wrong funnel stage; Events Manager shows raw pre-deduplication fires, so multi-source setups can show roughly double the expected count there without anything being wrong. Nick Theriot showed the failure live: an ad set reported 594 purchases on June 16 while actual backend purchases were just under 300 (~292), and he corrects the platform number to backend reality on the fly.
Sources: Jon Loomer, Meta Doesn't Invent Conversions That Never Happened, 2026-08-05; Nick Theriot, $30k days with Facebook Ads using Emotional Intensity, 2026-07-22
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-014 · Repeat purchases by the same person within the attribution window count as separate conversions, inflating conversions above unique customers
Tier: T3 · Status: active
Example: someone clicks, purchases, then returns 3 days later and purchases again; under 7-day click both purchases count as two conversions. This is why reported conversions can exceed total new customers. The breakdown by conversion count separates first conversions from all subsequent ones.
Sources: Jon Loomer, Meta Doesn't Invent Conversions That Never Happened, 2026-08-05
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-015 · The results column counts ANY qualifying standard event within the attribution window, not purchases of the advertised product specifically
Tier: T3 · Status: active
Meta credits an ad with a purchase conversion whenever the person who saw or clicked the ad fires a purchase event within the window, regardless of which product was bought. An advertiser comparing ad-attributed purchases against sales of the featured SKU will see a mismatch that is definitional, not an error.
Sources: Jon Loomer, Meta Doesn't Invent Conversions That Never Happened, 2026-08-05
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-016 · Default attribution counts view-through and engage-through conversions that carry no click, so UTM and third-party referral reports structurally undercount Meta-attributed conversions
Tier: T3 · Status: active
View-through and engage-through conversions require no link click, so they never appear in UTM-based or referral analytics; more often than not these are remarketing-assisted conversions where the person saw the ad and converted through another channel within a day. Click conversions also leak from third-party reports via delayed conversion (days later) and cross-device conversion. Use the breakdown by attribution setting to see the mix; the higher the 1-day-click share, the stronger the result.
Sources: Jon Loomer, Meta Doesn't Invent Conversions That Never Happened, 2026-08-05
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-017 · Paid ads drive unattributed conversions through owned content: six figures of attributed revenue landed on YouTube videos with under 500 views, from buyers who saw an ad, searched the creator, and converted off-track
Tier: T3 · Status: active
Buyers rarely follow the tracked path: they see an ad, look the person up, watch YouTube content, then book through whatever link is in front of them. Colin Ship reports well into six figures of attributed revenue on sub-500-view videos. Shiver adds the mirror observation from Hyros: as his Meta spend rises, booked calls attributed to YouTube and other platforms rise too, so killing ads on last-touch data alone understates their contribution.
Sources: Dr. Matt Shiver, How He Made $15M From YouTube (Full Breakdown), 2026-07-23
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-036 · Meta redefined 7-day click around March-April 2026 to require an outbound click, moving like, comment, share and page clicks into a new 1-day engaged view bucket, so 7-day-click ROAS stepped down overnight with no change in performance
Tier: T3 · Status: active
Before the change, any click on the ad unit counted inside 7-day click, including the like button, a comment, a share, or a click through to the Page, which almost nobody knew. Meta rewrote the definition to outbound clicks only and re-housed the engagement clicks under 1-day engaged view. Two operator consequences. First, reporting: a step-down in 7-day-click ROAS around March or April 2026 is a definitional artefact and must never be reported to a client as a performance decline, and any year-over-year or before-and-after comparison of that column spanning roughly March 2026 is broken. Second, setting: 7-day click alone now counts less than it used to, which is why Blue Sense pairs it with 1-day engaged view rather than reading 7-day click bare. Engaged view is retained because engaging with an ad and then buying implies a plausible causal link, and he finds that combined window tracks acquisition MER (see [[Attribution & Incrementality#AT-038|AT-038]]). Read via Columns, Compare attribution settings. [[Auction Mechanics & Bidding#AU-017|AU-017]] banks the delivery-side damage the same change did to click-optimized manual-bid accounts; this entry is the reporting half. Asserted from audit practice in two separate videos, with no changelog, screenshot or Meta announcement shown.
Sources: Blue Sense Digital, How to Scale an eCommerce Brand Profitably in 2026 The Full System, 2026-06-15; Blue Sense Digital, The 1 Bottleneck I See in 80 of eCommerce Audits, 2026-05-18
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-037 · In audited accounts, 30-50% of reported ROAS comes from 1-day view alone
Tier: T3 · Status: active
Named as the number-one finding across a stated 1,000+ audits of 7 to 9-figure brands: 1-day view left on across all campaigns and used in reporting. Strip it and 30-50% of the ROAS number disappears. The mechanism is warm-audience capture: existing and warm customers who were going to buy anyway scroll past an ad and get credited. He is careful that 7-day click and 1-day engaged view are both defensible, and it is the view window specifically that manufactures the inflation. Operator use: this is the "how much will I lose" number to set expectations with before changing the column, and it belongs in any client conversation that precedes the change.
Tension to record rather than resolve. [[Attribution & Incrementality#AT-012|AT-012]] sets a view-through tolerance of roughly 10% of conversions and treats anything larger as a rebuild trigger. If 30-50% is typical, the 10% account is a healthy exception and the 10% line describes the level you want to find rather than the level you should expect. Note also that the two banked cases with actual figures land on either side of the band: AT-009's retailer went 20x to 7x on stripping view-through, well inside it, while the one case with screenshots (2.14 to 1.68, a 21% cut) sits below it. The 30-50% band is recited from audits and was not shown.
Sources: Blue Sense Digital, How to Scale an eCommerce Brand Profitably in 2026 The Full System, 2026-06-15; Blue Sense Digital, The 1 Bottleneck I See in 80 of eCommerce Audits, 2026-05-18
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-038 · Choose the Meta attribution setting by which one reconciles to acquisition MER: in two audits incremental attribution converged on the business number while default attribution overstated by 3-4x
Tier: T3 · Status: active
Acquisition MER is new-customer revenue divided by total ad spend, a store-level figure the P&L produces. The test is congruency. Run Columns, Compare attribution settings, read the ladder, and pick the setting that matches the aMER the business actually earned. Audit one, a 9-figure fashion brand: 36x on default attribution, 17x on 7-day click, 8.22x on incremental attribution, against a business aMER of 8x. Audit two: 13.6x default, 8.6x 7-day click, 5.16x incremental, against an aMER of 4.4x. In both, incremental attribution converged on financial reality and default attribution was off by 3-4x. Fashion and CPG over-attribute worst because returning-customer revenue runs 30-75% of total. This is the practical form of the convergence in [[Attribution & Incrementality#AT-007|AT-007]] and it gives an operator a self-serve substitute for buying incrementality tooling. It does not make the incremental attribution number a measured lift for the account ([[Attribution & Incrementality#AT-035|AT-035]]). The two audit ladders were recited verbally, nothing shown on screen. One corroborating shown data point from the same practitioner's earlier webinar: an onboarded client's January platform ROAS of 2.14 against an acquisition MER of 1.41, with $79k attributed revenue against $56k of first-time-customer net revenue.
Sources: Blue Sense Digital, Why Most Fashion Brands Are Running Paid Media Wrong, 2026-05-25; Blue Sense Digital, How To Structure Your Meta Ads for Profit Free Live Webinar, 2025-02-19
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-039 · ROAS reliability rises as you move up the account structure: ad-level ROAS is corrupted by last-click sequencing, ad-set level is usable, and campaign-level 7-day-click ROAS with existing customers excluded reconciles to the P&L
Tier: T3 · Status: active
Meta optimizes across multi-click and view chains and reports credit on the final click. A three-ad sequence where the click lands on the third ad can read 6x, 2x and 1.4x at equal spend, which invites you to kill the two ads doing the top-of-funnel work. Cross-serving between ad sets also happens, because the algorithm prioritizes ads inside the same ad set (targeting is siloed there) without confining itself to them, so one ad set can look better purely by harvesting conversions another generated. Two further contaminants sit at ad level: repeat purchases inside the window ([[Attribution & Incrementality#AT-014|AT-014]]), and in fashion, cross-purchasing, where the click comes from a t-shirt ad and the purchase is a dress, which corrupts product-level ROAS even when channel-level attribution is sound. Operator rules: make kill and scale decisions at ad-set and campaign level, read the ad level for diagnostics only, and validate per-product spend against real sell-through per SKU rather than platform product ROAS. Record the tension: this pushes directly against the ad-level pruning doctrine used elsewhere in the codex, which grades individual ads on CPA and profit per transaction and cuts the losers. Both positions are practitioner assertions; neither showed a test of whether ad-level kill decisions actually cost money. Asserted, no data shown.
Sources: Blue Sense Digital, Meta Ads Creative Strategy in 2026 The Full System, 2026-05-11; Blue Sense Digital, How to Scale an eCommerce Brand Profitably in 2026 The Full System, 2026-06-15; Blue Sense Digital, Why Most Fashion Brands Are Running Paid Media Wrong, 2026-05-25
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-040 · Dynamic product ads read 5-10x on default attribution and land near 2x once corrected, and 7-day click and incremental attribution independently produce the same corrected number
Tier: T3 · Status: active
DPAs place almost exclusively immediately before purchase against most-aware retargeting audiences, so they harvest credit for conversions already in motion. Three audit reads. A $20-30M furniture brand had one DPA ad set holding 57% of total Meta budget at a surface 7x; broken out by 7-day click it read 1.9x, and by incremental attribution it also read 1.9x, below every other ad set in the account. A DPA showing 5x generally needs a ~50% haircut to 2.5x. Across fashion audits the surface number runs 7-10x and incremental attribution puts it near 2x. The cross-check is the useful part: two independent correction methods landing on the same figure gives operators without incremental attribution a usable substitute in 7-day click. Operator actions: never consolidate a DPA ad set into the same CBO as prospecting ads, because the ad type with the most inflated attribution wins the budget allocation; cap DPA budget hard at the $75-200k/month tier; treat an ad library dominated by DPAs (Zara at 200 live DPAs is his example) as a red flag. Numbers recited from audits, nothing shown.
Sources: Blue Sense Digital, How To Structure A Meta Ads Account At Every Spend Tier In 2026, 2026-06-01; Blue Sense Digital, Meta Ads Creative Strategy in 2026 The Full System, 2026-05-11; Blue Sense Digital, Why Most Fashion Brands Are Running Paid Media Wrong, 2026-05-25
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-041 · DPA ROAS is a derivative of top-of-funnel spend, which drives a budget-migration death spiral: the inflated number pulls budget down-funnel, starves the traffic the DPAs convert, and the account declines
Tier: T3 · Status: active
The causal direction is the load-bearing part. Spend more up-funnel and DPA ROAS rises, because there is more revenue at the bottom to capture. Cut up-funnel and it falls. The loop that follows: high reported DPA ROAS, agency raises DPA budget, brand concludes creative production and top-of-funnel spend matter less, top of funnel starves, the bottom of the funnel runs out of people to convert, the account declines. He describes seeing it six times in about two weeks across fashion brands, all spending roughly 80% of budget on DPAs, all in six-month declines. This supplies the mechanism behind the attribution correction in [[Attribution & Incrementality#AT-040|AT-040]]. He also states he has never seen a DPA scale on cold audiences: $1,000/day is achievable, $50,000/day is not. Audit pattern asserted, no account data shown, and the six brands are a selected sample of accounts that came in for audit because they were declining.
Sources: Blue Sense Digital, How To Structure A Meta Ads Account At Every Spend Tier In 2026, 2026-06-01; Blue Sense Digital, Why Most Fashion Brands Are Running Paid Media Wrong, 2026-05-25
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-042 · ROAS is only comparable inside one platform, and the bias is directional: whichever channel lands the last click harvests the credit created by the channel upstream of it
Tier: T3 · Status: active
Four convergent statements of one mechanism. Raising TikTok spend lifts Meta and Google ROAS while TikTok's own stays flat, because TikTok generates impressions with few off-platform clicks, Meta retargets the pixel pool, and the residual searches the brand on Google; a C-suite reading channel ROAS then cuts TikTok, trims Meta and piles budget into Google, which is a decision to defund the top of the funnel. The worked version: launch TikTok at 25% of Meta budget, TikTok reads 1-2x and looks unscalable while Meta's mid-funnel campaign jumps from 4x to 12x, and the operator raises Meta budget when the causal driver was the TikTok spend. Google specifically sits at the bottom of the funnel and claims a high ROAS on demand created by Meta, TikTok and affiliates, which produces two opposite failure modes he sees in onboarding audits: overspending into non-incremental Google, and the over-corrected camp that caps Google under $10k/mo while spending $250k/mo on Meta despite real cold search demand. The right Google question is whether back-end revenue moves when Google spend moves. The asymmetry generalizes: the platform you overspend on gets under-attributed and the platform you underspend on gets over-attributed, and the same holds inside one platform, where a cold campaign with heavy exclusions is systematically under-credited against an Advantage+ campaign with none.
Attribution windows also differ structurally, which alone breaks the comparison: Google defaults to 30-day click plus 7-day view plus 1-day view, Meta to 7-day click plus 1-day view, Pinterest counts view-throughs. Operator rules: compare two campaigns' ROAS only when their attribution windows and exclusion setups match, and settle cross-platform allocation with lift ([[Attribution & Incrementality#AT-050|AT-050]]) rather than with reported ROAS columns. Asserted from client accounts across four videos, no numbers shown, and the TikTok-launch figures are an illustrative scenario.
Sources: Blue Sense Digital, Everything You Need to Know About Finance in eCommerce, 2026-05-04; Blue Sense Digital, The 1 Bottleneck I See in 80 of eCommerce Audits, 2026-05-18; Blue Sense Digital, The Only Google Ads Strategy You Need for 2026, 2026-06-22; Blue Sense Digital, How To Structure Your Meta Ads for Profit Free Live Webinar, 2025-02-19
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-043 · Low-spend tertiary channels structurally over-report, because a small budget makes the algorithm default to the warm pixel pool
Tier: T3 · Status: active
Give any platform a small budget and it spends it on the cheapest-converting people available, which is your existing site visitors and customers. The result is a channel showing 7x while contributing nothing new, and it survives in the media plan precisely because its ROAS looks best. Named: Pinterest, Snapchat, Reddit. Pinterest carries an additional structural inflation. The board renders roughly six to twelve tiles at once, so any ad on screen can claim a view-through inside a 24-hour window, and its click-through ROAS reads far worse than the headline number. In the Australian market Pinterest also caps around $10,000/month of spend, so the build and optimization time costs more than the return versus putting the same money into Meta. Operator rules: never judge a small-budget test channel on its own reported ROAS; either fund it to a level where it has to go cold, or measure it with a geo test ([[Attribution & Incrementality#AT-050|AT-050]]); otherwise consolidate. ### AT-023 · Contested: whether to log and reuse a static incrementality discount factor per campaign type

### AT-050 · Geo-lift design: cluster regions on three historical series (daily ad spend, new-customer revenue, new-customer orders) and use combinations of states rather than single-state pairs

The likely reconciliation is the vertical boundary the speaker draws himself, which would bound any new-customers-only rule to non-fashion categories and to categories without a fast product-drop cadence.

### AT-056 · Traffic and reach campaigns are unfalsifiable at the budget share they are usually run at, so they need an experimental design attached or removal
Tier: T3 · Status: active
Across a stated 1,000+ audits he has never spoken to an operator running traffic campaigns who could name how they measure them. The justification he hears is always correlational: "we added them a year ago and revenue went up", or "we ran 3% of budget on traffic during Black Friday and Black Friday was good". At 3% of budget the activity is simultaneously too small to move the business and too small to detect in a before-and-after causal read, so it is unfalsifiable spend by construction. He does not say never run them. He says pair them with a design, geo lift ([[Attribution & Incrementality#AT-050|AT-050]]) or CausalImpact ([[Attribution & Incrementality#AT-053|AT-053]]), or delete them. Asserted from audit practice, no data shown.

## Testing and Decision Hygiene

### AT-027 · Trusting a 10-20% conversion-rate lift needs ~1,000 conversions per variant (~40,000 sessions per variant at 2.5% CR); most CRO "wins" are noise
Tier: T3 · Status: active
An 80k-session A/B test is beyond most 7-figure brands' monthly PDP traffic, so a green result after 2 weeks at ~100 conversions is noise. Daily peeking with early stopping substantially inflates false positives. Klaviyo campaign sends spike CR and contaminate forked-traffic tests unless email traffic is excluded, so real tests should run on custom landers fed only cold Meta traffic. Zoomed out 2 years, most "always-optimizing" accounts show flat revenue per session; real progressive CRO programs only make sense at 8-9 figures with sufficient volume.
Sources: Blue Sense Digital, eCommerce CRO Masterclass 2026: The Full System, 2026-08-10

### AT-028 · Meta buyers average ~1.5 clicks before converting, so ~50% of conversions involve more than one click, contaminating single-click LP attribution and tests
Tier: T3 · Status: active
Measured "pretty closely" across large client brands: average clicks-to-conversion on Meta is about 1.5. A user can click variant A on the first ad and variant B on the second, so landing-page split tests and last-click LP judgments mis-assign a large share of conversions in categories with extended time to purchase.

### AT-029 · Launching new ads/ad sets triggers Facebook compliance bots that crawl the site as deep as checkout, inflating Shopify sessions and deflating measured conversion rate
Tier: T3 · Status: active
On every launch day (worse when using UTMs), Facebook sends bot traffic that scans pages for compliance and clicks through the site including checkout, showing up as session spikes. Because the case-study store launched ads daily, its reported 1.54% conversion rate understated reality; the speaker calculates true CVR at roughly 2-2.5%. Implication: daily launchers should correct CVR for bot sessions before judging a lander.

### AT-030 · Put the submit-application pixel only on the qualified schedule page, and rebook manually recovered leads through a pixel-free calendar link to prevent double-fired events
Tier: T3 · Status: active
In his VSL application funnel, qualified applicants hit a schedule page carrying the submit-application pixel; disqualified applicants get a plain thank-you message with no pixel, so bad leads never train the event. When a lead who already fired the pixel is manually rebooked, they get a separate scheduling link with no pixel, because Meta's deduplication does not reliably catch a second fire and a duplicate event pollutes the signal.

### AT-031 · Judge a new ad only on ROAS or cost per conversion after a few days; one high-cost day is noise, but a cost that stays high and climbs over a full week is a real signal
Tier: T3 · Status: active
Meta needs days of real activity to figure out who responds; judging after hours or a single day "tells you almost nothing reliably". The separation rule: 1 day of high cost is a normal fluctuation, a week-plus of high and climbing cost is actionable. All other dashboard metrics should be ignored by beginners because they do not indicate profitability; this is what prevents switching off ads too early. Professor Charley T states the same thing as a hard floor: seven days is the smallest data window that has any value for an optimization decision, and day-to-day data is never actionable. He gives a concrete stability test with it, accepting roughly 34% day-to-day fluctuation as normal while expecting the weekly number to move only a few percent. Heath's variant in the same session ties cadence to conversion volume rather than to a fixed window: an account at thousands of conversions a day can be read in three hours, an account at six conversions a week may need a month to judge a new ad, and a statistical-significance calculator is the tiebreaker.
Sources: Ben Heath, What You NEED To Know To Get Started With Facebook Ads, 2026-08-06; Professor Charley T, How to CRUSH Facebook Ads with a Low Budget, 2026-02-28
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-032 · At $100/month with high-ticket customers, a profitability verdict can take ~3 months; secondary metrics (CTR, hook rate) are only diagnostic after a conversion-data verdict exists
Tier: T3 · Status: active
Example: $100/month spend against $10,000 customers means you could spend $2,000 to land one customer and still be profitable, so months of patience are needed. Decisions should be driven by cost per conversion or ROAS first; only dig into click-through and hook rates to explain why something did or did not work, never ahead of conversion data. At $100k/month, decisions become near-real-time because data volume is sufficient.

### AT-033 · Diagnostic fork for spend with bad CPA: bad cost-per-link-click means creative problem, good cost-per-link-click means landing page problem
Tier: T2 · Status: active
If an ad gets spend but bad cost per acquisition: check cost per link click. Bad CPLC (often with high CPM) equals a creative issue; good CPLC with high CPA equals a landing page issue. Shown case: a client with high CPP, high CPM and bad CPLC first got a creative swap (AI UGC to native ads) which fixed CPLC but left CPA high; relaunching the same creative with a new landing page dropped cost per purchase to $30.

### AT-034 · Proposed boundary on incremental attribution: skip it for beginners and for advertisers without an existing customer base, because the payoff is filtering conversions warm buyers would have made anyway
Tier: T3 · Status: active
Heath's positioning of the setting (ad set level, Attribution model, switch standard to incremental): its value is that a large share of budget on an account with a real customer base ends up spent on warm people who buy regularly regardless, and incremental attribution both reveals that and retrains delivery toward genuinely incremental conversions. His stated exclusions are beginner advertisers and advertisers without much of a customer base, on the grounds that there is little non-incremental conversion to filter and the reporting drop is discouraging without the upside. He also states the mechanism behind the pessimism: incremental attribution is fairly accurate inside the window but the window is 7-day click, 1-day view and 1-day engaged view by default, so considered purchases that close later are not captured at all, and results genuinely understate contribution rather than merely looking worse. Warning attached: expect worse-looking ROAS and cost per purchase, and expect the emotional reaction to that to be the real reason advertisers abandon the setting.
This boundary is a T3 assertion and it pushes against the T2 evidence already on file. [[Attribution & Incrementality#AT-003|AT-003]] finds the downside near zero for DTC-only brands (worst results roughly at par with standard), and [[Attribution & Incrementality#AT-004|AT-004]] reports Faris moving 100% of clients to incremental attribution with zero accounts getting worse. Neither of those tested small or new advertisers specifically, so the honest reading is that Heath's exclusion is an untested caution rather than a measured limit. Take the tiered evidence over the caution unless the account is genuinely new. The pessimism mechanism itself is consistent with [[Attribution & Incrementality#AT-007|AT-007]].
Sources: Ben Heath, Learn 97% of Meta Ads in Under 29 Minutes, 2026-08-18
Last touched: 2026-08-18

### AT-057 · Landing page views divided by link clicks measures click leakage: at 62%, nearly 40% of paid clicks never load the page and a $4.50 CPC is really an $8-9 effective CPC
Tier: T2 · Status: active
SHOWN on an audited account in Ads Manager. Build it as a custom metric and leave it on permanently. It separates a click-cost problem from a site-speed or redirect problem before any creative or landing-page verdict gets made, which inserts a third failure mode upstream of [[Attribution & Incrementality#AT-033|AT-033]]'s creative-versus-landing-page fork: clicks that never arrive at all. His profitability read on the same account: an $8-9 effective CPC is workable for a $40k-LTV high-ticket service and structurally impossible for a $100-AOV ecommerce brand. Caveat from [[Attribution & Incrementality#AT-047|AT-047]]: a 2026 iOS update is reported to have corrupted landing page view and content view events, so validate the landing page view count against server-side or analytics sessions before trusting the ratio.
