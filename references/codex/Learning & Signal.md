# Learning & Signal

Learning phase, optimization events, signal quality, EMQ, and how feeding the system better signal changes delivery.

Part of the [[00-Codex|Advertising Science Codex]]. Claims follow the tier system (T1 docs, T2 shown test, T3 practitioner, T4 theory).

## Claims

## Learning phase mechanics

### LS-001 · Ad sets exit the learning phase after about 50 results in the week following the last significant edit
Tier: T1 · Status: contested
Meta states the delivery system never stops learning, but ad sets exit the learning phase as soon as they can deliver stably, usually after about 50 results in the week after the ad set's last significant edit. The Delivery column reads "Learning" during this period, and Ads Manager exposes a "Last significant edit" column plus a Results column so you can count results since the reset. Exception: Shops ads need a minimum of 17 purchases through the website plus 5 through Meta to complete learning. Multiple independent sources confirm the threshold and its consequences: Ben Heath adds that most lead-gen businesses fall short of 50 leads a week, so early-funnel friction should be minimized to feed conversion volume, and Dr. Matt Shiver adds that learning happens at the ad-set level and that wave-like performance (great days alternating with dead days) is usually a symptom of too few event fires at the ad set. Andrew Faris cites Meta's repeated public guidance as 50 to 100 conversions per week per ad set and summarises the platform's framing as "conversions are currency for its machine learning". He draws the structural consequence: splitting SKUs or audiences into separate ad sets spreads conversions thin, so every split has to be paid for in signal. He qualifies it from his own book of accounts, saying he has not found the platform needs exactly that number to work perfectly. The mechanistic account of what happens BELOW the threshold, including what delivery reinforces on before 50 events land, is a separate claim at [[Learning & Signal#LS-036|LS-036]], not a restatement of this one. The structural gate that follows from the threshold is at [[Learning & Signal#LS-035|LS-035]].
DURATION ADDED 2026-08-20, AND IT IS T3, NOT PART OF THE T1 CORE. Meta states the exit CONDITION and never states how long the phase lasts. Ben Heath fills that gap: "The learning phase lasts typically, let's say, 24 to 48 hours, but it is dependent on conversion volume. So, the more conversions you generate, the faster Meta is able to learn, because they have more data, and the shorter that learning phase will last, too." The clock is downstream of the volume, so 24 to 48 hours describes an account already producing events at rate, not a floor any account can expect, and at thousands of sales a day he says learning compresses to almost nothing. Direct read for our own book: a clinic ad set at ten leads a week will never see a 48-hour learning phase, and quoting that range to a client sets an expectation the account cannot meet. It is the second duration figure on file and it agrees with the one-to-two-day resettle the same operator gives at [[Learning & Signal#LS-003|LS-003]]. From his managed book, nothing shown.
CONTESTED ON USE, NOT ON FACT. Blue Sense Digital does not dispute that the threshold exists or that Meta defines learning exit this way. He disputes using it to size test budgets, and names "setting budgets at the adset level based on the 50 conversions in a 7-day window rule" as one of four common mistakes below $50k/month spend. The arithmetic he walks through: back-propagating 50 conversions in 7 days at a $100 expected CPA gives $714/day for one concept ad set, so five to ten concurrent concept tests demand $3,500 to $7,000/day of test budget alone. His replacement formula is (target conversions x expected CPA) / test duration in days, so 20 conversions at a $100 expected CPA over 14 days = $143/day. The operator picks the significance threshold and the learning speed, then derives the daily budget, rather than inheriting Meta's number. Boundary: the learning-phase exit threshold stands as Meta documentation, its use as a budgeting input does not. Arithmetic worked on screen in both videos, no account test shown. His cruder verdict rule for the same decision is banked at [[Learning & Signal#LS-023|LS-023]]. A second and different objection to using the number arrived 2026-08-19 and is banked in full at [[Learning & Signal#LS-005|LS-005]]: Nick Theriot does not dispute the threshold, he disputes that reaching it changes anything, so he never manages an ad set toward the number and runs many ad sets that will never see 50 events a week.

### LS-002 · Six edits always reset the learning phase: targeting, creative, optimization event, adding a new ad, pausing 7+ days, and bid strategy
Tier: T1 · Status: active
Only a "significant edit" causes re-entry into learning; every edit affects delivery but not every edit resets learning. The always-significant list is: any change to targeting, any change to ad creative, any change to optimization event, adding a new ad to the ad set, pausing the ad set for 7 days or longer (learning restarts on unpause), and changing bid strategy. With Advantage+ campaign budget, switching the campaign bid strategy can reset multiple ad sets at once.
Blue Sense Digital adds the operating asymmetry that follows from the "adding a new ad" trigger. If the ad set is off KPI, add ads, remove ads, do whatever, because the sequencing the algorithm learned is not working anyway. If the ad set is hitting KPI, do not inject new ads at all. Launch them as a separate ad set named for the same concept (his example: "concept one shoot two") so the account stays filterable by concept. He grounds it in breaking a working ad set himself years ago. Same principle as [[Meta Delivery & Andromeda#MD-011|MD-011]]. Practitioner rule, no test shown.
The corollary he added on 2025-12-03 is how to feed a starved ad without paying the reset. Meta's ad-level creative testing tool forces a minimum spend through up to five ads that are ALREADY inside the ad set, which he says keeps the ad set's accumulated learning, while duplicating those same ads out into a fresh ad set to force spend there throws that learning away. Useful because it is the only route on file that increases spend on a specific ad without triggering any item on Meta's always-significant list. Asserted mechanism, nothing measured, and the tool's own availability limits belong to MD.
A SECOND OPERATOR'S RESET LIST, 2026-08-20, AND WHAT IS MISSING FROM IT. Ben Heath: "If you make a significant adjustment to a campaign, ad set, so for example, you create a new ad, you change some of the settings at the ad set level, potentially even change some of the settings at the campaign level, you will re-enter the learning phase." Two things to take. He confirms the ad-creation trigger independently, and that is the trigger the whole operating asymmetry above hangs on. And BUDGET IS ABSENT from his list. Record the absence as an observation, never as a denial. He is giving examples, not enumerating, so silence is not evidence of anything. It matters only because the codex carries this same operator's 2x budget-reset figure at [[Learning & Signal#LS-003|LS-003]], and the flag lives there.
CONFLICT ON THE ASYMMETRY, 2026-08-20. Blue Sense's rule above says never inject a new ad into an ad set that is hitting KPI. Matt Shiver's default at low volume is the opposite: under five conversions a day he puts new ads INTO the existing ad set on purpose, to concentrate pixel fires, accepting the reset as the cheaper cost. Neither ran the comparison. The numbers and the reasoning are at [[Learning & Signal#LS-035|LS-035]].

### LS-003 · Budget, spending-limit, and bid/cost/ROAS-goal changes reset learning only if the change is large; Meta's example is $100 to $101 (no reset) versus $100 to $1,000 (likely reset)
Tier: T1 · Status: active
Three areas are magnitude-dependent: ad set spending limit amount; bid control, cost-per-result goal or ROAS goal amount; and budget amount. Meta's own example: raising budget from $100 to $101 "isn't likely" to reset learning, while $100 to $1,000 "may" reset one or more ad sets. Under Advantage+ campaign budget, adjusting the campaign budget can push multiple ad sets back into learning.
T3 PRACTITIONER ENRICHMENT ADDED 2026-08-19. THE T1 CORE ABOVE IS UNCHANGED. Meta documents that a threshold exists and gives one illustration. It never says where the threshold sits, and that gap is the whole operating problem. Ben Heath fills it with a size-dependent rule from an 11-year, $300M managed book. A 3% daily raise is small enough that it is "unlikely to go back into the learning phase", which is why his automated scaling rules run at 3% a day. A doubling-scale manual raise does reset it. On his manual ladder, which starts at a 100% step at $50/day, he says flatly "You will re-enter the learning phase", and then "it's going to take Meta a day, maybe two, to resettle the campaign", which is why he holds five to ten days between manual steps. Check the arithmetic before treating this as new law: $50 to $100 is a 2x and Meta's own reset illustration is a 10x, so Heath's figure sits below Meta's example and is directionally consistent with it. This is enrichment, not contradiction.
What it changes is the question. Stop asking whether a budget change resets learning. Ask where the percentage threshold sits, because 3% and 100% land on opposite sides of it and nobody has mapped the middle. The 1 to 2 day resettle is the first resettle DURATION on file anywhere in this codex: [[Learning & Signal#LS-001|LS-001]]'s roughly 50 events in a week is the exit condition, this is how long the disruption itself lasts before results are worth reading. Heath shows no learning-status screenshot and no test in either video; the numbers come from his managed book. Budget step size as a scaling decision belongs to [[Scaling Models#SC-001|SC-001]], not here.
A second practitioner claim touches the same threshold from the opposite direction and is much weaker. Sam Piliero forces test spend by setting an ad set average daily minimum equal to 1x target CPA, removes it after exactly seven days, and says "you don't worry about the learning phase or resetting anything." Meta's documentation above lists ad set spending limit amount as one of the three magnitude-dependent areas, so removing a floor worth a full day's target CPA is not obviously under the threshold. Asserted, with no data on what happens to learning after removal. Treat as unverified.
FLAG ON THE PRACTITIONER FIGURE, ADDED 2026-08-20. Three months before the $50-to-$100 claim above, the same operator was asked what re-enters learning and answered with a list that names creating a new ad, changing ad set settings and possibly campaign settings, with no mention of budget at all (quoted in full at [[Learning & Signal#LS-002|LS-002]]). He was giving examples rather than enumerating, so this denies nothing and does not move the T1 core, which documents a magnitude-dependent budget threshold outright. Carry it anyway, because the operator this claim leans on for the only percentage figure anyone has offered did not name budget when listing resets. It is a reason to treat 3% and 100% as his working assumptions rather than as measurements.

### LS-004 · Advantage+ campaign budget's normal redistribution between ad sets does NOT reset learning, and an ad-set-level significant edit does not reset sibling ad sets
Tier: T1 · Status: active
Meta's FAQ states: ad sets won't re-enter learning as CBO distributes budget; a significant edit made at the ad set level does not reset other ad sets in the campaign; adding a new ad set to an Advantage+ campaign budget campaign does not reset the existing ad sets; and initial learning takes the same time as with ad set budgets. Changing a social-issues/elections/politics disclaimer IS a significant edit.
PRACTITIONER CONFIRMATION AND THE STRUCTURE IT BUYS, 2026-08-20. A guest on Charley T's session states the batching rule that falls straight out of this documentation: "how we're going to be doing this is structuring around creative batches, not individual ads. And the reason for that is because each new batch will go into a new learning phase, not the entire campaign itself." Charley T does not dispute it. No new platform fact, and none of the numbers change. What it adds is the reason the batch-as-ad-set structure exists at all: the T1 above is what makes launching a creative batch into its own ad set cheaper than injecting it into a working one, which is the same asymmetry Blue Sense argues from the other side at [[Learning & Signal#LS-002|LS-002]]. Asserted, nothing shown.

### LS-005 · "Learning limited" is diagnosed when an ad set is unlikely to receive about 50 optimization events in the week after the last significant edit
Tier: T1 · Status: active
Meta says learning limited "isn't a penalty" but a signal that budget isn't being spent effectively because the system can't optimize with the current setup. Named causes: small audience, low budget, low bid or cost control, high auction overlap, an infrequent optimization event, or running too many ads at once. Fixes Meta lists: combine ad sets/campaigns, expand the audience, raise budget, raise bid/cost control, or switch to a more frequent optimization event (for example purchase to add-to-cart). It clears to Active once enough events accrue. Shops ads become learning limited if they lack 17 website + 5 Meta purchases after 7 days.
CONTESTED ON VALUE, NOT ON DEFINITION, added 2026-08-19. Nick Theriot attacks the folk belief sitting on top of Meta's definition rather than the definition itself. The belief he names: an ad set running a 0.5 ROAS while learning limited will jump to a 3.0 the moment it flips to Active. His verdict is "you getting out of learning limited, it doesn't magically change performance", so he does not act on the status at all. He accepts the roughly 50 conversions a week requirement and says the only thing that gets an ad set there is better creative, not the fixes on Meta's list. Read carefully, the two positions are compatible. Meta says the label signals that budget is not being spent effectively; it never promises that clearing the label improves anything. What Theriot removes is the intervention: combining ad sets or raising budget in order to chase the Active label is, on his account, buying a label. This is the doctrine underneath his ad-set structure, contested at [[Learning & Signal#LS-035|LS-035]]. Practitioner position from real spend, no test, and no data on Meta's side either.

### LS-006 · During learning, ad sets are less stable with usually higher CPA; do not edit during learning, and too many concurrent ads per dollar starves every ad
Tier: T1 · Status: active
Meta's best practices: wait to edit until out of learning because editing resets learning and delays optimization; avoid unnecessary edits; avoid high ad volumes because "the delivery system learns less about each ad and ad set" when there are many, and combining similar ad sets combines learnings; use realistic budgets since very small or inflated budgets give the system an inaccurate signal, and frequent budget changes can re-trigger learning. Meta also says not to try to avoid the learning phase entirely because it is necessary for optimization. Fraser Cottrell independently confirms the overload mechanism from agency practice: too many concurrent creatives spreads budget so thin that no ad accumulates enough spend, ads fail to leave the learning phase, and results look like creative failure when the cause is budget dilution, which is why his volume prescriptions scale with spend.
A NUMBER FOR "AVOID UNNECESSARY EDITS", ADDED 2026-08-20. Meta says avoid unnecessary edits and never says how often is too often. Ben Heath sets the interval: "what I typically recommend advertisers do is try and set an optimization schedule. So, decide I'm going to adjust my campaigns no more than once every 7 days or 10 days. And you need to extend that time period because you are operating with a small budget and you are going to typically be generating lower conversion volumes." The interval is indexed to conversion volume, same as everything else in this section, and he names the failure mode as helicopter parenting, meaning an operator who resets learning by touching the account daily. Direct read for our own accounts, which are all low volume: the floor is 10 days and probably longer, and a weekly reporting rhythm is not a licence for a weekly editing rhythm. T3 prescription, no test.

### LS-023 · Spend 3x target CPA before a concept verdict, 5x for a clean yes/no, and run the test about one week
Tier: T3 · Status: active
The spend threshold: at 3x target CPA, three or more conversions keep it running, two is a yellow light worth one more increment, zero or one is a cut. At 5x there is no yellow light, only a decision, which he prefers because early spend is the system learning who to target and efficiency improves once it has. The one-week duration is derived from two forces rather than tradition. Day-of-week seasonality means a test launched on a good day reads better regardless of the creative. A 3 to 4 day time-to-purchase means a one-day test kills the top-of-funnel ad while its influenced buyers convert later through other creative. Stated ranges: 3-4 days for a large stable business with short purchase cycles, 10-14 days for a small business with long cycles and heavy daily seasonality. This is the practical companion to the budget formula in [[Learning & Signal#LS-001|LS-001]]: the formula sizes the daily budget, this sizes the verdict. [[Scaling Models#SC-010|SC-010]]'s same-day kill is the emergency override, not the normal window. Agency practice, no test shown.
TWO MORE OPERATORS ON THE SAME WINDOW, 2026-08-20, AND THEY INDEX IT DIFFERENTLY. Ben Heath indexes read time to conversion volume rather than to a spend multiple: "the campaign that generates six conversions a week, it might take a month to know if a new ad is performing well, whereas the one that's generating thousands a day, it might take 3 hours. Like literally, I'm not over exaggerating that." Take the low end seriously, because six conversions a week is an ordinary clinic ad set and a month sits far outside the 3-to-4-day and 10-to-14-day band above. If he is right, most weekly creative verdicts on a small lead-gen account are being called on noise. Nick Theriot holds a fixed clock instead: 7 days before an ad is assessed, and another 7-day commitment after every budget change, reasoning that "Facebook is consistently inconsistent. Give it 7 days and it'll have more time to optimize." Honesty note on that one, the transcript line sits inside him narrating a position he is reacting to, so the attribution is weaker than the rest of this claim. Three operators, three indexes (spend multiple, conversion volume, fixed clock), and not one of them ran a test.

### LS-024 · Incumbent ads decaying the day new ads launch on a fixed budget is cannibalization, not fatigue
Tier: T3 · Status: active
On a fixed budget a new ad can only take spend from ads that were already working, so every launch is a zero-sum reallocation. The incumbent decay operators read as fatigue is an accounting artefact of that reallocation, and the same split explains why the new ad also underperforms. Concrete diagnostic: if incumbent performance drops on the same day new ads go live with no budget change, that is cannibalization. The argument runs against launch-heavy testing cadences, since more launches on flat budget means more reallocation and no more reach. Asserted from managing over a million dollars a week, no data shown. A spend-share breakdown before and after a launch would test it directly. Read next to [[Meta Delivery & Andromeda#MD-006|MD-006]], which describes the same launch-day degradation from the delivery side (new ads served to the warmest audience first).

### LS-025 · Learning accrues at all three levels at once: the ad learns who to reach, the ad set learns which ad to show, the campaign learns which ad set to fund
Tier: T4 · Status: active
Stated explicitly against practitioners who claim learning lives at one level only. It is the mechanistic argument under the consolidation doctrine: every pause discards purchased learning at whichever level it accrued, and every new campaign restarts all three from zero. Asserted mechanism, no documentation cited and no test. Filed as T4 because [[Scaling Models#SC-014|SC-014]] and [[Scaling Models#SC-012|SC-012]] lean on this assumption and the codex had no entry stating it directly. Note the conflict with the platform record: Meta's own documentation in [[Learning & Signal#LS-001|LS-001]] and [[Learning & Signal#LS-004|LS-004]] describes the learning phase at the ad set level, and says an ad-set-level significant edit does not reset sibling ad sets, which is hard to reconcile with campaign-level learning being a real stored quantity.
INDEPENDENT SECOND VOICE, AND IT ADDS A FOURTH LEVEL, 2026-08-19. Blue Sense Digital states the same architecture for Google and generalizes it: an impression inherits data from the ad level, the ad group level, the campaign level and the ACCOUNT level simultaneously, and he says the same hierarchy governs Meta, TikTok and Pinterest. The account level is the piece Charley T's three-level version lacks, and it is not a lone assertion, because the codex already carries SC-094, that accumulated learning lives in the ad account rather than the pixel and a fresh account starts from zero on the same pixel. Two unrelated operators reaching the account level independently is the strongest support this claim has, and it means the claim is no longer Meta-only. He also puts weightings on it and they must NOT be codified: "maybe the account has 10% to do with the bid at the user level maybe the campaign data has 20% maybe the ad group has 10% and then the ad has 50%". He says on the record that those numbers are made up to make the point and that the true weights are unknown and vary by account. Take only the direction, that the ad's own data dominates the bid. The operating consequence is the load-bearing part: campaign structure IS the data-segmentation decision, so every split creates that many separate models each solving for the same customer in isolation, and conversion data crosses the account boundary only in very small amounts. The Google-specific prescriptions that follow (how many PMax campaigns, how many asset groups, at what daily spend) belong to GP, not here. Reasoned architecture on both operators' side, no documentation quoted by either, nothing measured.

### LS-035 · Split spend into another ad set only when the budget can carry every resulting ad set out of learning; ceiling of three, otherwise one
Tier: T3 · Status: contested
Charley T's single gating question for account structure, stated twice in four months. Three ad sets is the ceiling inside a CBO, and that count is conditional on the budget clearing the learning phase for all three. If it cannot, drop to one ad set. Stated plainly on 2025-12-06: "I'm only having multiple ad sets if I can get them out of learning." Ben Heath states the arithmetic version in a joint session: one campaign producing 50 conversions a week is much better than two campaigns producing 25 conversions a week each, because the split risks learning limited status and halves the data the operator can read. Charley T agrees and notes he published the same argument in 2022. The rule turns every structural choice into a signal cost, so a split is a net negative regardless of what is being tested if the resulting ad sets cannot each clear learning. The threshold itself is [[Learning & Signal#LS-001|LS-001]], the learning limited state is [[Learning & Signal#LS-005|LS-005]], and the ad-count half of the same structure is [[Learning & Signal#LS-034|LS-034]]. Three statements across two operators, none of them tested.
CONTESTED 2026-08-19, AND THE CONTESTING SIDE HAS AN ACCOUNT ON SCREEN. Nick Theriot runs the opposite structure and reports it working. Every new ad idea becomes a new ad set inside the one existing CBO, one ad set per numbered idea, all on the same audience, left to compete for spend. Six idea-numbered ad sets are visible in one campaign in his 2026-08-19 walkthrough, and he describes accounts holding 150 to 180 ad sets with 10 to 20 live at a time. His defence is that the signal cost Charley T names does not apply when the ad sets are not differentiated by audience: fragmentation would bite if each ad set carried a different interest group and demographic, and his do not, they differ only by creative. On his own numbers those ad sets cannot all be clearing learning. The campaign in the walkthrough runs $800/day at a $35 cost per purchase, so roughly 23 purchases a day across 10 to 20 live ad sets, one or two each, nowhere near 50 a week. That campaign moved from a $98 to a $45 cost per purchase over the window he shows. The gate Charley T calls decisive was not satisfied and the account got better anyway.
Neither operator ran the comparison. Charley T asserts the gate with nothing shown. Theriot shows the account but not the counterfactual, so his account improving proves the structure is survivable, not that it is optimal. Both agree the cost of a split is paid in signal; they disagree on whether an identical-audience split costs anything at all, and that is the testable question. Cross-platform support for Charley T's side arrives the same day from Blue Sense Digital on Google: one campaign holding 1,000 monthly conversions models the end consumer considerably better than the same volume split across campaigns, because every split becomes a separate model solving for the same demographic in isolation (mechanism at [[Learning & Signal#LS-025|LS-025]]). Carry his own internal tension rather than smoothing it: in the same video he argues a consolidated structure can report a higher ROAS while producing FEWER profit dollars than a structure segmented by gross margin, so consolidation wins on signal and can lose on money. That half is marketing math and belongs to MM. Illustrative numbers, no account shown.
TWO MORE ARITHMETICS ON HEATH'S SIDE, AND THE FIRST HARD THRESHOLD ANYWHERE ON THIS QUESTION, 2026-08-20. Heath restates the consolidation case twice more with worked numbers. On 2026-02-17: "Let's say you generate 70 conversions in a week. Instead of having that spread across 10 different adsets, so seven conversions roughly per adset per week, Meta is going to struggle to optimize those adsets." On 2026-02-20, scaled down to a small budget: "let's say we had five ad sets and we're generating 20 conversions a week. Well, that's four conversions per ad set on average. Much better to have the one ad set and have those 20 conversions going through the one ad set." His small-budget prescription is one offer, one campaign, one ad set, and where the operator has no data on which offer is most profitable he says guess from competitor focus or best margin and adjust later. Both are illustrative arithmetic, not measurements, and both restate the same mechanism rather than adding a second one.
Matt Shiver supplies the number the whole argument has been missing. His rule of thumb is a conversion-volume threshold for where new creative goes: "If you are getting less than five conversions per day... you're going to just want to throw these in the existing ad set... If you're getting more than five per day... we want to do new ad sets... if we're not getting enough conversion fires, we want to try to consolidate these things into an existing ad set so that we can have as much pixel fires as possible." Five a day is 35 a week, which sits between Heath's roughly 50 and Heath's own 20-conversion example, and it converts Charley T's gate from a judgment call into a countable one. Note where it collides. Blue Sense's rule at [[Learning & Signal#LS-002|LS-002]] says never inject new ads into an ad set that is hitting KPI, launch them as a separate ad set. Shiver says the exact opposite below five conversions a day, and pays the reset deliberately to keep the pixel fires in one place. Both positions are stated as rules of thumb from agency practice with nothing shown, and at low volume they give opposite instructions for the same account, which is precisely the situation every account on our own book is in.

### LS-036 · Below roughly 50 conversions the ad set samples 1,000 to 4,000 users and reinforces on soft engagement such as 50% video hold, so an off-target hook trains delivery toward the wrong audience
Tier: T4 · Status: active
This is a claim about WHAT delivery reinforces on during the learning phase. [[Learning & Signal#LS-001|LS-001]] is a claim about WHEN the learning phase ends. Different mechanisms, same number, and they should not be read as one fact restated. ### LS-042 · Negative reinforcement moves the model away from one coordinate in the vector space, never away from a whole attribute
Tier: T4 · Status: active
When a user the model predicted highly clicks and fails to convert, that failure pushes back into the tree-based models, and Blue Sense Digital's point is that the correction is surgical. Meta does not learn "stop targeting anyone interested in pets" because one pet owner failed to convert on a pet product. The vector representation exists precisely so the model can reinforce away from one undesirable attribute of that user while keeping the useful ones, and he frames the complexity of the labeling process as existing to enable that precision. This is the mechanistic explanation [[Learning & Signal#LS-030|LS-030]] lacks. It says why a handful of junk events does not poison a pixel, and why contamination only bites when the bad events dwarf the good ones by an order of magnitude. It also bounds the blast radius of a bad creative or a bad lead batch, since the correction lands on a coordinate rather than on a segment, which cuts against the folk fear that one bad week wrecks an account. Reasoned from architecture, no documentation cited, untested.
