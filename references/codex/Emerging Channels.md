# Emerging Channels

Paid channels outside Meta, Google and TikTok: Pinterest, Snapchat, Reddit, Amazon, X, and the Applovin/Axon network.

Part of the [[00-Codex|Advertising Science Codex]]. Claims follow the tier system (T1 docs, T2 shown test, T3 practitioner, T4 theory).

**Why this file exists.** Opened 2026-08-19 after two consecutive research runs produced claims with nowhere to live. On 2026-08-18 a merge agent correctly refused to file an Axon claim into TikTok Delivery rather than misfile it. On 2026-08-19 a reader flagged three more under a "NO HOME" heading. Four homeless claims across two days is enough to open the topic. The alternative was to keep forcing non-TikTok channels into the TikTok file, which would corrupt it.

**Reading warning.** Every claim here is T3 or below. No operator on our roster runs these channels as a primary book, so the coverage is opinion from people whose main business is elsewhere. Treat this file as a map of where the roster is sceptical, not as evidence about the channels themselves. Anyone proposing spend on one of these should expect to run their own test rather than cite this file.

## Claims

## Pinterest

### EC-001 · Pinterest view-through over-attribution is structurally worse than other platforms because the grid serves about six posts at once, so any ad in the viewport can claim a later conversion.
Tier: T3 · Status: active
Mechanism is the UI, not the attribution model: a Pinterest feed renders roughly six posts simultaneously, so a single scroll puts several ads in view at once. Any of them can then claim a conversion the user makes on that brand later. The result is view-through counts the operator describes as "crazy". This is the same structural failure our attribution laws describe on Meta, amplified by grid density. Practitioner observation across many clients plus a UI mechanism. No attribution data shown.
Related: the view-through law in [[Attribution & Incrementality]] (AT-012, AT-037). The operator action is the same, turn view-through off and audit for it, and it matters more here.
Sources: Blue Sense Digital, 2025-08-18, "Should You Should Run TikTok Ads In 2025?"
Last touched: 2026-08-19

### EC-002 · Pinterest works for a narrow category set, mainly furniture and bathroom ware, and does not scale, because user demand on the platform is thin.
Tier: T3 · Status: active
A category read from a real book of accounts. He names furniture and bathroom ware as the categories where it is workable and says the platform does not scale because there is not enough user demand on it. No spend, CAC or account figures given. Note this is a demand-side ceiling claim, not a delivery or auction claim, so it should be cheap to falsify on any single account.
Sources: Blue Sense Digital, 2025-08-18, "Should You Should Run TikTok Ads In 2025?"
Last touched: 2026-08-19

## Cross-channel

### EC-003 · Snapchat, Pinterest and TikTok hit the same attribution and scale limits as each other, and a free-ad-spend offer from a platform is a negative signal rather than a reason to test it.
Tier: T3 · Status: active
Drawn from an audit of a private-equity-owned multi-brand group spending across Pinterest, TikTok, Snapchat, Meta and Google. His argument is an arbitrage one: "if these were incredible arbitrage opportunities to drive new customer acquisition, everyone would be on them." The free-spend-credit half is the operationally useful piece, because a platform buying its way onto a media plan is evidence about demand for its inventory. No numbers from the audit are shown, so the claim rests entirely on the reasoning.
Sources: Blue Sense Digital, 2025-08-18, "Should You Should Run TikTok Ads In 2025?"
Last touched: 2026-08-19

## Applovin / Axon

### EC-004 · Axon is claimed to be the first new visibility source since Meta in 2012 to acquire net new customers at scale, at efficiency beating Meta in many cases, and to already hold more advertising market share than TikTok.
Tier: T3 · Status: active
Ezra Firestone's position. Two separate assertions ride in it and they carry different weight. The acquisition claim is a practitioner read from his own book. The market-share claim is a factual assertion about the industry with no source cited, and it should not be repeated to anyone as a fact until it is checked against a published figure. He simultaneously frames it as no real change, on the grounds that buying visibility is the same job in a new colour. Asserted, no account data shown, no market-share source cited.
**This claim was refused by the TikTok merge agent on 2026-08-18 rather than misfiled. It is the reason this topic file exists.**
Sources: Andrew Faris, 2026-05-15, "Ezra Firestone: Nothing Has Changed In Ecom In 20 Years"
Last touched: 2026-08-19
