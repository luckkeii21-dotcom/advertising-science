## 1. The mechanism

Here is a question you have answered twice this year, both times from argument rather than from a document.

A winner is tiring. You have three fresh ads ready. Do they go into the ad set the tiring winner lives in, or into a new ad set of their own?

On Meta this question has no published answer. Meta documents the consequence of the choice and stops there: adding a new ad to an ad set resets that ad set's learning phase. It does not tell you whether paying the reset is worth it. Four named operators have filled that silence and they do not agree. One says never inject ads into an ad set that is hitting its target, launch them separately. One says below five conversions a day do the opposite on purpose, pay the reset, keep every conversion firing in one place. One runs ten to twenty parallel ad sets that cannot all be clearing learning and shows an account improving anyway. One says do not open a second ad set below a hundred conversions a week, and even then only to solve a named problem. Nobody ran the comparison. Nobody showed the counterfactual.

TikTok answers the same question in writing, in its own help centre, in one sentence.

When you refresh creative, add the new creative to the **existing** ad group rather than creating a new one, in order to extend that ad group's lifetime.

That is it. No volume threshold, no exception for an ad group that is hitting target, no "it depends." The platform that documents less than Meta about almost everything happens to document this.

There is a second half of TT-004 that is just as useful, because it kills a habit rather than settling an argument. TikTok's stated trigger to refresh is **a condition, not a calendar**. Refresh when delivery results show a consistently declining trend, or when daily new users are low. There is no every-fourteen-days rule on that page. The trigger is something the account does, not something the date does.

The analogy that maps. A restaurant kitchen has one pass where every plate goes out. A new dish does not get its own pass. It joins the one that already exists, because the pass is where the staff have learned the rhythm, who is slow, which table waits. Open a second pass for the new dish and you have two half-trained passes instead of one trained one. And you do not change the menu on the first of the month. You change it when plates start coming back.

What TikTok has published is not a Meta rule. It is one platform's answer to a question every auction-based delivery system faces, which makes it evidence about the **class** of mechanism rather than about Meta's implementation. Hold it that way and it is worth a lot. Hold it as "Meta says so too" and you have invented a rule.

## 2. The evidence

[[TikTok Delivery#TT-004|TT-004]] is **T1**. It is TikTok's own creative best-practices document, last updated June 2025, read directly. Both halves come from the same page: the refresh trigger and the put-it-in-the-existing-ad-group instruction.

Three T1 claims sit behind it on the same platform and they agree with each other. [[TikTok Delivery#TT-002|TT-002]] says TikTok's learning volatility starts to decline after about 25 results or 7 days, and names edits that retrigger learning as a thing to avoid while you are in it. [[TikTok Delivery#TT-003|TT-003]] gives the structure recommendation, 3 to 5 active ad groups per account and 3 to 5 creatives per ad group, with big differences between creatives when you are testing. [[TikTok Delivery#TT-005|TT-005]] gives the creative signals, hook inside 6 seconds, proposition inside 3, native vertical 9:16, 5 to 10 words per second of on-screen text. Put those four together and TikTok's documented position is a narrow, consolidated account that refreshes inside its existing containers.

Now the Meta side, and notice the tier gap.

[[Learning & Signal#LS-002|LS-002]] is **T1**: adding a new ad to an ad set is on Meta's always-significant list, so it resets learning. That is the cost, documented. [[Learning & Signal#LS-001|LS-001]] is **T1** for the exit condition, roughly 50 results in the week after the last significant edit. [[Learning & Signal#LS-090|LS-090]] is **T1** for its sentences and it prices the other direction: every edit costs twice, once for the learning you restarted and once for the wrong instruction that was spending at full price the whole time, and only the first cost appears on screen.

The actual decision, where the new creative goes, sits at [[Learning & Signal#LS-035|LS-035]], which is **T3 and contested**. Four operators, four positions, nothing shown by any of them.

So the shape of the evidence is this. On Meta the costs are T1 and the decision is T3-contested. On TikTok the decision is T1. That asymmetry is the thing to learn, and it is more useful than either rule, because it tells you where to look next time. **When your platform leaves a decision to folklore, read the other platform's documentation before you read another operator's opinion.** It will not bind you. It will tell you which of the competing opinions has a documented sibling somewhere.

## 3. Our accounts

We run no TikTok spend. Nothing below is a TikTok result and nothing below validates TT-004. What the claim does is give us a published second opinion on a decision we already made on reasoning alone.

On 31 October the standing rule on our book became: new ads go **into** the existing ad set, and Meta decides which ones get spend. Separate ad sets only where a hard setting forces it, a different language with its own native-language form. That decision was reached from volume arithmetic, because every account we run sits low enough that concentrating conversion events beat preserving a clean container. TikTok's documentation reaches the same instruction from a different direction entirely, extending the ad group's lifetime, with no volume condition attached. Two independent routes to the same move is not proof. It is the difference between a bet and a bet with a second witness.

Where the volume arithmetic actually lands, from the week of 30 October to 5 November:

| Lane | Opt-ins in the week | Per day | Cost per opt-in |
|---|---|---|---|
| SJR Commercial, whole account | 124 | 17.7 | $6.21 |
| SJR van lane | 88 | 12.6 | $5.59 |
| SJR dump lane | 36 | 5.1 | $7.72 |
| Phoenix Truxx, whole account | 80 | 11.4 | $17.07 |
| Phoenix dump ring | 11 | 1.6 | $30.28 |

Read the account rows and both accounts look like they could afford a split. Read the lane rows and the picture changes: the Phoenix dump ring produces 1.6 opt-ins a day, and a second container there would be two ad sets that never clear anything. **The threshold has to be applied to the container you are about to create, never to the account total.** That is the mistake the account row invites.

Now the refresh trigger, TT-004's other half, applied to real rows as a reading exercise rather than as a TikTok rule:

- **Phoenix Box Truck v2**, 35 opt-ins at $13.36 the prior week, 24 at $16.82 this week. Volume down, cost up. That is a consistently declining delivery trend. Refresh indicated.
- **Phoenix dump ring**, $21.51 to $30.28, up 41%, leaning on a tiring Spanish video at $27.55, and the New York slice at 5 opt-ins and $39.40. Refresh indicated, and the lane is small enough that the fresh creative belongs inside it.
- **SJR Spanish Caesar dump**, which carried the account's highest fatigue reading at 0.32 on 29 October. A fresh Spanish variant was queued then and shipped on 4 November as the Isuzu dump ads, Jerry in English and Felix in Spanish, cut from Eric's own footage. One opt-in each with two days in the window. Too early to read, correctly logged as too early.
- **Phoenix Experience video**, 11 opt-ins at $14.95 to 30 at $10.33, now the account engine and now running on Instagram. Trend rising on both axes. No refresh indicated, and nothing about its age changes that.

That last row is the one to sit with. On a calendar rule it was due for replacement. On TikTok's documented trigger it is the last thing in the account you should touch.

## 4. The decision rule

**Refresh when the delivery trend declines, never when the calendar says so, and put the fresh creative inside the container the tiring one already lives in.**

Two tests, in this order, before you create any new ad set:

1. Is the trend actually declining, in volume or in cost or in both, across at least two comparable windows? If no, do not refresh.
2. Can the container I am about to create carry itself out of learning on its own share of the budget? Ask it of the **lane**, not of the account. If no, the new ads go in beside the incumbents.
