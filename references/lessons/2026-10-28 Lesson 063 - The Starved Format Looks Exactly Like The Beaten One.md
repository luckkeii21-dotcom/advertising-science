# Lesson 063 - The Starved Format Looks Exactly Like The Beaten One

Video: `video/2026-10-28-lesson-063.mp4`

Topic: Creative Science. Harvest-driven. This morning's research pass added [[Creative Science#CR-289|CR-289]] and moved [[Creative Science#CR-196|CR-196]] into contested status. Two operators now report the same delivery behaviour with opposite winners, which makes the behaviour itself the thing worth learning.

## 1. The mechanism

Put a video and four statics in one ad set. Give the ad set $50 a day. Come back in a week.

One of them will have spent almost all of it.

That is the whole mechanism. Inside an ad set, budget is not shared out evenly and it was never promised to be. Delivery finds an ad that works early and the money concentrates there. The other ads are still live, still approved, still sitting in the account looking like they were tested. They were not tested. They were listed.

Now look at the results column. The video has 18 opt-ins. The four statics have 2 between them. What do you conclude?

Most people conclude the statics lost. The statics never played.

Here is the analogy and it maps cleanly. You run a showroom with two salesmen. Every walk-in gets handed to the first one, because he closed well on a slow Tuesday in week one. End of the month he has 40 sales and the other has 2. You fire the man with 2. You never once asked how many people were put in front of him.

**A starved ad and a beaten ad produce the same number. Only the spend column tells them apart, and almost nobody opens it by format.**

The reason this is a trap rather than an inconvenience is that the conclusion feels earned. You ran both. You saw the numbers. The verdict is a measurement in your head and a guess on the page.

## 2. The evidence

Four claims matter here, and their tiers do the teaching.

[[Creative Science#CR-196|CR-196]], T3, as of today **contested**. One operator's cross-account observation: throw images and video in the same ad set and one cannibalises the rest. His words, and note which format he names as the winner: "all the videos will get spent and the images will get no spend". His prescription is to separate the formats. He publishes no spend split, no sample, no window. T3 is right for that.

[[Creative Science#CR-289|CR-289]], T3, banked this morning. A second operator, deliberately running the opposite structure with several formats in one ad set on purpose, reports the same starvation with the winner reversed: "Meta was allocating more and more budget to statics to images". Also no spend split, no account named, no window.

Read those two together and you get something more useful than either. **The mechanism now has two independent sightings and which format wins is not fixed.** That is a stronger finding than "statics are back", and it is the only part of today's harvest that survives contact with an account.

The numbers attached to CR-289 are a four-reading poll of the operator's own YouTube audience, ending at 54% static against 38% video. Do not carry them. No respondent count on any of the four readings, no spend weighting, "best results" never defined, self-selected pool, composition four years apart end to end. **That poll measures what advertisers believe their accounts returned.** A wave of new advertisers making cheap AI statics moves it with nothing changing in the auction at all. Carry the direction, never the 54%, and never show a client any of it.

[[Creative Science#CR-261|CR-261]], **T1**, Meta's own measurement. Ad sets carrying at least an image, a video and a vertical video with audio together delivered 7.3% lower cost per acquisition. Daily median advertiser rates, all purchase-optimised ads, 1 October to 30 November 2025. This sits on the mix-them side of the structure argument. Two honest limits: Meta never says 7.3% lower than what, so the size is unquotable even though the direction is usable, and it is observational, so an ad set carrying all three formats is also an ad set belonging to a better-resourced advertiser.

Notice what a T1 does and does not do here. It does not refute a T3 delivery observation, because both can hold at once: **the diverse ad set can perform better on average and still starve one of its formats.**

[[Creative Science#CR-239|CR-239]], T3, gives the conditional that beats any format law. Static yield rises with what the viewer already knows and falls toward nothing on an unexplained product sold to a stranger. Video is the format when the thing is new and has to be explained, or when the sale depends on believing the seller.

## 3. Our accounts

**Phoenix Truxx, and this one is ours to own.** The dump campaign launched 9 October, CBO $50, two ad sets. Dump | Spanish carries the Chriss video. Dump | English carries four statics: Red F-550, Peterbilt, Ford-350, Oversized Dump.

Week of 9 to 15 October: the lane produced 24 opt-ins at $13.74. The Spanish video took 18 of the 24 at $12.13. The review on 16 October recorded it plainly, Spanish is the dump engine and English dump barely spent. Week of 16 to 22 October: 19 opt-ins at $18.71, and only the Spanish Chriss video working.

So we have the starvation shape in a live account of ours, with video as the winner, which is the direction CR-196 named and the opposite of what was banked today.

**Now the part that costs us.** That split moved two things at the same time. Spanish ad set, video creative. English ad set, static creative. **The lane cannot tell us whether video beat static or whether Spanish beat English, because we never held one of them still.** Both are plausible on this account. Spanish spend is dump-only here by design, and the Spanish audience is the one the dump offer was built for. We got a result and we did not get a finding.

**The same account points the other way one month earlier.** September's drop was entirely the video campaign, 279 opt-ins down to 187. The image ads held 230 both months. What moved on the statics was their cost, $9.40 to $14.94 per lead across the weeks as frequency rose. Same advertiser, same quarter, two readings in opposite directions.

**StayWell:** static is explicitly not the workhorse there. It fatigues at 3.5 frequency and costs roughly twice video per lead.

**SJR Commercial:** the Jerry Windsor box video was the cheapest box ad in the account at $4.29 per form and sold a dump truck. The DUMP_05 Brand Authority static sold another one.

Four accounts and no format law. Which is the point.

## 4. The decision rule

**Before you judge a format, open the spend column by format inside its own ad set and read it in both directions; and never split an ad set on two variables at once.**

## 5. Quiz

Answers go in `_answers-inbox.md`.

**Q1.** A static and a video sit in the same ad set. The video has 18 results, the static has 1. What single column do you open before saying the static lost, and what are the two different things it can show you?

**Q2.** CR-289 reports 54% static against 38% video in a poll of an advertising audience. Name the specific reason that number cannot be quoted, and say what part of CR-289 you do carry.

**Q3.** CR-261 is T1 and says mixed-format ad sets delivered 7.3% lower cost per acquisition. CR-196 is T3 and says mixing formats starves one of them. Explain why the T1 does not settle the T3.

**Q4.** Applied. Phoenix Truxx dump lane, 9 to 22 October. The Spanish ad set with a video took 18 of 24 opt-ins and the English ad set with four statics barely spent. Kartik asks you to write "video beats static for dump trucks" into the hot sheet. What do you tell him, and what is the smallest change to the lane that would let the account answer the question properly?

**Q5.** Applied. You inherit a client running one ad set with two videos and six statics on $80 a day. Seven of the eight ads show under $4 of lifetime spend. You have budget for one structural change this week. What do you change, what do you expect to learn from it, and which claim in section 2 tells you not to simply delete the seven?

> [!note]- Answer key
> **A1.** Spend, broken out by ad inside that ad set. It can show the static was funded and lost, which is a verdict. Or it can show the static got almost no spend, which is no verdict at all, only an unfunded ad. The results column looks identical either way.
>
> **A2.** It is a poll of advertisers, so it measures what they believe their accounts returned rather than what the auction did. No respondent count on any of the four readings, no spend weighting, "best results" undefined, self-selected pool whose composition is four years apart end to end. What you carry is the direction and, more importantly, the second independent sighting of the starvation mechanism with the winner reversed. Never the percentages, and never in front of a client.
>
> **A3.** They measure different things and both can be true together. CR-261 is an average across many ad sets, so the mixed ad set can come out cheaper on average while still, inside any one of them, concentrating budget on one format and leaving another unfunded. A measurement of the average does not describe the distribution. CR-261 is also observational, so the mixed ad sets are partly just better-resourced advertisers.
>
> **A4.** Tell him the lane changed language and creative format at the same time, so the result cannot be attributed to either one. Then say what it does support: the Spanish Chriss video is the dump engine in that lane, which is a true sentence about that ad, and it belongs in the sheet as that. The smallest fix is to put a Spanish static into the Spanish ad set, or a video into the English ad set, so one variable is held still. Spanish twins of the approved statics are already an open item on this account.
>
> **A5.** Split the ad set, or at minimum move the starved formats into their own ad set with their own budget, so something other than the current winner can get funded. What you learn is which of the seven were beaten and which were never bought an audience. CR-196 and CR-289 together tell you not to delete them, because an ad at under $4 of lifetime spend has produced no evidence about itself in either direction. CR-261 adds a second reason to keep a mix present rather than collapsing to one format.
