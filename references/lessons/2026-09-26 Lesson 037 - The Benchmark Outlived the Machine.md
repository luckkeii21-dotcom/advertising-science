# Lesson 037 · The Benchmark Outlived the Machine

In July 2026 Google shipped a change to Demand Gen tROAS. Its own product copy says the new version stops the system "being overly cautious early on."

There is no toggle for it. There is no before-and-after number. The old bidder has no name and no version. Every Demand Gen tROAS result saved before July was produced by a machine that no longer exists, and nothing in Google Ads will tell you which side of that line your number sits on.

This lesson is about the day a platform swaps the machine and keeps the label.

## 1. The mechanism

A platform can move a number under you in two ways.

**It changes what the counter counts.** The label survives. In September 2026 Google moved Gmail image ads in Demand Gen from two-click to one-click. Under two-click, a Gmail click meant the ad expanded. Under one-click, the same counter means somebody landed on your site. Google's own help page states the rule: clicks are reported from the teaser ad click "whether the click expands the ad body or navigates directly to the advertiser's site." One counter, two different human actions, same column heading.

**It replaces the machine that produces the result.** The name survives. That is the tROAS change. Nothing in the account changed shape. The bidder did.

Both leave your export with no version column. The row from March and the row from August sit in the same table, in the same font, and look comparable. They are not.

Lesson 002 taught that the attribution column is an instrument, and that you choose which one to read. **This is the harder half. You did not choose this change and you cannot choose your way back out of it.** There is no "count the expand" checkbox once Gmail goes one-click, and no "cautious bidder" setting for a pre-July comparison. The old instrument does not come back for one week so you can check.

The analogy that maps. A cricket ground moves its boundary rope in by eight metres and repaints nothing. Every scorecard still says runs. Every average still says runs per dismissal. A batsman who scores more this season did not necessarily get better, and nobody will put the rope back for one innings so you can find out. The unit kept its name and the ground changed size.

**Now the part that decides whether you ever notice: you will not be told in the place you are looking.**

Our own engine checks Google every day. On 24 September, the day Google shipped a monthly Demand Gen product drop, the Google Ads Announcements page held 396 answer ids with 0 added and 0 removed. Not one of the three help articles that drop links to is among those 396. The page a daily check reads is not the page that carries the change.

It is worse than that. Those monthly drops have been running since at least October 2025 and this codex had read two of them. Reading two more, four months late, re-dated two things the September post appeared to introduce: Maps inventory and automotive product feeds both reached Demand Gen in May 2026. **A daily check answers "has the newest thing moved." It never answers "what is already on the shelf."**

## 2. The evidence

- **[[Google Auction & Smart Bidding#GA-082|GA-082]]**, T1. Google Ads Help, read verbatim at source. The click-counting rule above. It carries an honest limit that travels with it: the page is titled "on Display", the change lands in Demand Gen, and Google does not restate the rule on the Demand Gen page. The mechanism is established. The Demand Gen application is the reasonable assumption you verify in an account before quoting it.
- **[[Google Auction & Smart Bidding#GA-084|GA-084]]**, T1 for the existence of the copy. The tROAS upgrade, in Google's words. No toggle named, no figure published. That is precisely why it is operationally dangerous: it cannot be checked in an account and it cannot be switched off.
- **[[Google Auction & Smart Bidding#GA-081|GA-081]]** and **[[Google Auction & Smart Bidding#GA-083|GA-083]]**, both T1 for existence and product copy only. **T1 here means the words are on the page. It does not mean the feature works.** Nothing in either claim states that Demand Gen performs.
- **[[Attribution & Incrementality#AT-036|AT-036]]**, T1. Meta's March 2026 click redefinition: 7-day click narrowed to outbound clicks only, and likes, comments, shares and Page clicks moved into a new 1-day engaged view bucket. Same ads, same buyers, the column stepped down overnight.
- **[[Attribution & Incrementality#AT-097|AT-097]]**, T2, shown on screen. The size of that artefact. One campaign read 415 link clicks against 1,179 all clicks, nearly 3x, and the gap was widest on the video-heavy campaigns and narrowest on the image-heavy ones.

**Read AT-036's tier, because it moved and that is the lesson inside the lesson.** On 18 August, lesson 002 taught this claim at **T3**: one credible operator, no platform note on file. It is **T1** today. Meta did not change anything. What changed is that somebody read Meta's own product copy off the screen, with the old and new option sets side by side, and said the date out loud. **The tier attaches to the observation, never to the person making it and never to how long the claim has been sitting there.**

## 3. Our accounts

Start with the honest part. **We run no Demand Gen anywhere, on any account.** Our only Google account was Mattia Spinal Care, PMax on a roughly 12-mile presence ring around Orlando 32835, and that client offboarded on 24 July 2026. GA-082 and GA-084 cost us nothing this week.

Learn them anyway, because the same shape has already crossed our book once, on Meta, and nobody drew the line.

**Phoenix Truxx is the account with a benchmark sitting on top of a wall.**

The all-time counter opens on or about **22 February 2026**. The 24 April report states 1,069 leads at **$3.19** across **61 days**, and 61 days back from 24 April is 22 February. Meta's click redefinition landed in **March 2026**. So roughly the first five weeks of that counter ran under the old definitions and everything after ran under the new ones.

That counter then became the account's judging line. **$4.00 all-time blended appears as the benchmark in six filed reports**: 23 May, 27 June, 5 July, 9 July, 12 July and 16 July. Two of them use it as a pass/fail gate, in a scorecard row reading "CPL move needs a named cause (vs all-time ~$4.00)". The 9 July report calls it the best-ever benchmark outright.

**And our earliest filed report covers 17 to 24 April.** We hold no reading at all from before the change on this account. The wall is inside the benchmark and outside every report.

**Did it move the number? We cannot say, and the reason matters more than the answer.** Compare attribution settings does not break out on-platform Instant Form results, and our whole book is Instant Forms. Every window column renders a dash. T2 on our own data, from a within-account control on ChiroWorks on 26 August: four Instant Form campaigns all dashed, while one website-pixel campaign in the same view on the same date range populated normally at 1-day click 42 and 7-day click 46. **The instrument that would date this wall does not render on the accounts that need it.**

**The half that definitely changed and definitely does render is clicks.** Phoenix's filed CTR series runs 3.06%, ~3%, ~3%, 2.90%, 3.09%, 3.15%, 3.2%, 2.98%, 3.50%, 3.44%, 3.42%, 3.13%, 2.93%, 3.21% from 24 April to 20 September. Every reading is post-change, so the series is internally consistent and no filed report compares across the boundary. That happened because the reporting started in April. It was not a decision anybody made.

**One live flag on that same series, and it is a check rather than a finding.** The 18 to 24 September week reads **1.70%** against **3.21%** in the 1 to 20 September window that overlaps it by three days. A series that held between 2.90% and 3.50% for five months does not halve on its own. Before anyone writes "engagement dropped" in front of Ruddy, settle whether those two numbers are the same metric on the same denominator. They come from two different pulls and the windows overlap.

**ChiroWorks is the version we built ourselves.** The invisa-RED offer changed from a free consultation to a **$49 introductory session on 6 September 2026**. No Meta pull has been taken on that account since **30 August**. The ad and the live Instant Form still say free consultation. So anyone reading September against August on ChiroWorks is comparing two different offers, with no clean pre-change week on file to compare against, and the creative still advertising the old one. A wall we created, undated, with the before side missing.

## 4. The decision rule

**Before you compare two periods, find out what the platform shipped between them, write the date down, and if the change touched the metric, report the two sides as two series rather than one trend.**

The corollary, and it is the practical half: when a platform changes a metric, judge the change on the output furthest downstream that it did not touch. For the Gmail one-click switch that is conversions and cost per conversion. Never clicks, CTR or cost per click.

## 5. Quiz

Drop your answers in `_answers-inbox.md`. Lesson number plus answers is enough. Partial is fine.

**1.** A Gmail click counts the same under both formats. Name the two different real-world actions it covers, say which direction you expect cost per click to move at the switch with no change in demand, and name the one metric on that page that survives the switch.

**2.** Kartik wants a Phoenix Truxx CTR chart running February 2026 to today for a client deck. Name the three problems with that chart and describe what you build instead.

**3.** A Google rep offers to build Demand Gen for SJR Commercial and cites the 40% conversion increase and the 33% product-feed increase. Answer the rep in four sentences, and say what you would ask him for instead.

**4.** AT-036 was T3 on 18 August and is T1 today. Nothing about Meta changed in between. Say what changed, and state the general rule in one sentence.

**5.** Someone proposes reading ChiroWorks September against August to find out whether the $49 price hurt. Say why that read cannot answer the question, and name the two things that would have had to be true for it to work.

> [!note]- Answer key
>
> **1.** Under two-click, the click means **the ad expanded**. Under one-click, the same counter means **a person reached the landing page**. Cost per click should **rise** with no change in demand, because a site visit is a scarcer event than an expand, so the same spend buys fewer of them. The metric that survives is the separate count of **expanded ad clicks that lead to a website**, visible in Display reporting under the Gmail asset type. Full marks if you also said the ratio of clicks to landing-page sessions will move on its own, and that the honest limit on all of this is that the source page is written for Display while the change lands in Demand Gen.
>
> **2.** Three problems. **One, February and March do not exist in our files.** The earliest filed Phoenix report covers 17 to 24 April, so the left third of that chart would have to be built from a fresh pull nobody has run. **Two, the March 2026 redefinition sits inside the requested range**, so a pre-change CTR and a post-change CTR are two different metrics wearing one label, and AT-097 puts all-clicks near 3x link clicks on video-heavy campaigns. Phoenix ran a video engine for most of that period, which is the format that absorbs the biggest definitional drop. A client would read that as video getting worse. It did not. **Three, the 1.70% reading is unreconciled** and would be the last point on the line. What you build instead: one named click metric, stated on the chart, starting after the change date, with the redefinition drawn as a labelled vertical line if the earlier period is included at all, and the September figures reconciled to one pull before either goes in a deck.
>
> **3.** Four sentences. **One:** we spend nothing on Google for SJR today, so this is opening a new channel rather than improving an existing one, and it gets judged as a new channel. **Two:** the 40% is footnoted Google Internal Data, Global, Gmail Ads, February 2026, it measures **adding Gmail as a channel** rather than the one-click feature it is printed under, it was measured seven months before that feature shipped, and "at the same ROI" makes it a volume statement at constant efficiency, so it is not a cheaper opt-in. **Three:** the 33% publishes its own survivorship filter in the footnote, campaigns active since Q1 2024 with over 50 products, so every campaign that adopted feeds and got switched off is excluded from the cohort by construction. **Four:** neither number has a control group, which puts them in the same family as the 30% at GA-068. **What to ask for instead:** the Uplift Experiment and Campaign Type Attribution that Google shipped in May 2026. Those are counterfactual instruments. They turn the next argument with a rep into a measurement instead of two positions. Accept a fifth point if you made it: automotive product feeds landed in May 2026, not September, so nothing about the timing is urgent.
>
> **4.** What changed is **the quality of the observation**, not the fact. In August the codex had one operator asserting the redefinition with no platform note on file, which is T3. Then somebody read Meta's own product copy off the screen, with the old and new attribution option sets side by side, and dated it out loud. **The rule: the tier attaches to what was actually observed, never to who said it and never to how long the claim has been in the file.** A corollary worth carrying: a T3 claim you have acted on for a month is still T3 until someone goes and looks.
>
> **5.** It cannot answer the question because **the two months differ by more than the price.** The offer changed on 6 September, mid-month, so September is a blend of both offers rather than a clean $49 period. The ad and the live Instant Form still say free consultation, so the traffic in September was still being bought against the old promise and the price only appears later in the journey. And no Meta pull has been taken since 30 August, so the August side of the comparison does not exist in current data either. **The two things that would have to be true: a clean pre-change window pulled and filed before the change date, and the creative and the form saying the same thing as the offer from the day it changed.** Neither is true here, and the second one is a live fix rather than a measurement problem.
>
> **Carry forward.** Three things from this lesson. **The instrument gap is real and it is not closing:** Compare attribution settings cannot date a wall on any Instant Form account we run, so on Meta our only defence is writing platform change dates down ourselves. **Phoenix Truxx's 1.70% CTR needs reconciling before it reaches a client.** And **ChiroWorks needs the ad and the form changed to $49 and a pull taken**, because every day that passes adds another day of contaminated data to a comparison somebody will eventually run.
