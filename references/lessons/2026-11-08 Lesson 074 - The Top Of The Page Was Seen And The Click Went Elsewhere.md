## 1. The mechanism

Three different things can happen to an ad, and we have a column for one of them.

**Served.** The platform put it on the page. That is an impression.

**Seen.** A pair of eyes actually landed on it.

**Chosen.** Somebody clicked.

Google Ads and Ads Manager both report the first and the third. The middle one has never had an instrument in any account we run. Every time we read a weak click-through rate we are guessing about the middle, and the two guesses lead to opposite work. If people never looked, the fix is placement, position and budget. If people looked and moved on, the fix is what the ad said.

The analogy that maps: a shop window on a busy high street. Footfall past the door is the impression. Eyes on the window is the thing nobody counts. The door opening is the click. A shop owner with a thin day will tell you he needs a better spot on the street, and he will be right about half the time. The other half, everyone looked and nobody liked the window.

GA-098 is the first thing this codex holds that measures the middle. The instrument is gaze telemetry recorded on real Google result pages, and the finding is a clean dissociation.

> **99.7% of top-ads carousels are fixated but only 10% of clicks land on them.**

Almost everyone looks. One click in ten goes there. The same corpus records that the shipped ad boxes sit on placements taking about **16% of main-axis clicks**.

The third number is the one that proves this is not an anti-ad finding. On the same pages, **44% of People Also Ask boxes get looked at and exactly two clicks in the whole corpus land on one**. So being seen and not being chosen is a property of the element, and it runs far harder against Google's own organic widget than against the ads.

Read it the right way round and it is a diagnostic gift. At the top of a Google results page, being seen is close to a certainty and a click is scarce. So on a Search campaign holding high impression share on that block with a weak click-through rate, "nobody saw it" is close to ruled out, and the whole burden moves onto the ad text and the query match. Impression share on that block is closer to a ceiling measurement than a growth lever, which sits beside the budget floor at [[Google Auction & Smart Bidding#GA-096|GA-096]].

## 2. The evidence

Four claims, four tiers, and the ranking by usefulness runs almost backwards to the ranking by tier.

**[[Google Auction & Smart Bidding#GA-098|GA-098]] is T2 and it is the most useful thing in this lesson.** arXiv 2605.04949v4, the AllSERP enrichment of the AdSERP dataset, abstract read at source on 28 October. T2 because the instrument is stated and inspectable: **2,776 search trials** on real Google pages carrying gaze, cursor, scroll, pupil and click telemetry alongside the screenshots and the captured HTML. The full paper was not opened, and the abstract was enough to carry three measured numbers.

**Its guards are load-bearing and there are four of them.** This is a lab corpus recorded under eye-tracking, which is a context known to change how people scan. The abstract states no date range, no geography, no device mix and no query mix. **No vertical is named, so not one of these percentages transfers to a specific account and none of them may go in front of a client.** And the 10% is a share of clicks inside the corpus, not a click-through rate. Reading it as one is a plain error, not a judgment call.

**[[Google Auction & Smart Bidding#GA-099|GA-099]] is T1 and it gives us nothing to do.** Google's own blog, 28 October, read in full including both footnotes. Ads in AI Mode scaled to ten more countries, with Brazil, Mexico and the UK named for Q4, and **multiturn ads launched in the US**, which serves ads inside the follow-up turns of a conversation rather than only the opening query. Google published no number for multiturn, no format description, no campaign-type eligibility and no reporting. The two numbers it did publish are footnoted, and the footnotes are the story: queries three times longer than traditional search, sourced to a **May 2026 US report**, and click-through rates "already similar" between sponsored and organic product list items, sourced to a **15-day US internal window, 20 August to 3 September 2026**, with no volume, no vertical and no absolute rate.

Lesson 073 established that tier records provenance and never quality. This pair is that rule applied rather than restated. GA-099 is T1 and unusable because Google published a direction with no instrument behind it, the same shape as [[Attribution & Incrementality#AT-131|AT-131]]. GA-098 is T2 and usable because the method is on the page.

**[[Google Auction & Smart Bidding#GA-043|GA-043]] is T3 and it is the one with money attached.** Broad match, Shopping feeds, PMax and AI Max can all serve inside AI Overviews and AI Mode, with no setting to turn it off and no report that breaks out those clicks or that spend. Checked and found open on 17 September against [[Google PMax & Shopping#GP-047|GP-047]], a Merchant Center visibility product rather than ad reporting, and again on 24 September against [[Google Auction & Smart Bidding#GA-080|GA-080]], a within-Search journey view and not a surface-level breakout. **GA-099 widens that gap without touching it:** more countries, more surfaces, more turns per conversation, same blind spot.

Put GA-098 and GA-043 side by side and the shape of our Google ignorance gets specific. On the conventional results page we now know attention is near-saturated and clicks are scarce. On the AI surfaces we cannot state what share of spend landed there at all.

## 3. Our accounts

Honest answer first: **we hold zero live Google lanes.** Not a Search campaign, not PMax, not Demand Gen, on any account in this book. Nothing in this lesson changes a live number this week, and any sentence implying otherwise would be invented.

The one Google build in the vault is Mattia Spinal Care, and it is instructive because of how it ended. Google PMax, built to spec and pasted by the client on 8 July 2026, running a roughly **12-mile presence-only ring around 32835** in Orlando. Billing was **$1,297 a month for ad management, a $997 Google package and a $250 second-campaign add-on**, and on 4 July the decision was taken to put **100% of the $4,000 a month on Google**. Offboarded 24 July, campaigns left running untouched to 16 August. Kartik's read on record is that the campaigns were five weeks old and needed 30 to 45 days, with medical-term and keyword issues on Google still being cleared. Eric's read is that the account was damaged before us. Both are on file as true.

Two more things from that account are worth carrying. **A second, unrelated Google Ads account was contaminating the landing-page traffic data**, IDs 24031060460 and 24031057880, so check for shadow accounts before trusting any client's on-page conversion rate. And the other team's Mattia Google strategy transcript is a 0-byte file with no copy in Drive or Fathom, so the two live campaigns were audited directly instead, which was better evidence than any deck.

The only Google data we actively use is not a lane at all. The MetaTechAI ads writer was graded on the Google Ads Transparency Center's own days-delivered counts across **19 Google-verified winning software ads**, longest runner **896 days**. That is Google as a research instrument, which we are good at, and it should never be confused with Google as a channel we currently operate.

So the place GA-098 lands is the next Google proposal, not this week's dashboard. The moment somebody asks for a Search lane, the forecast has to state three things our Meta habits will not supply: that top-of-page impression share is close to a ceiling rather than a lever, that the click-through rate is an ad-text and query-match outcome, and that any AI surface in the build carries spend nobody can report on.

**One guard against the obvious misuse.** GA-098 measured a Google results page. It says nothing about a Meta feed, a Reel or an instant form, and carrying 99.7% into a Meta conversation would be exactly the error this lesson exists to stop. Our thin-click problems on Meta are governed by the claims in [[Meta Delivery & Andromeda]], not by this one.

## 4. The decision rule

**On a Google Search campaign holding high impression share on the top-ads block, treat a weak click-through rate as an ad-text and query-match failure and never as a visibility one, because the audience almost certainly looked.**

The buying gate that follows from GA-099 and GA-043: **no Google surface gets bought until somebody has written down what share of its spend will appear in a report.** If the answer is unknown, that is the number the client needs before the forecast, not after it.

> [!note]- Answer key
>
> **1.** 99.7% is the share of top-ads carousels that were fixated, so the denominator is ad blocks and the measure is whether at least one gaze landed. 10% is the share of all clicks in the corpus that landed on a top-ads carousel, so the denominator is clicks, not impressions and not carousels. 16% is the share of main-axis clicks taken by the placements the shipped ad boxes cover. The plain error is reading the 10% as a click-through rate: it is a share of clicks inside the corpus, so it cannot be divided by impressions and cannot be compared to any account's CTR. Deduct for any answer that gives the 10% an impressions denominator.
>
> **2.** GA-098 states its instrument and its sample, so every objection to it is checkable: 2,776 trials, real Google pages, gaze and cursor and click telemetry, plus a stated set of guards about the lab context and the missing query and device mix. GA-099 is Google describing its own rollout, with no number for the new product, no control, no reporting commitment, and its two published figures resting on a five-month-old US report and a 15-day internal window. One is inspectable and limited. The other is unverifiable and promotional. Full marks require naming the multiturn launch as the part with no number at all. No credit for an answer that rests on who published it.
>
> **3.** Conclude: at 82% impression share the audience on that block almost certainly looked, so the weak click-through is pointing at the ad text and the query match, and "buy more visibility" answers a question that is close to already answered. Do not conclude anything numeric. GA-098 names no vertical, states no device or query mix, and none of its percentages transfers to this account or may be quoted to the prospect. The governing claim on budget is [[Google Auction & Smart Bidding#GA-096|GA-096]], because a floor decides whether the channel functions at all and that is asked before the first dollar, while raising a budget that is already working is [[Scaling Models#SC-001|SC-001]] territory. Add that a raise-the-budget recommendation from the company selling the inventory is not evidence the raise pays. Ask to see first: the search terms report, the ad text against the top queries, and whether broad match, PMax or AI Max is in the account, because that decides how much of the spend is reportable at all.
>
> **4.** Something close to: "Broad match and Performance Max can both serve inside AI Overviews and AI Mode, and Google currently provides no setting to turn that off and no report that separates those clicks or that spend, so we will not be able to tell you what share of the budget landed there." Second sentence: "We will report every surface Google gives us a column for, we will re-check for a surface-level breakout each month, and we will not estimate the unreported share, because any figure we gave you would be a guess." Promise the reporting we can do and the re-check. Refuse a modelled split. Deduct for promising to exclude AI surfaces, because there is no control to do it with.
>
> **5.** GA-098 measured gaze on a Google search results page, where the ad block sits in a fixed position above a list the user came to read. A Meta feed is a scroll, the ad is interleaved with content the user chose, and nothing in this corpus measured it. So the 99.7% licenses no statement about whether a Meta creative was seen, and the Meta hook-rate question is governed by the Meta claims instead. The fact about our book: **we currently run no live Google lane on any account**, so everything here is an input to the next Google proposal and to how we read somebody else's Search account, not a change to a number we are managing this week.
