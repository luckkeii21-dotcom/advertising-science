# Lesson 041 · We Measured It and the Argument Did Not Move

🎬 **Watch first (3m 27s):** [[video/2026-09-30-lesson-041.mp4|Lesson 041 video]]

On 16 September we measured ad-set overlap on two of our accounts.

It was a good measurement. Two accounts, one identical eleven-day window, both exports reconciling to the account total exactly on impressions and on spend. The account frequency decomposed to Meta's own printed eight decimals on both. A chance baseline was computed so a low number could not be mistaken for a small account.

The argument it was built to settle is exactly as open today as it was on 15 September.

That is worth an hour of your time, because nothing was done wrong.

**If you only answer two, answer Q3 and Q4.** Q3 is a test you could start on ChiroWorks this week. Q4 is a correction you will have to make out loud to Kartik at some point.

## 1. The mechanism

Two people argue about whether running the dishwasher at night saves money. One says yes, the off-peak rate is lower. The other says the tariff is flat all day, so the hour makes no difference.

You settle it. You measure the dishwasher across ten cycles: 11 litres and 1.05 kilowatt-hours a cycle, accurate to two decimals, repeatable.

Neither position has moved an inch. They were arguing about the tariff and you measured the machine.

The measurement is correct. It is about a different quantity than the one in dispute, and no amount of precision converts one into the other.

Every contested claim has a disputed quantity buried in it, and it is almost always smaller than the argument sounds. Three things are separable:

1. **Whether the pattern exists.**
2. **Why it exists.**
3. **How you would test it.**

People in a loud disagreement usually agree about the first and the third, and fight only about the second. Both of those agreements are worth money. Agreement on the first means the pattern was observed twice by people who were not coordinating, which is real evidence. Agreement on the third hands you the instrument for free, and you no longer have to design one.

So the job before any test is to find the disputed quantity and check that the instrument you are about to pick up actually produces it.

## 2. The evidence

TikTok is today's rotation topic. We run zero TikTok accounts and the topic still carries the cleanest instance of this shape in the whole codex.

[[TikTok Delivery#TT-017|TT-017]] (T3, contested) holds two operators under the same agency banner reporting the identical split: a minority of accounts where TikTok lift proves out, a majority where a holdout shows nothing. They name different reasons.

**Decider A is category, gated on creative supply.** Nine tests across one book. Four of five fashion lift experiments came back strongly positive. Four of four inverse holdouts outside fashion showed nothing, and those four clients were pulled off the platform on the result. His real decider is whether the account has a native organic TikTok operation producing volume, because TikTok creative fatigues fast.

**Decider B is market size and revenue.** Across two full transcripts he never mentions organic TikTok operation once. His floor is a revenue floor: $5-6M and already profitable, revised down on camera from $10-30M ([[TikTok Delivery#TT-020|TT-020]]). He has run state-level cuts repeatedly on US accounts spending $20,000 to $40,000 a month and got a null every time ([[TikTok Delivery#TT-018|TT-018]]), including an 8-figure client who cut about $40,000 a month with no revenue drop. He also volunteers the one that went the other way: a third-party geo-lift on a very large US brand that came back clean with statistical relevancy ([[TikTok Delivery#TT-019|TT-019]]).

Neither side shows a lift percentage. Neither shows a dashboard. Both are asserted.

**The two deciders are confounded in both books.** A fashion brand at 8 figures in the US differs from a non-fashion brand at 7 figures in Australia on every dimension at once. Category and scale move together in every case on file, so reading more of either operator cannot pull them apart. More evidence of the same shape adds nothing.

**And they agree completely on the instrument.** A geo holdout or an inverse holdout. Both sides reach for it. That agreement survived a disagreement about everything else.

[[TikTok Delivery#TT-024|TT-024]] shows what you keep when a contest will not close at all. The same operator states opposite directions four months apart on whether TikTok tanks pop-up submission rate. A third voice declines to confirm either and names the missing control: his clients run TikTok and have always run it, so no holdout exists. Three positions on file from two people.

What survives is narrower and untouched by the contradiction: **a metric measured at the submission stops meaning anything once TikTok is on.** Every version of the story is consistent with that. A contest does not leave you with nothing. It leaves you the part all sides' data agrees on, and that part is usually safe to operate from.

## 3. Our accounts

Our own TT-017 has been running for a year.

[[Auction Mechanics & Bidding#AU-056|AU-056]] (T3, contested) is Ben Heath: self-overlap between your own ad sets costs nothing, because thousands of advertisers are already bidding for the same people and one more ad set rounds to zero.

[[Auction Mechanics & Bidding#AU-058|AU-058]] (T3, contested) is Solutions 8: your ad sets cannibalise each other in the auction, so keep age bands and interests mutually exclusive with expansion off.

Neither showed a CPM comparison. Both named the same test.

On 16 September the quantity finally got measured on our own book, at [[Auction Mechanics & Bidding#AU-090|AU-090]] (T2, ours), window 10 to 20 July 2026.

| | ChiroWorks | SJR Commercial |
|---|---|---|
| Ad sets | 7 | 4 |
| Spend | $553.26 | $1,024.02 |
| Impressions | 19,408 | 50,128 |
| Account reach | 6,920 | 29,944 |
| Sum of ad-set reach | 8,743 | 31,725 |
| Ad sets per reached person | 1.26343931 | 1.05947769 |
| Saw exactly one ad set | at least 73.7% | at least 94.1% |

It found something neither operator had said. **Ad-set count predicts nothing.** ChiroWorks runs 1.75 times the ad sets and carries 4.4 times the overlap. What predicts overlap is whether the split line is one of Meta's four hard boundaries. SJR splits on language, a real fence, and reads 1.0595. Six of ChiroWorks' seven are Advantage+ in one town separated only by the creative concept in the name, which is a suggestion, and the account reads 1.26344 against a chance baseline of 1.0047 to 1.0110. That is 24 to 56 times chance, so the convergence is real and account smallness does not explain it.

Genuinely good work. And AU-056 against AU-058 is still open, because **those two are arguing about price and the reach column reports exposure.** The fragmentation is now measured. Whether it costs a cent is exactly as unknown as it was before.

The test sits inside AU-056's own text and has never been run: same creative, same total budget, one consolidated ad set for a fortnight against the split ones for a fortnight, then compare delivered CPM and cost per opt-in.

Two more on the book have the same shape.

**The retargeting ad set.** `NJ/SI - 3s VV Retarget` is the only non-broad ad set we run anywhere. It took $142.05 for 30 opt-ins, $4.74 each against $8.19 in its lane, 42% under, and it reads as our cheapest row. On the two columns a non-broad ad set should actually be graded on, it is our most expensive: **$40.94 CPM against $13.38 and $16.55** for the broad ad sets, and **$52.89 per thousand people reached against $24.07 and $25.86**. Its pool is our own three-second video viewers, so every person in it was found and paid for by another ad set first. [[Attribution & Incrementality#AT-061|AT-061]] (T4) predicts that cheap row whether the ad set added thirty opt-ins or zero. The disputed quantity is total account opt-ins, and the ad-set column cannot produce it however many decimals you take it to. AT-061 names its own falsification: switch the ad set off for a fortnight with the campaign budget held fixed, and read the account.

**The optimisation event.** `Hal w hook` ran under two different events on SJR in one week. From the 9 to 16 June 2026 export: content-view optimisation took $275.32 for 669 content views and **2 opt-ins at $137.66 each**. Form optimisation took $69.94 for **20 opt-ins at $3.497 each**. A factor of 39.

The event moved, the destination moved, and the budget moved four-fold, all together. That is a natural pairing rather than an experiment, and the 39 belongs to the bundle. TT-017's confound, sitting in our own account, with a bigger number on it than anything either TikTok operator showed. The fix is already written down: hold the destination fixed on the website, vary only the event between content view and website lead, same creative, same total budget, same window, read on opt-ins.

## 4. The decision rule

**Before you run the test, write down the single number the argument is about, and check that the test you are about to run produces that number.**

Two riders, both load-bearing.

When a contest will not close, state the narrower claim every side's data agrees with, operate from that, and stop quoting either decider as settled. TT-024 is the model.

A measurement that misses the disputed quantity is still worth having. AU-090 killed the ad-set-count theory both operators had implicitly assumed, constrained AU-057 hard, and built a four-minute instrument that works on any account. It earned its keep. It did not earn the thing it was aimed at, and the honest version of the finding says so in its own text.
