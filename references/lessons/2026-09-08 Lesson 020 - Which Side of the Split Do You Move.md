# Lesson 020 · Which Side of the Split Do You Move

> Lesson 017 was about forcing a shared wallet to feed a starved container. This one is about the other option in that same meeting: breaking the container in two. The whole decision turns on a detail nobody argues about, which is which half you pick up and carry.

**If you only answer two questions, answer Q2 and Q4.** Q2 is the ChiroWorks call sitting unchecked on the hot sheet since 25 August. Q4 asks you to disagree with the lesson using a claim that is already in the codex.

## 1. The mechanism

A shared-budget campaign is one wallet and several containers. The wallet routes money toward whichever container already has conversion history, because history is what the system predicts from. Do that for a few weeks and one container is taking most of the money. Call it the winner.

Then comes the obvious move. This thing is good, so protect it. Give it its own campaign and its own wallet so nothing can starve it.

Here is what that move misses. Part of what made the winner look like a winner was the wallet it was sitting in. It was being fed. Lift it out and it arrives somewhere new with none of that. Meanwhile the container you left behind now has the whole wallet and still no history, because it never got enough delivery to build any.

Think about repotting a plant. One is thriving and one is barely alive. Repot the thriving one and you find out how much of it was the pot. Repot the dying one and you risk nothing, because it is already producing nothing.

So a split has a direction, and the direction is the decision. Move the side that has nothing to lose.

## 2. The evidence

**GP-032, T3.** Two operators arrive at this from opposite ends. Austin only ever extracts what is failing, because the winner is carrying campaign-level learning that a brand new campaign does not inherit. Nathan reports running the opposite play many times, pulling a high performer out into its own campaign at roughly $200 a day, and says he has never once seen it work. Repeated practitioner experience on both sides, no test, no numbers beyond that budget figure. T3 is the right ceiling for it.

**GP-023, T3.** The size gate that runs alongside the direction gate. Any segment you pull out has to hold enough conversion volume to survive on its own. There are four accepted reasons to split at all: brand versus non-brand, geography, different margin or efficiency goals, and genuinely distinct product categories. "It feels like best practice" is the named failure case.

**GP-022, T3, and GP-036, T3.** Campaigns learn in isolation, so campaign count follows spend. Below roughly $50 a day per container you are splitting a budget that cannot feed both halves.

**LS-025, T4, contested, and read the contest.** This is the claim the whole transfer to Meta rests on: learning accrues at the ad, ad set, campaign and account level at once, so every new campaign restarts all of them from zero. Two unrelated operators reached it independently, which is why it is in the codex at all. It also collides with Meta's own documentation. LS-001 and LS-004 put the learning phase at the ad set level and say an ad-set edit does not reset its siblings. Nobody has tested it. T4 means treat it as the reason the pattern might be true, never as proof that it is.

**LS-035, T3, contested.** Split into another container only when the budget can carry every resulting container out of learning. Theriot runs 150 to 180 ad sets in accounts that get better anyway, so this one has a live opponent with an account on screen.

**AT-109, T2, ours.** The only shown numbers in this whole chain are our own, and they are in section 3.

Three things in that table.

The funded ad set got steadily more expensive while it was being funded: $36.25, $40.07, $45.05. Its own cheapest recorded figure is $19.65, from AT-109, back in July.

Chiro Care did not get outbid. It got switched off. $67.29 to $15.96 to $4.25, ending the third week with no active ads at all. Its share of the wallet went 11.1% to 5.1%.

The chiropractic side recovered to 24.6% in the last week entirely through two brand new ad sets. One of them, Shoulder and arms, produced the cheapest opt-ins in the account that week at $25.56, on 10.7% of the budget, while the incumbent produced them at $45.05 on 65.7%. That is 1.76 times the cost going to the container holding two thirds of the money. **Two opt-ins is an anecdote, so read that as a reason to fund the ad set, never as a measured result.** The export carries no campaign column, so this is read at ad-set level inside a total that matches the recorded $66-a-day CBO.

**The open decision, and we have written it down both ways.** The 24 July client attribution audit put two fixes on the table: a minimum spend floor inside the CBO, or pulling chiropractic back into its own campaign with its own wallet. That is demoting the tail, and GP-032 agrees with it. The 25 August weekly then decided the reverse, that invisa-RED splits into its own campaign so budget divides between the two programs. Same problem, opposite direction, six weeks apart. The 25 August box is still unchecked, owner Lucky, fourteen days old.

**SJR Commercial got the direction right.** The dump CBO ran $957.81 over 19 July to 17 August at a $9.04 cost per opt-in, and Caesar took 63% of it at $9.18 while walking $3.20, $6.49, $8.70, $9.18. The same three new statics have been starved for about seven straight weeks: $0.58 combined in one window, $4.03 in another. The fix on file is to send new concepts to a separate ABO lane split by language and cap Caesar where it sits. That moves the tail and leaves the head alone. It is the right shape.

**Phoenix Truxx is the one time we tried the other direction, and the record is suggestive rather than decisive.** Video02 English was the account's second anchor, 4.77% CTR, $2.66 all-time when it broke out at $1.64 on $23 in Campaign 3. The following week it took $144 and returned $4.10 at 2.72% CTR. The 9 May report then gave it a dedicated ad set with 70% of the new Campaign 02 budget and max spend enforced. **The promotion was decided off the all-time number while the current number had already gone $1.64 to $4.10 and the CTR had halved.** It ran about four days and was retired 14 May at 401 all-time opt-ins and $2.83. Between the 398-at-$2.79 mark and the 401-at-$2.83 mark, three opt-ins cost somewhere between $6.81 and $9.47 each, which is a band because both all-time figures are rounded to the cent. Do not lean on that band. Targeting opened from two markets to nine states in the same days, and the 14 May retirement was a planned refresh with the ad still healthy. Two causes and a four-day window cannot settle anything.

## 4. The decision rule

**When you split a shared budget, move the side with nothing to lose. Demote the tail, never promote the head. If the budget cannot carry both halves out of learning, do not split at all, fix the routing inside the one container instead.**

Three checks before any split ships:

1. **Direction.** Which side physically moves into the new campaign? If the answer is the performer, stop.
2. **Size.** Can the half you are moving survive on its own budget? GP-023's gate, and on our accounts it usually cannot.
3. **Alternative.** A spend floor inside the existing campaign gets the starved side fed without anyone re-entering learning. Lesson 017 has the arithmetic for setting it.
