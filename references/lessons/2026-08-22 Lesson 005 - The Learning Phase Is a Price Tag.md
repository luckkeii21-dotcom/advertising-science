# Lesson 005: The Learning Phase Is a Price Tag

> Yesterday's research run put a number on something you have been living with for months without naming it. Every single account on our book sits in limited learning permanently, and that is not a fault any of us can fix.

## The mechanism

Meta's rule reads like a skill threshold. An ad set exits the learning phase once it can deliver stably, usually after about 50 optimization events in the week following its last significant edit. Until then the Delivery column says "Learning". If Meta thinks you will not get there, it says "Learning limited".

Everyone treats that 50 as a competence bar. It is a bill.

The threshold is stated in units and paid in money. Think about a supplier who offers free shipping over 50 units. Whether that offer is generous or absurd has nothing to do with the supplier and everything to do with what one unit costs you. At $2 a unit it is $100 and you take it. At $300 a unit it is $15,000 and the offer was never for you.

Meta's 50 works identically. Multiply 50 by your cost per optimization event. That is the weekly budget the label demands. Matt Shiver ran that arithmetic on his own account out loud: his cost per booked call is about $500, so 50 conversions in 7 days would be $25,000 a week. His conclusion was not that something is broken. It was "I am always in limited learning", and he stopped managing toward the label at all.

This matters because the label has been quietly steering decisions it has no right to steer. Consolidate the ad sets to clear learning. Raise the budget to clear learning. Drop to a shallower event to clear learning. Take the qualification questions off the form to clear learning. Every one of those is a real cost paid to reach a threshold that, on a high-ticket account, was never in reach at any budget the client was going to approve.

## The evidence

**LS-001 is T1 and it is not in dispute.** Meta's own help centre documents the roughly 50 results in the week after the last significant edit. Ads Manager even ships a "Last significant edit" column so you can count from it. The threshold is real and the number is Meta's.

**LS-060 is T3 and it is one operator's arithmetic on his own account.** It is banked as new yesterday. The reasoning is airtight because it is just multiplication, and the source figures are real costs from a real book. What it does not have is any performance data comparing the two states, so it tells you the floor is unreachable and tells you nothing about what that costs you.

**Watch the weak half of LS-060, because this is the part you will be tempted to quote.** In place of Meta's 50, Shiver offers "if you're getting at least two conversions per day, you're going to be good." That is 14 a week. Nothing sits behind it. No test, no comparison, no definition of "good". It also lands near two other unsourced floors already on file, his own five-a-day structure gate at LS-035 and Ben Heath's 20 to 25 a week event-selection floor at LS-022. Three operators, three numbers, none measured. Do not present any of them as a threshold.

**LS-005 is the converging voice, and it comes from the opposite vertical.** Nick Theriot, in ecommerce, refuses to act on the status at all: getting out of learning limited "doesn't magically change performance". Two operators, ecommerce and high-ticket lead gen, independently ignoring the same label is the strongest support this position has. Neither ran a test. Say that out loud when you use it.

**LS-060 also lands on law 6, and this is the part worth thinking hardest about.** The volume-first side of the qualification argument (LS-009, contested) had one genuinely strong mechanism: every qualification gate cuts event volume toward the learning floor, and that floor is T1 documentation. If the floor was never reachable, the gate is not costing you something you had.

**Do not swing to the other doctrine on that alone.** LS-062 was banked the same day and it is the first reported FAILURE of the quality route anywhere in our codex. An operator rewrote his ads and VSL to call out high earners and restricted what went back to the pixel. His words: "We do all of the best practices, right? But that didn't work." Nothing is shown, so it refutes nothing. It fixes a selection bias, because until yesterday the codex held only wins on that side.

**The second half of LS-062 is the more useful finding.** He checked income against actual buyers. It did not predict purchase. People earning $30,000 to $50,000 bought a $3,000-plus offer, and higher earners were sometimes less likely to buy. An income question on that form would have excluded the customers. A gate is only as good as the variable it gates on, and nobody checks the variable.

## Our accounts

**MetaTechAI already wrote this rule down before the codex had it.** `_HOT` line: allowable cost per qualified booked call **$308**, budget **$200/day**. Run the arithmetic. 50 x $308 = **$15,400 a week**. The account spends $1,400 a week. It is 11x short, and no creative or structure decision closes an 11x gap. The hot sheet's own conclusion is already correct: "At $200/day no honest downstream event clears the learning phase."

**ChiroWorks is the clearest case because the budget is a hard cap.** Aug 8-16, 9 days: **$628.89 spend, 15 leads, $38.32 consolidated CPL**. 50 x $38.32 = **$1,916 a week**, about **$8,300 a month** against a **$2,000/month** cap the client set. Roughly 4x the entire budget, on the shallowest event in the account, a form fill. Actual rate is about **12 leads a week against 50**. The two booked appointments in that window mean a booking-optimized ad set would need 50 a week when the account produces under 2.

**SJR Commercial is the interesting one, because one campaign nearly cleared it and we turned it off.** Last 30 days read (19 Jul to 17 Aug): dump CBO **106 leads at $9.04 CPL on $40/day**, which is about **25 leads a week**, half the floor, and clearing it would need about **$65/day**. Box & Wreckers ran **197 leads at $4.23 CPL**, about **46 a week**, effectively at the threshold. That is the only engine in our entire book that gets near 50, and it is **OFF**. The Aug 14-20 report already flags turning it back on for volume reasons. Add this one: it is also the only signal-rich ad set we have.

**Phoenix Truxx is the trap.** 96 unique leads in the Jul 31 to Aug 6 window looks like it clears 50 comfortably. It does not. Learning is per ad set (LS-001), and that 96 is a CRM account total spread across campaigns and ad sets. An account-level count never answers an ad-set-level question. Check where the number is measured before you celebrate it.

## The decision rule

**Multiply 50 by your cost per optimization event before you argue about the learning phase. If the answer exceeds the client's weekly budget, the label is permanent, so stop proposing consolidations and budget raises whose stated purpose is clearing it, and never write "Learning limited" into a client report as a problem.**
