# Lesson 006 · A Step Size Is a Ceiling, Not a Schedule

No video today. Sunday. Video days are Monday, Wednesday and Friday.

**If you only answer two questions, answer Q1 and Q4.** Five lessons have gone out and the inbox has come back empty five times. Two answers is a real submission.

## 1. The mechanism

Every scaling video you have ever watched gives you a percentage. Raise the budget 20% a day. Or 30% every three days. Or 5% three times a week. You have probably read one of those as an instruction and mentally drawn the curve, because that is what a percentage looks like.

The percentage is not the curve. It is a speed limit sign.

A 60 mph limit does not mean you cover 60 miles in the next hour. You cover 60 miles only if every light is green, no junction slows you and nothing merges in front of you. The sign tells you the fastest you are allowed to go on a stretch of road where you are already permitted to move. It says nothing about how many stretches of road you get.

A budget step size works exactly the same way. "20% a day" means: on a day where yesterday's cost per result cleared the target, you may move the budget up to 20%. On a day it did not clear, the step is zero. The number is a permission ceiling attached to a gate. Almost everyone repeats the ceiling and drops the gate, and then the ceiling gets read as a growth plan.

Here is what the difference costs. An operator screen-shares an account and says he keeps bumping the budget 20% a day. In the same breath he dates the account's history: $100/day up to $5,000/day, over almost a year. That is 50x.

Compounding 20% a day reaches 50x in **21.5 days**. It took roughly **365**.

So the account had about **22 qualifying days in a year**. Roughly one green light every seventeen days. Every other day the gate was shut and the step was zero. The ceiling was 20%. The realised average was **1.08% a day**, which is about **eighteen times smaller** than the number he said out loud, and neither of them is wrong. They are answering different questions.

That is the entire lesson. A ceiling and a realised rate are different quantities, and the whole 3%-to-100% spread in our codex may be nothing more than operators quoting one while their audience hears the other.

## 2. The evidence

- **SC-134, T3, banked today.** The $100-to-$5,000-over-a-year account. Source is Nick Theriot, 2026-08-21, screen-shared. His CPA gate is explicit and account-specific. The arithmetic above is ours, not his, so treat it as a reading correction and not as a new measurement.
- **SC-001, T3, contested.** The step-size range itself: 3% to 100% per day depending on who is talking. Now readable as a cadence artefact rather than a real disagreement about the world.
- **SC-100, T3.** Ben Heath makes step size a decreasing function of budget: 100% at $50/day, 50% at $100/day, 20% at $800/day, each held 5 to 10 days. Theriot holds 20% flat all the way to $5,000/day. Two operators, directly opposed on whether the step shrinks as budget grows, neither with a test.
- **SC-132, T3, banked today.** The brake: "Turning off ads is the same thing as changing the budget. So, never turn off more than 20% of the spend on any day." Same class of intervention, same ceiling. Watch the denominator, because the same operator elsewhere prescribes pruning 10% of ad COUNT, and count and spend are not the same thing.
- **Law 7 in the skill file.** Read it before quoting any number near it. Sam Piliero now has three incompatible figures on the record, 20-30%, 10-30%, and "doubling spend is common practice", so his step-size testimony is no longer usable as a point estimate.

Tier check, and say it out loud when you use this: **every claim above is T3.** Practitioner testimony from real spend, no controlled test anywhere. Nobody has ever run a budget-step-size experiment and shown the data. Google announced a multi-campaign budget A/B for September 2026 that could settle it on the Search side. Until something like that runs, step size is folklore with good sources.

**Correction, and it is against us.** The codex and the skill file both say the realised step is "a small fraction of one percent per day". Recomputed from the raw numbers, it is 1.08% a day. That is a fraction of the 20% ceiling, not a fraction of one percent. Small error, real error, and it was in a law. Check our arithmetic the same way you check theirs.

## 3. Our accounts

The reason this matters here is that **not one of our five accounts is gated by a percentage.** They are gated by things the percentage cannot see.

**ChiroWorks.** The client cap is $2,000/month, which is $65.75/day. We consolidated to a single CBO at $66/day on 21 July, so we are already at the cap. From the original $50/day, a 20%-per-day ceiling would have covered that distance in **1.5 days**. The step size was never the binding constraint. The contract was. Aug 1-16 paced $69.88/day, about $2,096/month, and that overrun is FTN spend, not a scaling decision.

**StayWell / Chiropraise.** Running about $50/day, up from $35/day in late June. The Aug 20 report says it plainly: a budget step-up is justified once shows convert, not before. Show rate that week was 0%. In step-size language, **the account has had zero qualifying days.** A percentage would have told us to raise. The gate says do not.

**SJR Commercial.** $60/day box trucks and $40/day dump as of 5 August. If you read 20% a day as a schedule, the dump lane reaches $5,000/day in **27 days**. It obviously will not, and the reasons are all outside the formula: client approval, one-price confirmation, inventory on the lot, and a CBO that starves new creative before it can produce a qualifying day.

**Mattia.** Two CBOs at $33/day each. At that level the step is $6.60. The move is smaller than a single lead's cost, which means daily stepping is noise and the honest cadence is weekly or slower.

## 4. The decision rule

**Never state a step size without the gate and the hold: what percentage, at what budget, on what condition, held for how long. If you cannot name the condition that makes a day qualify, you do not have a scaling plan, you have a number.**

1. In one sentence, what is the difference between a step-size ceiling and a realised growth rate?
2. An operator says "20% a day" and his account went 50x in a year. About how many qualifying days did that account have, and roughly how often is that?
3. What tier is every step-size claim in the codex, and what is the one sentence you must attach when you use one in a client decision?
4. **Applied.** ChiroWorks is capped at $2,000/month and already running $66/day. A video tells you to scale winners 20% a day. What do you actually do, and what is the first question you ask the client?
5. **Applied.** SJR's dump lane is at $40/day and last week's cost per lead beat target on two days out of seven. Using SC-132 and SC-134 together, what is the largest budget move you would make this week, and what is the largest cull?

> [!note]- Answer key
> **1.** A ceiling is the maximum move permitted on a day that clears the gate. A realised rate is the average actual growth across all days, including the ones where the step was zero. Full marks for naming the gate.
> **2.** About 22 qualifying days, roughly one every 17 days. Accept anything from 20 to 25 days. The arithmetic: 50x at 20% per step needs 21.5 steps.
> **3.** T3, practitioner testimony from real spend with no controlled test. The sentence: this is a bet, not a law, and no one has ever shown the data on step size.
> **4.** Nothing on Meta. The account is at the cap, so the percentage cannot execute. The first question to the client is whether the $2,000/month cap moves, because the constraint is the contract and not the algorithm. Second-best answer: reallocate inside the existing $66/day. Also credit anyone who checks the FTN overrun first, since we are already pacing $69.88/day.
> **5.** Two qualifying days at a 20% ceiling on $40/day is at most $40 x 1.2 x 1.2, so about $57.60/day, and only if both moves were made on the days the gate cleared. A safer read is one move to $48/day held for the week, because at $40/day a 20% step is $8 and lead cost swamps it. The cull cap under SC-132 is 20% of daily spend, which on the $100/day live total is $20/day of ads. Full marks for noticing that the CBO starving new creative means most days never get the chance to qualify.
