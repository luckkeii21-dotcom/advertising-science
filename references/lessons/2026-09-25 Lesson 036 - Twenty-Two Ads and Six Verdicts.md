# Lesson 036 · Twenty-Two Ads and Six Verdicts

🎬 **Lesson video (3m 20s, silent, watch anywhere):** [[video/2026-09-25-lesson-036.mp4]]

Yesterday's harvest banked a receipt nobody on this roster has produced before. An operator ran the structure he publicly argues against, on purpose, for seven days, and published what it cost. Then he published what he stopped doing because of it.

Both halves are about the same thing, and it is not performance. It is how many decisions a budget is allowed to produce.

## 1. The mechanism

You have forty hours to hire one person.

Interview four candidates for ten hours each and you will know something real about four people. Interview forty for one hour each and you will know nothing about any of them, and you will still have spent the forty hours. The hours were fixed. The number of candidates was your choice. Choose forty and you have not run a wider search. You have converted a hiring decision into a coin flip and paid full price for it.

Ad testing works the same way, and the exchange rate is published. [[Scaling Models#SC-044|SC-044]] puts the floor at **three times the target cost per result** before any unit of a test is allowed a verdict. Below that, the row is not a weak result. It is not a result.

So the budget for a window sets a hard count: window spend divided by three times target cost per result. That is how many verdicts the window can buy. Launch more units than that and the extra ones do not fail. They come back blank, and the money that went into them bought nothing.

The second half follows. If a unit cannot clear the gate, stop making decisions inside it. Move the decision up a level, to the container that can.

## 2. The evidence

**[[Scaling Models#SC-044|SC-044]], tier 3.** Three independent operators, two verticals, one number: spend 3x the target cost per result before changing an ad set. Nick Theriot adds the part the other two never state, and it is a delivery floor rather than a statistical one: the 3x has to accumulate over **at least 3 days**, because "it takes about 72 hours for Facebook to fully optimize". An ad set that burns the gate between midnight and 2am has cleared the number and told you nothing. Nobody has shown why 3x rather than 2x or 5x, which is why this sits at T3 after a year.

**[[Scaling Models#SC-171|SC-171]], tier 2. The price, from the account.** Theriot ran a dedicated ABO testing campaign for 7 days: about **51 tests**, each ad set launched at **$100 a day** and cut at 2 to 3 days, three creatives per ad set. Total **about $10,000**, at a **$106 new-customer cost per acquisition against a $65 target**. He estimates a CBO would have learned the same thing for about **$2,000**, so roughly **$8,000 of the $10,000 bought certainty rather than results**. That $2,000 is his estimate and no control ran, so treat the delta as unverified and the $10,000 as measured.

Worth noticing what falls out of his own numbers. $10,000 across 51 ad sets is **$196.08 per test**, and three times his $65 target is **$195**. He never names SC-044 and he landed on it to the dollar. Read that as consistent with the gate, not as proof of it.

**[[Scaling Models#SC-172|SC-172]], tier 2. What he stopped doing.** Inside those 51 tests the same shape kept appearing: one creative taking nearly everything, a sibling converting better on a fraction of the spend. His worked example is **$3,600 on one ad, 33 cents on another, zero on the third**. The reflex he names out loud is "no brainer, turn off the top one." Verbatim result: **"99% of the time it did not hold performance at all."**

So he moved the decision unit: "I'm not looking at ads for one campaign. I'm actually breaking down each individual ad set. And I'm turning just the whole ad set off." The ad level stopped being a place where decisions happen.

This is the largest stated count anyone has put behind the kill-the-hog question, and it lands on the leave-it-on side. It does **not** settle [[Scaling Models#SC-085|SC-085]] or un-contest [[Scaling Models#SC-008|SC-008]]. Those are CBO claims about the top two ads in a campaign, this is an ABO claim about the top ad inside an ad set, and "99% of 51" was recalled on camera with no log shown and no definition of "did not hold performance". Strongest direction on file. Round number a person said out loud.

**[[Meta Delivery & Andromeda#MD-163|MD-163]], tier 3**, supplies the mechanism for why blank units are not harmless: "if half the ads it was learning from yesterday are gone today, it has no confidence on what to do tomorrow."

## 3. Our accounts

The gate needs a target cost per opt-in. **No account we run has one written down.** So below I use each account's own achieved figure as the stand-in and name it as a substitution, which makes these gates descriptive rather than agreed.

**ChiroWorks.** 6 July to 22 September: **$4,794.02, 143 opt-ins, $33.52 each**, reconciled against Ads Manager on three hand-checked windows. Gate: **$100.57 per unit.**

Now the window in [[Scaling Models#SC-168|SC-168]], 11 to 20 September: **$624.10 across 22 ads that spent.**

$624.10 divided by $100.57 is **6.2**. The window could buy six verdicts. It was spread across twenty-two ads.

One ad cleared it. The window splits into three bands.

| Band | Ads | Spend | Each | Verdict bought |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Top ad | 1 | $247.60 | $247.60 | **2.46** |
| Spent real money, no verdict | 5 | $340.95 | $68.19 | 0.68 each |
| Spent and returned zero opt-ins | 16 | $35.55 | $2.22 | 0.02 each |

The sixteen come straight out of SC-168: **16 of the 22 ads spent and produced zero opt-ins, on $35.55 between them, 5.7% of the window.** That is **$2.22 an ad**. Nobody can read anything off $2.22, in either direction. And the five that took real money still landed at $68.19 each, two thirds of the way to a verdict and therefore short of one.

So twenty-one of twenty-two ads returned nothing readable. Not a weak result twenty-one times. No result twenty-one times.

Ad-set level does not rescue it either. When the structure was last counted, on the 10 to 20 July window, ChiroWorks ran **seven ad sets, six of them Advantage+ in one town split only by creative concept**. If that count still holds, $624.10 across seven is **$89.16 per ad set**, still under the $100.57 gate. The account has no unit at any level that can produce a verdict in ten days, except the top ad.

**StayWell.** 15 June to 23 September: **$3,704.08, 144 opt-ins, $25.72 each.** Gate: **$77.17.** Window 11 to 20 September: **$337.49 across 12 ads**, which buys **4.4 verdicts**. The top ad took $21.48 a day, **$214.80**, clearing it. **Ten of the twelve ads produced zero opt-ins on $98.05 between them**, which is **$9.80 each, 0.13 of a verdict each.** Those ten ads hold 1.27 verdicts' worth of money and return nothing readable, because it was cut ten ways.

**SJR Commercial, the same rule, the opposite answer.** 21 to 27 August: **$866.14, 312 opt-ins, $2.78 blended.** Gate: **$8.33.** That one week buys **104 verdicts.** Phoenix Truxx at its all-time $4.00 per opt-in gates at **$12.00.**

Same agency, same week, same arithmetic. SJR can decide at the ad level a hundred times over. ChiroWorks gets six decisions in ten days and StayWell gets four. **A cheap opt-in does not only buy volume. It buys the right to read a smaller unit.**

And this is where [[Scaling Models#SC-167|SC-167]] gets its boundary. Theriot recovered about $6,000 a month by switching off 52 ad sets that spent and never converted, because each of them had cleared his gate and returned nothing. Our silent ads are a different animal: StayWell's ten are worth **$9.81 a day**, the worst share on our whole book, and ChiroWorks' sixteen are worth **$3.56 a day**. They have not failed the gate. They never reached it.

So the two silent tails need two different words. **A tail that cleared the gate and returned nothing is leakage, and switching it off is money.** A tail that never reached the gate is untested, and switching it off is tidiness. Only one of them belongs in a client conversation about recovered spend.

## 4. The decision rule

**Before you launch, divide the window's budget by three times the account's cost per opt-in. That is how many verdicts the window can buy. Launch that many test units and put the decision at whatever level makes each unit big enough to clear the gate.**

Corollary, straight from SC-172: when a unit cannot clear the gate, do not decide inside it. Turn the whole container off or leave it alone.

> [!note]- Answer key
>
> **1.** **Spend three times the target cost per result on a unit before changing it**, and the 3x must accumulate **over at least 3 days**. The failure it prevents is a unit clearing the money gate on the clock rather than on delivery: an ad set that burns $150 between midnight and 2am has hit 3x a $50 target and has told you nothing, because Theriot puts Meta's optimisation period at about 72 hours. Full marks for noting the gate is T3 after a year, three operators asserting one number, nobody showing why 3x.
>
> **2.** Ten days at $62.41 is **$624.10**. Divided by the $100.57 gate that is **6.2 verdicts**. Eight new concepts on top of whatever already runs means every unit lands near or under $70 and none of them clears. **Launch two, at most three**, and let the existing top ad keep its funding, because at $24.76 a day it is the only asset on the account that produces a readable result. If the real requirement is eight concepts, the honest answer is that it needs a bigger budget or a longer window, and you say which: eight units at $100.57 is $804.56, which is thirteen days of current spend with nothing left for the incumbent. Bonus for adding that ChiroWorks also has no written target cost per opt-in, so the $100.57 gate is a stand-in and worth agreeing with the client before it governs a launch plan.
>
> **3.** **SJR gets it, ChiroWorks does not.** The deciding number is cost per opt-in: **$2.78 against $33.52**, which sets the per-unit gate at **$8.33 against $100.57**. SJR's $866.14 week buys 104 verdicts, ChiroWorks' $624.10 buys 6.2. It has nothing to do with creative because the gate is arithmetic on price and budget only. The same creative in both accounts would be readable in one and silent in the other.
>
> **4.** **Leave the $180 ad on.** The claim is [[Scaling Models#SC-172|SC-172]], T2: across 51 ad-set tests that exact reallocation failed to hold performance 99% of the time, and the operator's response was to stop making ad-level decisions inside a test campaign. Two things to say out loud. First, the sibling has not earned a verdict: $22 against a $77.17 gate is 0.29 of one, and 2 opt-ins at $11.00 is a small-base reading rather than a cheaper ad. Second, the honest limit on SC-172: it is one operator recalling 99% of 51 on camera with no log shown, it is an ABO claim while [[Scaling Models#SC-008|SC-008]] and [[Scaling Models#SC-085|SC-085]] remain **contested** for CBOs, so leaving it on is the better-evidenced bet and not settled law. If the client insists, the reversible version in SC-008 is to switch it off for 2 to 3 days and read **account-level** cost per opt-in, never the sibling's own row.
>
> **5.** An account running **above** target has an achieved cost per opt-in **higher** than its target, so 3x the achieved figure sets the gate **too high**, and the window appears to buy **fewer** verdicts than it really does. The bias is conservative, which is the safe direction to be wrong in, and it is still wrong. It also means the gate silently loosens as an account improves. Best answer names the fix: write a target cost per opt-in per account and gate on that, so the instrument stops moving with the result it is judging.
>
> **Carry forward.** Two items out of this lesson. **Write a target cost per opt-in for all five accounts**, because every gate above is currently anchored to the number it is supposed to judge. And **count the live ad sets on ChiroWorks and StayWell in the current window**, because the seven-ad-set figure is from 10 to 20 July and the whole ad-set-level conclusion in section 3 is conditional on it.
